PTI Long March Enters Karak, CM Afridi Declares Resolve

By Umer Javed | 5 October 2026 | 6 min read
Photo: Syed Qaarif Andrabi / Pexels

Chief Minister Afridi has launched the Pakistan Tehreek-e-Insaf long march towards Islamabad, with the convoy now having entered Karak district. The march signifies a strong declaration of intent from the party leadership.

Chief Minister Afridi has launched the Pakistan Tehreek-e-Insaf (PTI) long march towards Islamabad, with the convoy now having entered Karak district, marking a significant development in the nation's political. This mobilization, initiated by the Chief Minister, is a clear demonstration of the party's resolve to press its demands through a large-scale public demonstration.

The journey began with CM Afridi's firm declaration, stating, "We are not yes-men," a statement that reverberates with the party's stance against perceived external pressures and its commitment to an independent political trajectory. This sentiment underpins the entire endeavor, providing a rallying cry for the participants and setting the tone for the march as it traverses through various regions.

Karak district, a region known for its arid and a population largely engaged in agriculture and oil and gas sector employment, now finds itself at the heart of this national political procession. The entry of the convoy into this area transforms the routine life of its residents, bringing with it a temporary but noticeable shift in the local environment. For the people of Karak, the sight of a large political motorcade is not an everyday occurrence, prompting both observation and discussion among the local populace.

The long march, by its very nature, is a test of endurance and commitment for those participating. Thousands of party workers and supporters have joined the convoy, leaving the comfort of their homes to embark on a journey that demands physical resilience and unwavering dedication. Their collective motivation stems from a shared belief in the party's agenda and leadership, hoping to exert pressure on the federal government through this massive show of public strength.

The Journey Through Karak

As the convoy made its way through Karak, the district's characteristic dusty roads and sparse settlements offered a unique backdrop to the procession. Residents along the route gathered, some to cheer, others simply to witness the spectacle unfold. The presence of such a large gathering inevitably brings logistical challenges, including managing traffic flow and ensuring security for the participants and the general public. Local authorities, according to reports, were engaged in maintaining order and facilitating the movement of the marchers.

The marchers themselves experience the journey intimately. Days spent on the road, often under the open sky, involve communal meals, shared conversations, and the constant rhythm of slogans and political songs. This collective experience fosters a strong sense of camaraderie among participants, transforming disparate individuals into a unified force. For many, it is not just a political act but a personal pilgrimage, a sacrifice made for a cause they deeply believe in.

The impact on local businesses in Karak, particularly those situated along the main thoroughfares, can be varied. While some might experience a temporary surge in sales of refreshments and basic necessities, others could face disruptions due to road closures and altered traffic patterns. The temporary influx of a large number of people also places demands on local infrastructure, however brief.

The atmosphere surrounding a long march is typically charged with anticipation and emotion. Flags wave vigorously, banners displaying party symbols and messages are prominently displayed, and the air resonates with political slogans. This vibrant display of political activism serves as a powerful reminder of the democratic right to protest and express dissent, a right deeply ingrained in Pakistan's political history.

Voices from the Road

Chief Minister Afridi's statement, "We are not yes-men," encapsulates a broader sentiment within the party, reflecting a desire for greater autonomy and a rejection of what they perceive as subservience. This message resonates with many who feel that their political voice needs to be heard more forcefully in the national dialogue. The long march is designed to amplify this voice, carrying it from the provincial heartlands directly to the federal capital.

The journey through districts like Karak allows for direct engagement, however fleeting, between the political leadership and the people. While the convoy moves swiftly, the sheer presence of senior leaders and thousands of supporters creates a direct connection with the communities it passes through. This interaction, even if brief, serves to invigorate the party's base and communicate its message beyond traditional media channels.

For the younger participants, many of whom may be experiencing their first long march, the event is an education in political mobilization. They learn about the organizational demands, the power of collective action, and the endurance required to sustain a movement over several days and hundreds of kilometers. It is an experience that can shape their understanding of civic participation and political advocacy.

The long march is not merely a physical journey; it is a symbolic one. It represents a journey of political assertion, a movement from the periphery to the centre of power, aiming to force a reconsideration of policies and governance. The path through Karak is just one segment of this longer, more complex narrative that seeks to redefine the political discourse in the country.

Beyond the Immediate Impact

While the immediate focus remains on the movement of the convoy and its entry into various districts, the long-term implications of such a large-scale protest are often profound. These marches can shape public opinion, influence political negotiations, and even alter the course of national events. The commitment shown by participants, the logistical challenges overcome, and the messages conveyed all contribute to a narrative that extends beyond the physical presence of the marchers.

The decision to undertake a long march is never taken lightly, given the significant resources, coordination, and public engagement it requires. It signals a deep-seated grievance and a determination to pursue political objectives through direct action. As the convoy continues its journey from Karak towards Islamabad, every kilometer covered adds to the weight of its message, aiming to resonate with decision-makers in the capital.

The event highlights the dynamic nature of Pakistani politics, where public demonstrations often play a significant role in expressing dissent and advocating for change. The entry into Karak is a step on this arduous path, symbolizing the unwavering spirit of those who believe in the power of collective action to bring about desired political outcomes. The eyes of the nation now watch as this procession continues its determined course towards Islamabad, carrying with it the aspirations and demands of its participants.
