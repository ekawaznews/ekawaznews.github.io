Pakistan Heads Secretariat of Makkah Joint Defence Pact

By Umer Javed | 25 September 2026 | 3 min read
Photo: Tuğba / Pexels

Deputy Prime Minister Ishaq Dar met Saudi and Turkish counterparts to review progress on the Makkah Joint Defence Agreement. The tripartite pact empowers member states to treat an attack on one as an attack on all.

Deputy Prime Minister and Foreign Minister Ishaq Dar met his Saudi Arabian and Turkish counterparts on Friday to review progress on institutionalising the tripartite Makkah Joint Defence Agreement.

Background of the Pact

The Makkah Joint Defence Agreement, signed on August 7, 2024, establishes a mutual defence clause that obliges the three signatories—Pakistan, Saudi Arabia and Turkey—to consider any attack on one of them as an attack on all. This framework is designed to enhance collective security in the region. It also outlines the mechanisms for rapid mobilisation of forces in response to a threat.

Inaugural Meeting

The alliance held its inaugural meeting on August 31, 2024, in Istanbul, where officials from the three countries convened to formalise the institutional structure. The meeting focused on setting up command and coordination mechanisms, as well as defining the operational parameters of the pact. It also reviewed the legal framework governing the alliance.

Secretariat Appointment

Pakistan was selected to lead the secretariat of the pact, which will be based in Riyadh. The decision reflects Pakistan’s experience in managing defence cooperation and its strategic position between the Middle East and South Asia. It also signals Pakistan’s willingness to take a leadership role in regional security initiatives.

Riyadh Review Meeting

During the Riyadh meeting, Ishaq Dar met with Saudi Arabia’s Foreign Minister and Turkey’s counterpart to assess how the institutional framework is progressing. The discussion covered the status of secretariat appointments, the establishment of liaison offices, and the initiation of joint training programmes. It also addressed the timeline for implementing the agreed actions.

Progress and Future Plans

According to reports, the officials agreed that the secretariat has begun drafting a comprehensive operational manual. They also reviewed the progress of initial joint exercises scheduled for the coming months and discussed the allocation of resources required for these activities. The draft manual is expected to be finalized by the end of the year.

Regional Security Implications

The tripartite pact is expected to strengthen regional stability by creating a deterrent against external threats. By aligning the defence postures of Pakistan, Saudi Arabia and Turkey, the alliance aims to deter aggression and provide a coordinated response to any security challenges. The pact also facilitates intelligence sharing across the three nations.

Challenges Ahead

Despite the progress, the alliance faces challenges related to coordination across time zones, differences in military doctrines and the need for sustained funding. The parties acknowledged that overcoming these obstacles will be essential to realise the full potential of the pact. They agreed to set up a joint committee to monitor progress.

Conclusion

Ultimately, the meeting reaffirmed the commitment of Pakistan, Saudi Arabia and Turkey to institutionalise the Makkah Joint Defence Agreement. The parties agreed to continue their collaborative efforts to enhance collective security in a rapidly changing geopolitical. The pact's institutionalisation marks a significant step in Pakistan’s foreign policy, showcasing its role as a bridge between the Middle East and South Asia.
