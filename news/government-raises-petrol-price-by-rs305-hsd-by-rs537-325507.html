Government Raises Petrol Price by Rs3.05, HSD by Rs5.37

By Umer Javed | 11 September 2026 | 5 min read
Photo: Fahad Puthawala / Pexels

The government on Thursday increased the price of petrol by Rs3.05 per litre and high-speed diesel (HSD) by Rs5.37 per litre. This latest adjustment in fuel prices is expected to have a ripple effect across various sectors of the economy.

The government on Thursday increased the price of petrol by Rs3.05 per litre and that of high-speed diesel (HSD) by Rs5.37 per litre. This latest adjustment in petroleum product prices will come into effect immediately, impacting consumers and businesses across the country.

The decision to raise fuel prices is a recurring feature of Pakistan's economic, often attributed to a combination of international crude oil prices, the exchange rate of the Pakistani Rupee against the US Dollar, and various government levies. These factors collectively determine the final price at which petroleum products are sold to the public.

Understanding Fuel Price Determination

Fuel prices in Pakistan are typically reviewed fortnightly by the Oil and Gas Regulatory Authority (OGRA), which then forwards its recommendations to the Finance Division. The government, after considering these recommendations and other economic factors, makes the final decision on price adjustments. The pricing mechanism involves several components, each contributing to the final retail cost.

A significant component is the international price of crude oil, which serves as the primary benchmark. Pakistan imports a substantial portion of its petroleum requirements, making it susceptible to volatility in global oil markets. Any upward trend in international prices directly translates into higher import costs for the country.

The exchange rate also plays a substantial role. A depreciation of the Pakistani Rupee against the US Dollar means that importing the same quantity of crude oil or refined petroleum products becomes more expensive in local currency terms. This cost is later passed on to the consumers.

Beyond the cost of imports, the government imposes various taxes and levies, which constitute a significant portion of the retail price. These include the Petroleum Levy (PL), General Sales Tax (GST), customs duties, and import parity price. The Petroleum Levy, in particular, is a key revenue-generating tool for the government, and its rate can be adjusted to meet fiscal targets or to manage price fluctuations to some extent.

Impact on Inflation and Daily Life

The increase in petrol and HSD prices is widely expected to exert additional inflationary pressure on an economy already grappling with rising living costs. Fuel is a fundamental input for nearly every sector, from transportation to agriculture and manufacturing. A hike in its price inevitably leads to an increase in the cost of producing and transporting goods.

For the common citizen, the immediate impact will be felt in transportation costs. Commuters relying on public transport, as well as those using private vehicles, will face higher expenses. The cost of daily necessities, including food items, vegetables, and other essential commodities, is also likely to rise as transporters pass on their increased fuel expenses to consumers.

Farmers, who use diesel for agricultural machinery such as tractors and tube-wells, will experience higher operational costs. This can potentially affect agricultural output prices, further contributing to food inflation. Similarly, industries that rely on diesel generators for backup power or use petroleum products in their manufacturing processes will see their production costs escalate.

Government's Fiscal Challenges and Revenue Needs

The government often faces a delicate balancing act when it comes to fuel prices. While higher prices burden the public, they are also a significant source of revenue for the national exchequer. The Petroleum Levy, in particular, is a non-tax revenue item that is often targeted to meet fiscal deficit reduction goals, especially those agreed upon with international financial institutions.

Maintaining stable fuel prices through subsidies can place immense pressure on the government's budget. In situations where global oil prices are high or the local currency depreciates sharply, providing subsidies requires substantial funds that could otherwise be allocated to development projects or social welfare programs. The decision to increase prices, therefore, often reflects a need to manage the fiscal deficit and ensure financial stability.

Economic observers often point out that the government's ability to absorb international price shocks is limited, particularly when the country is under various loan programs that necessitate strict fiscal discipline. These programs frequently encourage the government to pass on the full impact of international price variations to consumers to avoid accumulating further circular debt in the energy sector.

Broader Economic Implications

The cumulative effect of fuel price adjustments extends beyond immediate inflationary pressures. Consistent increases can dampen economic activity by reducing consumer purchasing power and increasing operational costs for businesses. Small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs), which are often more vulnerable to rising input costs, may find it harder to sustain operations.

The energy sector itself experiences challenges. The pricing structure and timely adjustments are important for the viability of oil marketing companies and refineries. Delays in price adjustments or inadequate cost recovery can lead to liquidity issues and hinder investment in the sector.

The consistent need for price hikes also highlights the structural challenges within Pakistan's economy, including its reliance on imported energy and the continuous pressure on the exchange rate. Addressing these fundamental issues through diversification of energy sources and strengthening the local currency remains a long-term goal for policymakers.

Public Sentiment and Future Outlook

Public reaction to fuel price increases is consistently one of concern and frustration, given the direct impact on household budgets. Citizens frequently express apprehension about the rising cost of living and the erosion of their purchasing power.

Looking ahead, the trajectory of international crude oil prices and the stability of the Pakistani Rupee will remain critical determinants of future fuel price adjustments. The government's continued efforts to manage the economy, control inflation, and generate revenue will invariably involve difficult decisions regarding such essential commodities.

The latest increase in petrol and HSD prices highlights the ongoing economic challenges faced by Pakistan and the intricate balance required to maintain fiscal health while addressing the needs of its citizens.
