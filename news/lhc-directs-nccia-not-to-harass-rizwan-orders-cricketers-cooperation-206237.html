LHC Directs NCCIA Not to Harass Rizwan, Orders Cricketer's Cooperation

By Umer Javed | 22 September 2026 | 6 min read
Photo: Pragyan Bezbaruah / Pexels

Lahore High Court Chief Justice Aalia Neelum has restrained the National Cyber Crime Investigation Agency from causing any harassment to cricketer Mohammad Rizwan. She has also instructed the wicketkeeper-batsman to join and cooperate fully in the ongoing inquiry proceedings.

Lahore High Court Chief Justice Aalia Neelum has issued directives restraining the National Cyber Crime Investigation Agency (NCCIA) from causing any harassment to cricketer Mohammad Rizwan in connection with an inquiry initiated against him. The Chief Justice has also instructed the prominent wicketkeeper-batsman to actively join and cooperate with the inquiry proceedings.

These instructions were stated in a written court order that emerged on Monday. The order reflects a hearing held on September 17 concerning Rizwan’s plea against the NCCIA inquiry and a call-up notice previously issued to him. The court order also confirmed the dismissal of Rizwan's petition, signifying that while his plea against the inquiry itself was rejected, the court has provided protective directives regarding the conduct of the investigation.

The Court's Directives and Background

The written order from the Lahore High Court Chief Justice Aalia Neelum is a significant development in the ongoing matter involving the national cricketer. It clearly delineates the boundaries for the investigating agency, ensuring that while the inquiry proceeds, it does so within legal and ethical parameters, free from any actions that could be construed as harassment.

The hearing on September 17 addressed the concerns raised by Mohammad Rizwan through his legal representation. His petition sought relief against the inquiry initiated by the NCCIA and the subsequent call-up notice. The court's decision to dismiss the petition indicates that the inquiry itself is deemed legitimate, but the judiciary has asserted its role in overseeing the conduct of state agencies.

By directing Rizwan to cooperate, the court has reinforced the legal obligation of citizens, including public figures, to participate in official inquiries when summoned. This dual approach aims to uphold both the rights of the individual under investigation and the state's authority to investigate potential offenses effectively.

Understanding the NCCIA's Mandate

The National Cyber Crime Investigation Agency operates under the overarching umbrella of the Federal Investigation Agency (FIA). Its primary role is to combat the increasing prevalence of cybercrime across Pakistan. The agency is mandated to investigate a wide array of digital offenses, ranging from financial fraud and data breaches to online harassment, defamation, and the misuse of digital platforms.

In an era of rapid digital transformation, the NCCIA plays a vital role in safeguarding Pakistan's digital and ensuring accountability for online conduct. Its establishment and operation reflect the country's commitment to addressing the complex challenges posed by modern cyber threats and maintaining law and order in the virtual domain.

The agency's operations are guided by specific legal frameworks, primarily the Prevention of Electronic Crimes Act (PECA) 2016. This legislation empowers the NCCIA to conduct investigations, gather digital evidence, and pursue legal action against individuals found to be in violation of cybercrime laws. The Act aims to strike a balance between protecting individual digital rights and preventing the misuse of electronic communication.

Legal Framework and Citizen Rights

The legal system in Pakistan provides mechanisms for individuals to seek redress or protection when they believe their rights are being infringed upon by state agencies. Petitions such as the one filed by Mohammad Rizwan are a common recourse, allowing citizens to bring their grievances before the courts and seek judicial review of administrative actions.

The judiciary, in its capacity as the guardian of fundamental rights, plays a critical role in ensuring that investigative agencies operate within the confines of the law. This oversight is essential for maintaining public trust in state institutions and for upholding the principles of due process and fair treatment for all citizens, regardless of their public standing.

The court's decision to dismiss Rizwan's petition against the inquiry while simultaneously issuing directives against harassment highlights this delicate balance. It acknowledges the legitimate basis for the inquiry but also places clear boundaries on how that inquiry is to be conducted, emphasizing respect for the individual's dignity and legal rights throughout the process.

Balancing Inquiry with Protection

Chief Justice Aalia Neelum's order effectively strikes a balance between the state's to investigate potential wrongdoing and the individual's right to protection from undue pressure or harassment. This approach is fundamental to a just legal system, ensuring that investigations are thorough and fair without becoming oppressive.

The restraint placed upon the NCCIA means that while they can continue their investigation into Mohammad Rizwan, they must do so strictly in accordance with legal procedures. Any actions perceived as harassment or intimidation would be in violation of the court's explicit directives. This provides a layer of protection for the cricketer during the inquiry process.

Conversely, the instruction for Rizwan to cooperate actively in the proceedings places a clear responsibility on him. Cooperation implies providing truthful information, attending summons, and assisting the agency with their legitimate requests for information or evidence. This ensures that the inquiry can progress efficiently and reach a fair conclusion based on facts.

Significance for Public Figures

Mohammad Rizwan, as a celebrated national cricketer, occupies a prominent position in Pakistani society. His public profile means that any legal or investigative matter involving him naturally garners significant attention from the media and the public. Cases involving public figures often serve as important precedents or reminders about legal obligations and rights for all citizens.

The digital age has brought new challenges for public figures, where their online activities, statements, and interactions can quickly become subjects of scrutiny, both public and official. This case serves as a broader reminder that even those in the public eye are subject to the country's laws, particularly those related to online conduct and cybercrime.

The Lahore High Court's intervention highlights the principle that no individual, regardless of their fame or status, is above the law. At the same time, it reaffirms that even during an inquiry, individuals are entitled to protection against harassment and to a fair process, reinforcing the rule of law for everyone.

The Path Forward

With the Lahore High Court's directives now in place, the NCCIA inquiry against Mohammad Rizwan is expected to proceed. The exact nature of the allegations or the specific details that prompted the NCCIA inquiry were not elaborated upon in the written court order that emerged. However, the focus will now shift to the investigation itself and Rizwan's cooperation.

Mohammad Rizwan is now legally bound to join the inquiry proceedings and provide his full cooperation to the investigating agency. The outcome of the inquiry will depend on the findings gathered by the NCCIA, which will determine whether any electronic crime has been committed and what further legal steps, if any, are warranted.

This development highlights the ongoing efforts within Pakistan's legal system to adapt to the complexities of cybercrime, ensuring that investigations are conducted effectively while upholding the fundamental rights and protections afforded to all individuals under the law.
