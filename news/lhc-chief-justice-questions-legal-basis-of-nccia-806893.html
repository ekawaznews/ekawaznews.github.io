LHC Chief Justice Questions Legal Basis of NCCIA

By Umer Javed | 3 October 2026 | 5 min read
Photo: Ann H / Pexels

The Lahore High Court Chief Justice has raised questions about the formation of the National Counter Terrorism and Intelligence Agency, demanding clarity on its legal foundation and parliamentary approval.

The Lahore High Court Chief Justice has raised questions about the formation of the National Counter Terrorism and Intelligence Agency, demanding clarity on its legal foundation and parliamentary approval.

Chief Justice Questions NCCIA Origins

In a recent hearing, the Chief Justice of the Lahore High Court, Justice (retd) Muhammad Qazi, asked the federal government to explain how the NCCIA was established. He highlighted the absence of clear documentation and parliamentary debate surrounding the agency’s creation.

During the session, the Chief Justice requested copies of the legislative acts, executive orders, and any parliamentary resolutions that authorized the NCCIA. He also sought statements from the Attorney General and the Minister of Interior regarding the legal basis of the agency.

Background of NCCIA

The National Counter Terrorism and Intelligence Agency was announced by the federal government in 2017 as part of a broader strategy to consolidate counter‑terrorism intelligence. The agency was designed to coordinate efforts between the Inter‑Services Intelligence, the Federal Investigation Agency, and other security bodies.

According to reports, the NCCIA was intended to simplify information sharing and improve operational readiness against extremist threats. However, its establishment was not accompanied by a formal act of Parliament or a clear statutory framework, a fact that has now come under judicial scrutiny.

Historical Context

Prior to its formation, Pakistan relied on a fragmented network of intelligence agencies that often duplicated efforts. The decision to create a single umbrella agency was presented as a necessary step to enhance national security. The government claimed that the NCCIA would operate under the guidance of the National Security Council.

The announcement of the NCCIA coincided with a series of high‑profile terrorist incidents, prompting lawmakers to call for a more coordinated approach. Nonetheless, the lack of a detailed legislative process left many questions unanswered.

Legal Basis and Parliamentary Oversight

Justice Qazi’s questions touch on the principle that major security agencies must be established through a transparent legislative process. He noted that the Constitution requires any new security body to be authorized by an act of Parliament to ensure accountability and oversight.

He asked whether the NCCIA was created by a presidential ordinance, an executive order, or a parliamentary act. The Chief Justice also sought clarity on whether the National Assembly debated the agency’s mandate and budget.

Parliamentary Procedure

In the absence of a clear parliamentary record, the Chief Justice demanded that the government provide minutes from any sessions where the NCCIA was discussed. He emphasized the importance of parliamentary scrutiny in maintaining democratic oversight over security institutions.

According to reports, no official parliamentary debate has been recorded in the public domain, raising concerns about the legitimacy of the agency’s authority.

Government Response

The federal government responded by stating that the NCCIA was established through a presidential ordinance in 2017, citing the need for swift action in the face of escalating terrorism threats.

The Attorney General confirmed that the ordinance was later ratified by the National Assembly, though the exact details of the vote were not disclosed. The Minister of Interior added that the agency operates under the supervision of the National Security Council and is subject to periodic reviews.

Legal Challenges

Lawyers and civil society groups have expressed concern that the creation of the NCCIA without a clear legislative framework may undermine the rule of law. They argue that the agency’s powers could exceed what is permissible under the Constitution without proper parliamentary endorsement.

According to reports, a petition has been filed in the Supreme Court seeking clarification on the agency’s legal status. The petition claims that the absence of a statutory basis could lead to potential misuse of power and erosion of civil liberties.

Implications for Counter‑Terrorism Efforts

The NCCIA’s mandate includes gathering intelligence on extremist networks, coordinating with military and law enforcement agencies, and providing actionable data to prevent attacks. Its effectiveness depends on clear legal authority and accountability mechanisms.

Critics argue that the lack of a formal legislative framework could hinder cooperation with foreign intelligence partners, who often require a documented legal basis before sharing sensitive information.

Public Safety and Oversight

Public safety is a top priority for the federal government, and the NCCIA is positioned as a central pillar in that strategy. However, the absence of transparent oversight raises questions about the agency’s operational procedures and data protection practices.

The Chief Justice has requested that the agency submit a report on its internal checks and balances, including how it handles confidential information and ensures compliance with human rights standards.

Judicial Oversight

In his remarks, Justice Qazi highlighted the judiciary’s role in safeguarding constitutional rights while supporting national security. He urged the federal authorities to provide a detailed legal justification for the NCCIA’s existence, ensuring that the agency operates within the bounds of the law.

He also suggested that the Supreme Court could play a role in reviewing the agency’s statutory foundation if the matter escalated to the highest court.

Public Reaction

The public reaction has been mixed. Some security experts applaud the creation of a unified counter‑terrorism body, while others fear that the lack of transparency may lead to unchecked powers.

Civil society organizations have called for a public hearing to examine the NCCIA’s legal framework and to ensure that the agency remains accountable to elected representatives.

Next Steps and Legal Process

The Lahore High Court has directed the federal government to submit all relevant documents within two weeks. The court will review the material and decide whether the NCCIA’s establishment requires further judicial scrutiny.

Meanwhile, the Supreme Court is expected to weigh in on the petition filed by legal advocates. The outcome of these proceedings will determine whether the NCCIA can continue to operate without a formal legislative mandate.

In the meantime, the government has promised to conduct an internal audit of the NCCIA’s operations and to report findings to Parliament, aiming to restore confidence in the agency’s compliance with constitutional norms.

Conclusion

The questioning of the NCCIA’s legal foundation by the Lahore High Court Chief Justice highlights the importance of democratic oversight in national security matters. The forthcoming judicial review and parliamentary scrutiny will shape the future of Pakistan’s counter‑terrorism strategy and its adherence to constitutional principles.
