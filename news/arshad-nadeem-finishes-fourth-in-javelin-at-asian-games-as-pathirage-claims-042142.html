Arshad Nadeem Finishes Fourth in Javelin at Asian Games as Pathirage Claims Gold

By Umer Javed | 28 September 2026 | 3 min read
Photo: Ali Creative / Pexels

Arshad Nadeem threw a season-best 80.29m but finished fourth in the javelin final at the Asian Games in Nagoya, as Sri Lanka's Rumesh Pathirage claimed gold in rainy conditions.

Arshad Nadeem finished fourth in the men's javelin throw final at the Asian Games in Nagoya, Japan, as Sri Lanka's Rumesh Pathirage claimed the gold medal in a rain-soaked competition at the Mizuho Park Athletic Stadium on Monday.

Olympic Champion Faces Difficult Conditions

The Olympic champion from Pakistan competed under heavy skies and on a slick runway, facing challenging conditions from the start of the competition. Nadeem managed 76.69m on his first attempt before committing a foul on his second throw, putting immediate pressure on his medal hopes.

The Pakistani athlete improved drastically on his third attempt with a season-best throw of 80.29m, which moved him up to fourth place in the standings. However, he was unable to build on this performance, as three consecutive fouls followed, ending his challenge for a podium finish.

Pathirage Lives Up to Expectations

Pathirage, the World No. 1 and heavy favorite for the gold medal, lived up to his billing in the final. The Sri Lankan threw with precision and consistency throughout the competition, securing the gold medal in front of the home crowd in Nagoya.

The victory marks a significant achievement for Pathirage, who has established himself as the dominant force in men's javelin throwing in Asia. His performance in the rainy conditions demonstrated his adaptability and technical skill on a difficult runway.

Nadeem's Season Best Despite Disappointment

Despite finishing outside the medals, Nadeem's season-best throw of 80.29m represents a significant achievement in itself. The distance places him among the top performers in the competition and demonstrates his continued ability to compete at the highest level of international athletics.

The Pakistani athlete has been a consistent performer in international javelin competitions since winning gold at the Tokyo Olympics. His technique and power have made him one of the most dangerous throwers in the world, and his performance in Nagoya, while not resulting in a medal, showed his competitive edge remains sharp.

Rainy Conditions Affect Competition

The rainy weather in Nagoya created challenging conditions for all competitors in the javelin event. The slick runway made it difficult for athletes to maintain their usual footing and technique, leading to several fouls throughout the competition, including three consecutive misses from Nadeem after his best throw.

The Mizuho Park Athletic Stadium, located in the city of Nagoya, hosted the athletics events for the Asian Games. The venue provided a challenging environment for throwers, with the wet conditions adding an extra element of difficulty to an already demanding discipline.

Pakistan's Athletics Program

Nadeem's performance continues Pakistan's tradition of excellence in javelin throwing, a discipline where the country has historically produced world-class athletes. His Olympic gold medal in Tokyo raised the profile of athletics in Pakistan and inspired a new generation of throwers.

The Pakistani athletics federation has been working to improve training facilities and support for field event athletes. Nadeem's success has helped secure increased funding and attention for the sport in the country, though challenges remain in developing depth across all throwing events.

Looking Ahead

With this performance at the Asian Games, Nadeem will look to build on his season-best throw in upcoming competitions. The fourth-place finish, while not matching his Olympic achievement, still represents a strong result against world-class competition in difficult conditions.

The javelin event at the Asian Games showcased the depth of talent in Asian athletics, with athletes from multiple countries competing for medals. As the season progresses, all eyes will be on how Nadeem and Pathirage continue to develop their performances ahead of future major championships.
