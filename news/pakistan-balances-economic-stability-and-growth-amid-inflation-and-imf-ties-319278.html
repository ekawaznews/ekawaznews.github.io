Pakistan Balances Economic Stability and Growth Amid Inflation and IMF Ties

By Umer Javed | 24 September 2026 | 3 min read
Photo: Mikhail Nilov / Pexels

Pakistan must balance macroeconomic stability with growth ambitions, a Dawn article notes. The country faces high inflation, fiscal deficits, and IMF program demands.

Pakistan faces a dilemma between maintaining economic stability and pursuing rapid growth, according to a recent Dawn analysis.

Economic

Inflation has risen to its highest level in nearly two decades, with consumer prices climbing steadily across the country. The government has responded by tightening fiscal policy, aiming to curb the widening deficit. The central bank has raised policy rates to keep inflation in check, but the higher cost of borrowing can slow investment.

Fiscal Discipline vs Growth Initiatives

The national budget shows a fiscal deficit that has expanded in recent quarters, driven by rising subsidies and public sector spending. In response, the finance ministry has announced measures to reduce expenditure, including targeted cuts in non‑essential areas. At the same time, the government is investing in infrastructure projects that promise to boost long‑term productivity.

Monetary Policy and Inflation

The State Bank of Pakistan has maintained a policy rate of 6.5 percent, a level above the global average. This stance is designed to restrain price growth and preserve the currency’s purchasing power. However, higher rates may dampen credit demand from businesses and households.

External Debt and IMF Program

Pakistan’s external debt stands at nearly 80 percent of its GDP, a figure that has attracted scrutiny from international lenders. The country is currently under an IMF programme that requires fiscal consolidation and structural reforms. Meeting these conditions is essential to secure continued financial support.

Sectoral Growth Prospects

Manufacturing and services sectors have shown resilience, with export volumes rising in the last quarter. The agricultural sector remains a key driver of employment, yet it faces challenges from water shortages and climate variability. Energy projects, particularly in renewables, are gaining momentum as part of the government’s diversification strategy.

Challenges Ahead

Balancing macroeconomic stability with growth is complicated by external shocks such as global commodity price swings and regional security concerns. The government must also address structural issues like tax collection gaps and regulatory inefficiencies. Public confidence in the economic plan is fragile, with many households worried about rising living costs.

Policy Options

One option is to maintain a tight fiscal stance while gradually easing monetary policy as inflation falls. Another path involves targeted stimulus to high‑growth sectors, coupled with reforms that improve the business environment. Both routes require careful sequencing to avoid triggering a recession.

Public Perception

Surveys indicate that citizens prioritize job creation and affordable food over short‑term growth metrics. The perception that government measures are primarily aimed at controlling inflation rather than expanding opportunities can influence consumer confidence.

Future Outlook

Analysts predict that if the government can keep inflation under control while implementing growth‑oriented reforms, Pakistan could achieve a moderate growth rate of around 5 percent in the next fiscal year. The success of this plan hinges on the timely execution of fiscal consolidation and the ability to attract foreign investment.

Conclusion

Pakistan’s path forward demands a careful balance between stability and growth. The country must navigate these twin priorities while responding to the demands of international partners and domestic stakeholders.

The article appears in Dawn and provides a detailed examination of the country’s economic strategy.
