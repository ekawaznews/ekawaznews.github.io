Pakistan Inflation Slows to 10.75% in September Amid Gulf War Uncertainty

By Umer Javed | 27 September 2026 | 4 min read
Photo: Markus Winkler / Pexels

Inflation is projected to ease to 10.75% in September, while oil prices remain volatile due to the Gulf conflict. Analysts warn that US rejection of Iran's proposal could push prices higher.

Inflation in Pakistan is projected to slow to 10.75% in September, according to recent economic forecasts.

Inflation Forecast

The latest figures released by the State Bank of Pakistan indicate a modest decline in the consumer price index for the month of September. This easing follows a series of upward adjustments in the previous quarter, when inflation hovered above 12% amid rising food and energy costs. The 10.75% figure represents a 0.3 percentage point reduction from August, signalling a potential shift in the inflationary trajectory.

Economists note that the slowdown is not driven by a sudden drop in domestic demand but rather by a temporary flattening of global commodity prices. The global oil market has seen a brief dip as supply concerns ease, and the local currency has strengthened against the dollar, reducing the cost of imported goods. Analysts expect the trend to continue if global price pressures remain subdued.

Oil Prices Amid Gulf Conflict

Stakeholders in the financial sector have maintained that oil prices will remain between $90 and $100 per barrel during the remaining days of September and into October. This range reflects the prevailing uncertainty in the Gulf region, where tensions have escalated following the recent announcement by President Donald Trump that he had rejected Iran’s proposal for a ceasefire.

According to reports, Iran now anticipates possible attacks from the United States, heightening fears that oil prices could surge. The potential for conflict in the Persian Gulf has historically amplified volatility in the energy market, and traders are closely monitoring diplomatic developments for any sign of escalation.

Analysts predict that oil prices will stay near $100 per barrel if the war continues, citing the limited supply routes through the Strait of Hormuz and the strategic importance of the region. Even a brief spike in price could reverberate across Pakistan’s import bill, affecting the balance of payments and potentially stalling the current easing of inflation.

Impact on Pakistani Economy

The projected slowdown in inflation comes at a time when the Pakistani government is grappling with a high debt burden and a need to stabilize the national currency. A reduction in inflation can ease pressure on the central bank’s monetary policy, allowing for a more flexible stance in the coming months.

However, the possibility of a sudden rise in oil prices could counteract these gains. Import duties on petroleum products represent a significant share of the government’s revenue, and an increase in global oil prices could lead to higher domestic fuel costs, eroding the gains from the inflationary slowdown.

Government and Market Response

The State Bank of Pakistan has reiterated its commitment to maintaining price stability while supporting economic growth. In a recent statement, the governor confirmed that the central bank would monitor the oil market closely and adjust policy tools if necessary to safeguard the economy.

In the stock market, shares of energy companies have experienced modest gains in response to the expectation of stable oil prices. Conversely, consumer goods firms have seen a slight dip as traders anticipate a possible rebound in import costs should oil prices climb.

Historical Context and Future Outlook

Pakistan’s inflation has fluctuated significantly over the past decade, with peaks above 20% during the early 2010s and a more recent decline to the low teens. The current 10.75% forecast aligns with a gradual trend toward stabilization, yet external shocks such as the Gulf war could derail the trajectory.

In light of the ongoing geopolitical tensions, analysts advise caution. They argue that the economy’s resilience will depend on the government’s ability to manage import costs and maintain investor confidence. Should oil prices surge, the central bank may need to tighten monetary policy to prevent a resurgence in inflation.

Conclusion

Pakistan’s inflation forecast for September offers a glimmer of relief amid a complex global backdrop. While the current projection suggests a modest easing, the volatile oil market linked to the Gulf conflict remains a key variable. Policymakers and investors alike will watch the developments closely, as any sudden shift could reshape the country’s economic outlook in the coming months.
