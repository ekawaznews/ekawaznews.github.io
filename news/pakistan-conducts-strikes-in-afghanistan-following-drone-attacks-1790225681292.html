Pakistan Conducts Strikes in Afghanistan Following Drone Attacks

U
Umer Javed
Political Reporter
| 🕒 24 September 2026 at 09:54 am | 📖 6 min read
Share: 💬 WhatsApp 📘 Facebook ✈️ Telegram 🐦 Twitter
📷 Photo: Danila Popov / Pexels | ekawaznews.github.io

Pakistan conducted precision aerial and drone strikes at 10 locations within Afghanistan in the early hours of Thursday, responding to recent drone attacks launched from the neighbouring country a day earlier. The action was confirmed by Information Minister Attaullah Tarar, who announced the strikes via a post on social media platform X.

Minister Tarar stated that these operations were carried out “in exercise of its sovereign right to self-defence.” He elaborated that the strikes were directed “against 10 locations in Afghanistan used for launching and storing these drones.” This marks a significant development in the ongoing security challenges along the shared border between the two nations.

The Information Minister further characterised the strikes as “deliberate, precise, calibrated and proportionate, and strictly limited to identified targets.” This specific phrasing highlights the government's intention to convey a measured, targeted response aimed at specific threats rather than a broader military engagement.

Official Justification and Policy Stance

The Pakistani government has consistently voiced its concerns regarding the use of Afghan soil by militant groups to launch attacks into Pakistan. These concerns have been a recurring theme in diplomatic exchanges and public statements, particularly since the return of the Taliban to power in Afghanistan.

For years, Pakistan has been grappling with the resurgence of terrorist activities, especially from groups like the Tehrik-i-Taliban Pakistan (TTP). These groups, according to Pakistani authorities, have found sanctuaries in Afghanistan, from where they plan and execute attacks against Pakistani security forces and civilians.

The decision to conduct cross-border strikes reflects a long-held position by Pakistan that it reserves the right to defend its territory and its people from external threats. This principle of self-defence is enshrined in international law, which allows a state to take necessary and proportionate action against imminent threats originating from another state, particularly if the host state is unable or unwilling to address those threats.

Minister Tarar's statement highlights the government's commitment to protecting its national interests and ensuring the safety of its citizens. The emphasis on precision and proportionality suggests an effort to minimise collateral damage and avoid escalating tensions beyond the immediate security.

Background of Cross-Border Tensions

The border between Pakistan and Afghanistan, often referred to as the Durand Line, has historically been a source of complex challenges. Its porous nature and the presence of rugged terrain have made effective border management difficult for decades.

The security situation along this border has deteriorated significantly in recent times. Pakistan has witnessed a sharp increase in terrorist incidents, many of which are attributed to groups operating from Afghanistan. These incidents have included attacks on military installations, law enforcement personnel, and civilian targets.

Pakistan has repeatedly urged the interim Afghan government to take decisive action against these militant groups. Diplomatic channels have been utilised to convey these demands, with Pakistan stressing the importance of Afghanistan fulfilling its international obligations not to allow its territory to be used for terrorism against other states.

Despite these overtures, Pakistani officials have expressed frustration over what they perceive as a lack of concrete action by the Afghan authorities. This perceived inaction has led to a growing belief within Pakistan's security establishment that more direct measures might be necessary to safeguard national security.

Pakistan's Security Imperatives

The security of Pakistan's western border is a matter of vital national importance. The country has endured a prolonged period of conflict and instability due to terrorism, incurring significant human and economic costs.

The recent drone attacks launched from Afghanistan represent a direct threat to Pakistan's sovereignty and its internal peace. Such incidents necessitate a strong response to deter future aggressions and dismantle the infrastructure supporting these attacks.

Pakistan's military leadership has consistently affirmed its resolve to eliminate terrorism from its soil. This commitment extends to addressing threats that emanate from across its borders, especially when those threats directly endanger Pakistani lives and property.

The government's actions highlight a strategic shift towards a more assertive posture in confronting cross-border terrorism. This approach is rooted in the understanding that sustained peace and stability within Pakistan are inextricably linked to a secure western frontier.

Diplomatic Ramifications and Regional Stability

The precision strikes in Afghanistan are likely to have significant diplomatic ramifications. While Pakistan asserts its right to self-defence, such actions can strain bilateral relations and draw international attention to the volatile regional security.

The interim Afghan government has, in the past, reacted strongly to any perceived violations of its sovereignty. This latest development could lead to a renewed cycle of accusations and counter-accusations, further complicating efforts to foster stability in the region.

Regional powers and international bodies will be closely observing the situation. The delicate balance of power and the ongoing humanitarian crisis in Afghanistan mean that any military action carries broader implications for regional peace and security.

Pakistan has consistently advocated for a stable and peaceful Afghanistan, recognising that instability in the neighbouring country directly impacts its own security. However, the current actions signal that Pakistan's patience with the cross-border threat has reached a critical point, necessitating a direct response.

Broader Context of Regional Dynamics

The security situation in Afghanistan has been a complex issue for the international community. Since the withdrawal of international forces, concerns have persisted about the potential for Afghanistan to once again become a haven for various militant groups.

Pakistan, as a frontline state, bears a disproportionate burden of the consequences of instability in Afghanistan. The influx of refugees, the rise in cross-border smuggling, and the persistent threat of terrorism are all direct outcomes that impact Pakistan's socio-economic fabric.

The latest strikes by Pakistan should be viewed within this broader context of regional dynamics, where national security interests often intersect with complex geopolitical realities. Pakistan's actions are a clear message that it will not tolerate the use of Afghan territory for hostile activities against its people.

This firm stance is consistent with Pakistan's long-standing policy of non-interference in the internal affairs of other states, while simultaneously upholding its inherent right to protect its own borders and citizens from external aggression. The government's communication strategy, as evidenced by Minister Tarar's prompt announcement, aims to articulate this position clearly both domestically and internationally.

Looking Ahead

The immediate aftermath of these strikes will be closely monitored for any reactions from the interim Afghan government and other regional actors. The potential for escalation, while always a concern in such situations, appears to be managed through Pakistan's stated intent of proportionate and targeted action.

The coming days will likely see further diplomatic exchanges as Pakistan reiterates its security concerns and justifies its actions on the international stage. The international community will be looking for signs of de-escalation and a renewed commitment to addressing the root causes of cross-border terrorism.

For Pakistan, the objective remains clear: to secure its borders and ensure that no entity can use Afghan soil to threaten its national security. This latest military action serves as a stark reminder of the serious challenges Pakistan faces and its determination to confront them head-on.

The government will continue to pursue a comprehensive strategy that combines strong border security measures with diplomatic efforts to encourage Afghanistan to fulfill its commitments. The long-term stability of the region hinges on effective cooperation between all stakeholders to counter the menace of terrorism.
