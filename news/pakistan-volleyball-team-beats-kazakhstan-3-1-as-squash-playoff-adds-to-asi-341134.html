Pakistan volleyball team beats Kazakhstan 3-1 as squash playoff adds to Asian Games medal haul

By Umer Javed | 28 September 2026 | 6 min read
Photo: Pavel Danilyuk / Pexels

Pakistan's men's volleyball team opened their Asian Games campaign with a 3-1 win over Kazakhstan while Noor Zaman defeated Mohammad Ashab Irfan in an all-Pakistan squash playoff. The country's medal tally now stands at four bronze medals at the Aichi-Nagoya Games.

Pakistan's men's volleyball team opened their Asian Games campaign with a 3-1 victory over Kazakhstan on Sunday while Noor Zaman defeated compatriot Mohammad Ashab Irfan in an all-Pakistan squash playoff as the country's medal tally reached four bronze medals at the Aichi-Nagoya Games.

The volleyball side recovered from losing the third set to win 25-16, 25-22, 17-25, 25-20 in their Pool A opener at the Okazaki Chuo Sogo Park Gymnasium. Murad Khan led the scoring for Pakistan with 21 points according to match statistics released by the tournament organisers.

Volleyball team finds rhythm after third-set dip

Pakistan started strongly in the first two sets, controlling the tempo with effective serving and blocking. The opening set saw the team build an early lead and close it out comfortably at 25-16. The second set was tighter with Kazakhstan pushing hard but Pakistan held their nerve to take it 25-22.

A lapse in concentration cost Pakistan the third set as Kazakhstan capitalised on unforced errors to win 25-17. The fourth set saw Pakistan regroup and restore their advantage, finishing the match 25-20 to secure three points in the group standings.

Team officials said the comeback in the fourth set demonstrated the squad's mental resilience. The coaching staff had emphasised maintaining discipline during the break between sets according to reports from the team camp.

Murad Khan leads attack with 21 points

Opposite hitter Murad Khan emerged as Pakistan's top performer with 21 points comprising 18 spikes, two blocks and one ace serve. His consistency in the first, second and fourth sets provided the platform for the victory.

Other contributors included the setter who distributed the ball effectively and the libero whose reception stability allowed the offence to function. The middle blockers combined for five blocks across the four sets according to the official match report.

Pakistan will next face Uzbekistan on Monday before taking on hosts Japan in their final group match. The match against Japan is expected to be the toughest test in the pool given the host nation's home advantage and higher world ranking.

All-Pakistan squash playoff decides last-16 spot

In squash, Noor Zaman overcame Mohammad Ashab Irfan in an all-Pakistan playoff to advance to the round of 16. The match was arranged after both players finished in positions that required a playoff to determine progression in the individual event.

Zaman won in straight games according to tournament sources. The victory ensures Pakistan retains a presence in the latter stages of the men's singles draw. Irfan's campaign ends at the playoff stage despite a solid showing in the group phase.

Pakistan has a proud history in Asian Games squash with multiple gold medals in previous editions. The current generation is working to rebuild that legacy after a period of transition in the national programme.

Medal tally reaches four bronze

With the conclusion of the first few days of competition, Pakistan's overall medal tally stands at four bronze medals. The medals have come from different disciplines reflecting a spread across the contingent.

Officials from the Pakistan Olympic Association said the early medals provide momentum for the remaining athletes. The association has set a target of improving on the medal count from the last Asian Games according to statements made before the delegation's departure.

The bronze medals have been won in weightlifting, wrestling and two other sports according to the official medal tracker. Detailed breakdowns of each medal event were not immediately available from the mission headquarters.

Asian Games context and Pakistan's preparation

The 19th Asian Games are being held in Aichi and Nagoya prefectures in Japan after a one-year postponement from their original 2022 schedule. The multi-sport event features over 12,000 athletes from 45 nations competing across 40 sports.

Pakistan sent a delegation of approximately 150 athletes competing in 20 sports according to the final entry list published by the Pakistan Sports Board. The contingent includes seasoned campaigners and debutants across individual and team events.

Preparation for the Games was affected by domestic scheduling challenges and funding constraints according to sports administrators. Several federations reported limited international exposure in the lead-up period which may impact performance in the early rounds.

Volleyball pool dynamics and path ahead

Pool A consists of four teams with the top two advancing to the quarter-finals. Pakistan's win over Kazakhstan gives them an early advantage in the race for a knockout spot. Uzbekistan lost their opener to Japan according to the pool standings.

The match against Uzbekistan on Monday is considered a must-win for Pakistan to control their own destiny. A victory would likely secure a quarter-final berth before the final group match against Japan.

Japan entered the tournament as one of the favourites having won the Asian Championship recently. Their home crowd support and familiarity with conditions add to the challenge for visiting teams.

Squash programme building for future success

The Pakistan Squash Federation has invested in a development pipeline aimed at restoring the country's dominance in the sport. The current Asian Games squad includes players who have come through the junior ranks with international exposure.

Noor Zaman's progress to the round of 16 is seen as a positive indicator for the programme. The federation plans to use the Asian Games experience to identify areas for improvement ahead of the World Championships later this year.

Mohammad Ashab Irfan's participation in the playoff itself represents progress given his age and experience level. The federation's long-term strategy includes sending younger players to major games for exposure.

Mission leadership monitors progress daily

Chef de Mission of the Pakistan contingent holds daily briefings with team managers to track athlete welfare and competition readiness. Medical staff are on standby for any injury concerns with the compressed schedule.

The mission has established a recovery centre at the team hotel equipped with physiotherapy and cryotherapy facilities. Nutritionists are working with each sport to optimise meal plans for competition days.

Security and logistics coordination with the Japanese organisers has been smooth according to the mission leadership. Transport arrangements for training and competition venues are operating on schedule.

Public interest and media coverage back home

Back in Pakistan, sports fans are following the Games through television broadcasts and digital platforms. The Pakistan Television Corporation is providing daily highlights packages for prime-time audiences.

Social media engagement has been high with volleyball and squash matches generating significant discussion. Former internationals from both sports have been offering analysis and encouragement to the current squad members.

The Pakistan Sports Board has urged the public to support all athletes regardless of results. The board chairman emphasised that participation at this level represents years of dedication by athletes and their support teams.

Looking ahead to week two of competition

As the Games enter their second week, Pakistan's focus shifts to sports where medal prospects are considered stronger. Wrestling, weightlifting and athletics events are scheduled in the coming days.

The wrestling team includes multiple Asian medallists who are expected to contend for podium finishes. Their weight categories have drawn competitive fields but the Pakistani wrestlers have recent form on their side.

In athletics, the javelin and sprint events feature Pakistani athletes who have met the qualifying standards. The coaching staff has managed their competition schedules to peak at the Games according to the national federation.

The volleyball team's next assignment against Uzbekistan will be closely watched as a barometer of their quarter-final credentials. A win would set up a decisive final group match against Japan with a potential knockout spot already secured.
