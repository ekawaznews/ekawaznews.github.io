JI Chief Announces Islamabad March Against Petroleum Levy on September 20

U
Umer Javed
Staff Reporter
| 🕒 13 September 2026 at 09:39 pm | 📖 5 min read
Share: 💬 WhatsApp 📘 Facebook ✈️ Telegram 🐦 Twitter
📷 Photo: Ann H / Pexels | ekawaznews.github.io

Jamaat-e-Islami (JI) chief Sirajul Haq has announced that his party will stage a march on Islamabad on September 20 to protest the government's continued imposition of a petroleum levy. The announcement was made as the party intensifies its campaign against what it describes as anti-people economic policies.

Sirajul Haq described this upcoming demonstration as entering a 'decisive phase' in the party's efforts to compel the government to reconsider its fiscal measures. The JI leadership has consistently voiced strong opposition to the rising cost of living, with the petroleum levy being a central point of contention.

The petroleum levy is a charge imposed by the government on petroleum products, collected at the source of production or import. It serves as a significant non-tax revenue stream for the national exchequer, playing an important role in the country's fiscal management.

Governments often employ this levy as a means to generate revenue to meet budgetary targets, reduce fiscal deficits, and fund development projects. It is a direct tax on consumers, as it adds to the final price of petrol, diesel, and other petroleum products, impacting daily expenditures for households and businesses.

For ordinary citizens, the petroleum levy translates into higher fuel prices, which in turn contributes to inflation across various sectors. Increased transportation costs affect the prices of essential commodities, making daily life more expensive for the average Pakistani household.

Background to the Protest

Pakistan has been grappling with persistent economic challenges, including high inflation rates and a soaring cost of living. The prices of essential goods, food items, and utilities have seen a steep increase over recent months, putting immense pressure on household budgets.

The government's reliance on the petroleum levy has often been linked to its commitments under international financial agreements, particularly with the International Monetary Fund (IMF). Such agreements frequently stipulate measures for revenue generation and fiscal consolidation.

These measures, while aimed at stabilizing the national economy, often result in increased burdens on the populace through taxes and levies. The petroleum levy is one such instrument that directly impacts the purchasing power of citizens, leading to public discontent and calls for relief.

The economic, marked by currency depreciation and global commodity price fluctuations, further complicates the situation. While external factors play a role, domestic policy decisions, including the imposition of various levies, are frequently cited by opposition parties as exacerbating public hardships.

The Jamaat-e-Islami's Stance

The Jamaat-e-Islami, a prominent religious-political party, has a history of advocating for public welfare and standing against government policies perceived as detrimental to the common person. The party often positions itself as a voice for the marginalized and those affected by economic downturns.

Under the leadership of Sirajul Haq, JI has consistently organized protests and rallies across the country to highlight issues such as inflation, unemployment, and what it terms as 'bad governance'. The party's focus on the petroleum levy aligns with its broader agenda of seeking economic justice for the masses.

JI leaders and workers have engaged in various forms of agitation, including street protests, sit-ins, and public awareness campaigns. Their messaging often emphasizes the need for the government to provide relief to citizens rather than imposing additional financial burdens through indirect taxation.

The party's call for an Islamabad march is an escalation of its ongoing protest movement. It aims to gather a large number of participants from different parts of the country to demonstrate the scale of public resentment against current economic policies, particularly the petroleum levy.

Implications of the March

The planned march on September 20 carries several implications for the political and the government. Such large-scale demonstrations are typically intended to exert pressure on the ruling dispensation to review its policies and consider public demands.

A significant turnout in Islamabad could amplify the call for a reduction or removal of the petroleum levy, potentially forcing the government to address the issue more directly. Political parties often use such events to galvanize their support base and demonstrate their strength.

The government, on its part, will likely monitor the situation closely. Historically, authorities have responded to such protests through a combination of security measures and, at times, attempts at dialogue with the protesting parties. The aim is often to maintain law and order while addressing the core grievances to an extent.

Public marches in the capital city can also disrupt normal life and administrative functions, making them a potent tool for political opposition. The timing of this march, amidst ongoing economic difficulties, adds to its potential impact.

Previous Instances of Public Agitation

Pakistan has a long history of public protests and political movements against rising prices and economic hardships. Citizens and political parties have frequently taken to the streets to express their discontent over inflation, utility price hikes, and new taxation measures.

In the past, various governments have faced similar challenges from opposition parties and civil society organizations demanding relief from economic pressures. These movements often draw strength from the widespread impact of economic policies on daily household budgets.

The effectiveness of such protests has varied, with some leading to government concessions or policy adjustments, while others have served primarily to raise public awareness and consolidate political opposition. They represent an important aspect of democratic expression in the country.

The public sentiment regarding economic policies remains highly sensitive, particularly when it involves essential commodities like fuel. Any measure that directly increases the cost of living is often met with strong reactions from various segments of society.

As September 20 approaches, the focus will remain on the preparations for the march and the government's response to the growing calls for economic relief. The outcome of this demonstration could set a precedent for future public engagement on economic policy matters.
