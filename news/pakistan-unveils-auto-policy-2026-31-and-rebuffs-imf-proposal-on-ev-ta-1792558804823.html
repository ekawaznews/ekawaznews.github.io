Pakistan Unveils Auto Policy 2026-31 and Rebuffs IMF Proposal on EV Taxes

Approval of Auto Policy 2026-31 and Rejection of IMF Tax Demands

The federal government has officially approved the new Automotive Policy 2026-31, establishing an extensive framework aimed at expanding local manufacturing, boosting overseas shipments, and encouraging cleaner automotive technologies. As reported by Dunya News, the five-year plan concentrates heavily on elevating international sales while expanding the adoption of electric transport across the country. The release of the policy marks a transition in state priorities toward export orientation and green mobility.

A central element of the development is the government decision to turn down a proposal from the International Monetary Fund to raise the sales tax on electric vehicles to 18 per cent. ARY News reported that authorities formally declined the suggestion, choosing instead to protect early-stage adoption of clean energy transport from elevated consumption levies. Pakistan Connect also confirmed the rejection of the multilateral lender recommendation, indicating that economic planners preferred industrial growth incentives over immediate tax collection increases.

The policy document indicates that the state intends to expand overall vehicle exports to 6 billion dollars over the course of the program. To achieve this objective, economic authorities have introduced an overhaul of tariff levels, targeted production benchmarks, and financing options intended to attract consumers toward newer automotive platforms. The plan also incorporates gradual decreases in vehicle import duties alongside increased expectations for local components manufacturing.

Tax Structure and Financing Relief for Electric Vehicles

Under the provisions outlined in the policy document, battery electric vehicles will continue to enjoy a concessional sales tax rate of one per cent from 2026 until 2031. This low rate is intended to keep entry-level electric cars within reach of urban drivers seeking relief from elevated fuel expenses. ARY News reported that other forms of electrified transport will operate under separate tiers, with range-extended electric vehicles facing a six per cent sales tax and plug-in hybrid cars subject to a nine per cent rate.

While entry and mid-range electric cars receive favorable tax treatment, the policy introduces an additional charge on luxury variants. Officials have proposed a 10 per cent federal excise duty on electric vehicles valued at more than 75,000 dollars. This distinction ensures that higher-income purchasers of high-end foreign models contribute directly to public revenues, even as smaller zero-emission vehicles receive state fiscal backing.

To assist middle-income buyers, the framework lowers import duties on electric cars through a structured schedule, offering specialized tariff reductions for compact units priced under 15,000 dollars. Credit access has also been expanded. The borrowing ceiling for electric vehicle financing is set to rise from Rs3 million to Rs10 million. In parallel, commercial lenders will be permitted to offer extended repayment horizons, with the maximum financing period expanding from three years to five years.

Export Targets and Penalties for Vehicle Assemblers

The 2026-31 policy introduces rigorous performance requirements for domestic automotive manufacturers, moving away from past protectionist structures that lacked export conditions. As detailed by ARY News, vehicle makers will now operate under mandatory export quotas. Assemblers that satisfy these targets will receive financial compensation equal to five per cent of their total export value, providing a direct subsidy to improve price competitiveness in foreign markets.

Car companies that exceed their baseline performance can secure even greater public assistance. An additional 10 per cent incentive has been incorporated for export volumes that surpass shipments recorded during the preceding year. This two-tier incentive design is constructed to reward assemblers that demonstrate continuous growth in regional and international commerce, rather than those relying entirely on domestic market sales.

Strict consequences will accompany failure to meet the required trade levels. Assemblers that fall short of their assigned export targets will be subject to fiscal penalties. If a manufacturer fails to fulfill its assigned quotas for two consecutive years, regulatory bodies may cancel its official manufacturing certificate. This disciplinary measure signals a stricter regulatory approach toward firms that fail to develop export capabilities.

Automotive Development Levy and Domestic Industry Upgrades

To finance industrial modernization and support component vendors, the government has proposed the introduction of an Automotive Development Levy. According to ARY News, this levy will apply specifically to automobiles fitted with engine displacements exceeding 1,000cc. Smaller, entry-level engine cars will be exempted from the charge to protect budget-conscious buyers from higher showroom prices.

Official projections state that the Automotive Development Levy could yield more than Rs232 billion over its five-year tenure. The resulting funds will be channelled into dedicated industrial development initiatives, technological modernization programs for parts vendors, and the payout of export incentives. Necessary statutory legislation to enact the levy is targeted for formal completion by December 2026.

The parts manufacturing segment has been assigned specific milestones under the strategy. Authorities have set an export target of 1.2 billion dollars for locally made auto components by fiscal year 2030-31. Assemblers are required to increase their procurement of domestic components, and companies that fall short of local production targets could forfeit their eligibility for government incentives. Across the broader sector, tariffs are scheduled to decrease gradually to 15 per cent by 2030-31 to encourage operational efficiency.

Safety Standards, Consumer Protections, and Industry Oversight

Beyond fiscal measures, the policy establishes updated regulatory requirements for build quality and customer rights. ARY News reported that global safety standards will become mandatory for both locally assembled models and completely built imported automobiles. This change is designed to eliminate differences between vehicles sold in Pakistan and those distributed in overseas markets where vehicle safety laws are strict.

Consumer rights are also given formal status in assembler contracts. Car manufacturers will be legally required to incorporate clear consumer protection terms within their sales agreements with automobile purchasers. These clauses are meant to prevent unilateral contract revisions, address vehicle delivery delays, and guarantee post-sale customer support for ordinary vehicle owners across the country.

Regulatory monitoring will be reinforced through regular institutional reviews. The policy outlines two formal audits every year to guarantee financial and operational transparency across the automotive industry. Payouts distributed under export incentive programs and local vendor assistance funds will face independent third-party audits to prevent misuse. The policy also calls for the establishment of the Pakistan Automotive Testing Institute to manage vehicle certification, industry research, and objective technical oversight.

Future Timelines and Economic Expectations

The newly approved framework outlines an active transition period heading into the next decade. With legislative drafting for the Automotive Development Levy scheduled before the close of 2026, car makers must adjust their operational planning, local parts sourcing, and supply chains to meet both the export quotas and safety benchmarks that take effect across the 2026 to 2031 window.

The program includes targets for specialized technical training to help local workers acquire the engineering capabilities required for electric drivetrains and automated components. By combining human resource development with technology acquisition funds, economic planners hope to transition the domestic auto sector into a competitive manufacturing hub capable of serving both local drivers and foreign buyers.

Market observers will watch how quickly vehicle assemblers invest in export channels and how commercial financial institutions implement the expanded Rs10 million loan ceiling for electric cars. The success of the policy will depend on the smooth passage of the necessary December 2026 legislation, consistent oversight through biannual audits, and the capacity of local component vendors to meet the ambitious 1.2 billion dollar export milestone by 2031.
