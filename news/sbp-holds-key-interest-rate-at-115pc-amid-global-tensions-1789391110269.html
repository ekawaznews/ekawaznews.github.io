SBP Holds Key Interest Rate at 11.5pc Amid Global Tensions

U
Umer Javed
Staff Reporter
| 🕒 14 September 2026 at 06:05 pm | 📖 6 min read
Share: 💬 WhatsApp 📘 Facebook ✈️ Telegram 🐦 Twitter
📷 Photo: RDNE Stock project / Pexels | ekawaznews.github.io

The State Bank of Pakistan’s (SBP) Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) on Monday announced its decision to keep the key policy rate unchanged at 11.5 per cent, a move that reflects the central bank’s ongoing assessment of both domestic economic conditions and significant international developments.

This decision, reached by a majority vote of seven out of ten members of the MPC, comes at a time when the global economic is being significantly shaped by geopolitical factors. Prolonged tensions in the Middle East have been identified as a primary driver, leading to an upward trajectory in global oil prices and contributing to persistent inflationary pressures across economies, including Pakistan.

The MPC, in its detailed statement, specifically highlighted the recent escalation in the Middle East conflict. This intensification, according to the committee, has directly contributed to an increase in global commodity prices and has exacerbated existing disruptions in international supply chains. These external factors exert considerable influence on Pakistan's import-dependent economy, translating into higher costs for consumers and businesses.

Understanding the central bank's policy rate is essential for comprehending the broader economic implications of this decision. The key policy rate, often referred to as the benchmark interest rate, is the rate at which commercial banks borrow money from the SBP. It serves as a fundamental tool for monetary policy, influencing borrowing costs across the entire economy, from consumer loans and mortgages to business investments and government borrowing.

When the SBP raises the policy rate, it typically aims to curb inflation by making borrowing more expensive, thereby reducing demand and slowing economic activity. Conversely, a reduction in the rate is often intended to stimulate economic growth by encouraging borrowing and investment. Keeping the rate unchanged, as in this instance, signals a careful balancing act, suggesting that the MPC believes the current monetary stance is appropriate to manage existing inflationary pressures without unduly stifling economic momentum.

Global Shocks and Domestic Economy

Pakistan, as a net importer of oil, is particularly vulnerable to fluctuations in international crude prices. An increase in global oil prices directly impacts the country's import bill, placing additional strain on its foreign exchange reserves and exacerbating current account deficits. Domestically, higher oil prices translate into increased costs for transportation, power generation, and industrial production, which inevitably feed into higher prices for goods and services across the board.

This phenomenon, known as imported inflation, presents a complex challenge for the SBP. While domestic monetary policy can address demand-side inflation, its effectiveness in countering price increases driven by external supply shocks, such as those originating from geopolitical conflicts, is more limited. The central bank must therefore weigh the potential benefits of further tightening monetary policy against the risk of slowing down an economy already grappling with various structural challenges.

The MPC's statement highlights this dilemma, explicitly linking the unchanged policy rate to the global oil price surge and supply chain disruptions stemming from the Middle East conflict. This indicates a recognition that a significant portion of current inflationary pressures is exogenous, originating beyond Pakistan's borders and its direct monetary control.

The Role of the Monetary Policy Committee

The Monetary Policy Committee plays a critical role in shaping Pakistan's economic trajectory. Comprising a diverse group of economic experts, including the SBP Governor, Deputy Governors, and external members, the MPC is tasked with making informed decisions on interest rates and other monetary policy tools. Its primary objective is to maintain price stability, which means keeping inflation at a manageable level, while also supporting sustainable economic growth.

The committee's decision-making process involves a thorough analysis of a wide array of economic indicators. This includes inflation trends, fiscal developments, external sector performance, global economic outlook, and domestic growth prospects. The majority decision of seven out of ten members to keep the policy rate unchanged suggests that a consensus was reached that the existing rate provides the necessary balance given the prevailing circumstances.

While the initial announcement focused on the external factors, the MPC statement also referenced recent domestic macroeconomic data, though the broader implications of this data were not fully detailed in the initial announcement. This indicates that the committee's decision was not solely based on international developments but also incorporated an assessment of Pakistan's internal economic health, including factors such as industrial output, employment figures, and consumer spending patterns.

Inflationary in Pakistan

Inflation has been a persistent concern for Pakistan's economy over recent periods, impacting the purchasing power of citizens and creating uncertainty for businesses. While the SBP has employed various measures to address this challenge, the influx of imported inflation due to rising global commodity prices adds another layer of complexity. The cost of living for the average Pakistani household is directly affected by the prices of essential goods, many of which are influenced by international markets.

The SBP's approach appears to be one of cautious observation, allowing the existing policy rate to work through the economy while monitoring the evolution of both global and domestic conditions. An unchanged rate can provide a degree of stability and predictability for financial markets and businesses, allowing them to plan with greater certainty in an otherwise volatile environment. It suggests the SBP is giving previous rate hikes time to fully transmit through the economy.

The committee's assessment likely considered that raising the policy rate further might not significantly mitigate inflation driven by external supply shocks, but could instead dampen domestic economic activity unnecessarily. This strategic patience allows the central bank to preserve its policy space, reserving potential future actions for when they might be more effective in addressing specific domestic inflationary pressures.

Future Outlook and Economic Challenges

Looking ahead, Pakistan's economy continues to face a complex set of challenges. The persistent geopolitical instability in the Middle East means that global commodity prices, particularly oil, could remain elevated or even increase further. This will continue to pose a significant risk to Pakistan's external accounts and domestic inflation trajectory.

The SBP, through its Monetary Policy Committee, will need to remain highly vigilant, continuously monitoring these external shocks alongside domestic economic indicators. The effectiveness of monetary policy in achieving price stability is intertwined with fiscal policy and structural reforms. A coordinated approach involving prudent fiscal management and efforts to enhance productivity and export competitiveness will be essential to build resilience against external vulnerabilities.

The decision to hold the policy rate at 11.5 per cent reflects a careful and measured approach by the State Bank of Pakistan. It acknowledges the significant impact of global events on the domestic economy, particularly the inflationary pressures stemming from international oil and commodity prices. While maintaining a steady course, the SBP remains committed to its mandate of ensuring price stability and supporting sustainable economic growth for the nation.
