Pakistan and Lebanon Agree to Boost Security Cooperation in Key Areas

By Umer Javed | 16 September 2026 | 5 min read
Photo: Nemika F / Pexels

Federal Interior Minister Mohsin Naqvi and Lebanese counterpart Ahmed Al-Hajjar agreed to enhance cooperation in counterterrorism, anti-narcotics, cybercrime, and illegal immigration during talks in Islamabad.

Federal Interior Minister Mohsin Naqvi and his Lebanese counterpart, Ahmed Al-Hajjar, agreed on Tuesday to enhance cooperation in security, counterterrorism, anti-narcotics efforts, illegal immigration, money laundering, and cybercrime prevention. The two ministers held a one-on-one meeting at the Ministry of Interior in Islamabad, followed by delegation-level talks aimed at strengthening bilateral ties between Pakistan and Lebanon. The discussions were described as constructive and forward-looking by officials close to the negotiations.

The meeting comes at a time when regional security concerns are intensifying across the Middle East and South Asia. Both Pakistan and Lebanon face distinct but overlapping challenges, including terrorism, drug trafficking, and organized crime. The decision to deepen cooperation reflects a shared recognition among both governments that these threats cannot be addressed in isolation. Coordinated action between the two countries signals a serious commitment to tackling these issues at their roots.

Key Areas of Cooperation

According to reports from the Interior Ministry, the two sides held detailed discussions on a wide range of security-related issues during the meeting. Counterterrorism cooperation stood out as a central focus, with both ministers acknowledging the need for better intelligence sharing and coordinated operations against terrorist networks operating across borders. The fight against narcotics trafficking also featured prominently in the agenda, given the growing cross-border movement of illicit drugs through the region.

Illegal immigration and human smuggling were identified as areas where both countries could benefit from closer collaboration. The two ministers directed their officials to develop frameworks that would facilitate information exchange and joint action on irregular migration. Money laundering and cybercrime prevention were also discussed at length, reflecting the increasing global threat posed by financial crimes and digital attacks. The two sides agreed to explore practical mechanisms for cooperation in each of these areas, with follow-up meetings to be arranged to monitor progress.

Official Statements

Minister Naqvi welcomed Minister Al-Hajjar to Islamabad and expressed Pakistan's readiness to work closely with Lebanon on shared security objectives. He stated that Pakistan remains committed to supporting international and regional efforts to combat terrorism and transnational crime. The Interior Minister confirmed that both sides have identified concrete steps to move forward following Tuesday's talks. He told reporters that the discussions were frank, focused, and productive, and that both delegations were aligned on the need for swift implementation of the agreements reached.

Minister Al-Hajjar, in return, expressed appreciation for Pakistan's engagement on security matters and its willingness to cooperate with Lebanon. He stated that Lebanon highly values its relationship with Pakistan and sees significant potential for collaboration across the areas discussed. The Lebanese minister confirmed that his delegation is prepared to begin working on the agreements reached during the meeting. He added that the security challenges facing the two countries share common characteristics, which makes bilateral cooperation both necessary and effective.

Sovereignty and Regional Support

In a separate development, Dar reaffirmed Pakistan's support for Lebanon's sovereignty and territorial integrity. This statement came amid ongoing regional tensions and instability across the Middle East. Pakistan's position on Lebanon's sovereignty aligns with its broader foreign policy principles, which emphasize respect for national boundaries and non-interference in the internal affairs of other states. The reaffirmation was welcomed by Lebanese officials and is seen as a continuation of Pakistan's consistent stance on the issue.

The reaffirmation of support carries significance given the complex political and economic situation in Lebanon. The country has faced prolonged economic hardship, political dysfunction, and security challenges in recent years. Pakistan's continued backing of Lebanese sovereignty signals its commitment to a stable and peaceful region. It also reinforces the message that Pakistan considers the principles of national sovereignty and territorial integrity to be fundamental to international order and security.

Regional Discussions and UNGA Preparations

The Deputy Prime Minister held discussions with Grossi on the current regional situation and the Islamabad Memorandum of Understanding. According to reports, the two sides reviewed ongoing regional dynamics and explored ways to address shared concerns through diplomatic and cooperative channels. The Islamabad MoU is expected to serve as a framework for continued engagement on regional security matters and bilateral cooperation. Officials confirmed that the discussions were substantive and covered a broad range of topics related to peace and stability in the region.

The programme for the upcoming United Nations General Assembly session was also reviewed by officials. Preparations are underway for Pakistan's participation in the annual gathering of world leaders in New York. The UNGA session will provide a platform for Pakistan to raise important regional and global issues before the international community. According to reports, the programme includes a series of bilateral meetings, panel discussions, and diplomatic engagements that Pakistan plans to use for advancing its foreign policy objectives.

Significance of the Partnership

The security partnership between Pakistan and Lebanon adds to Pakistan's growing network of bilateral cooperation agreements in the security domain. Pakistan has been increasingly active in international counterterrorism efforts and has engaged with multiple countries on intelligence sharing and coordinated action against terrorist groups. The agreement with Lebanon is part of this broader effort to build strong partnerships that enhance collective security.

The cooperation framework agreed upon on Tuesday could serve as a model for future partnerships with other nations facing similar security challenges. Both countries have a vested interest in maintaining regional stability and preventing the spread of terrorism, drug trafficking, and organized crime. The practical nature of the agreements, covering areas from cybercrime to illegal immigration, suggests that the partnership is built on real-world needs rather than symbolic gestures.

The outcomes of Tuesday's meeting will be closely watched by regional observers and international partners. If implemented effectively, the agreements reached between the two ministers could contribute to improved security conditions in both Pakistan and Lebanon. The international community will be paying attention to how these commitments translate into concrete action on the ground. Both countries have an opportunity to demonstrate that bilateral cooperation remains a powerful tool for addressing the security challenges of our time.
