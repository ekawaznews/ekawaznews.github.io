Northern Regions and the Federal Capital

Weather across the northern belt of Pakistan is projected to remain largely dry and mild over the next four days, with the notable exception of Gilgit where unsettled weather will prevail through midweek. In Islamabad, daytime temperatures will hover near twenty-eight degrees Celsius before rising slightly to twenty-nine degrees on twenty-two October. Nighttime temperatures in the federal capital will hold steady at seventeen degrees Celsius throughout the period. Skies above the capital city are forecast to stay partly cloudy on nineteen and twenty October, clearing out to full sunshine on twenty-one and twenty-two October with zero percent chance of rainfall across all four days.

Peshawar will experience very similar daytime warmth, recording a consistent maximum temperature of twenty-eight degrees Celsius from nineteen to twenty-two October. Minimum temperatures in the Khyber Pakhtunkhwa capital will ease down slowly from seventeen degrees on nineteen October to sixteen degrees on twenty and twenty-one October, dipping to fifteen degrees on twenty-two October. Skies will be clear initially, turn partly cloudy on twenty October, and return to clear conditions for the remaining two days without any measurable precipitation expected.

In contrast to the dry plains, Gilgit will see unsettled conditions driven by cloud cover and precipitation. The forecast indicates drizzle on nineteen October with a high of twenty degrees, a low of ten degrees, and a forty-one percent chance of rain. On twenty October, Gilgit will see another round of drizzle as the low drops to nine degrees and the precipitation chance rises to forty-nine percent while the maximum remains twenty degrees. Conditions remain damp on twenty-one October with drizzle, a high of twenty degrees, a low of ten degrees, and a thirty-four percent rain probability. Clear skies will finally arrive on twenty-two October, allowing the temperature to peak at twenty-one degrees with an overnight low of nine degrees and a zero percent chance of rain.

Forecast for the Central Plains and Balochistan Plateau

Warm and settled conditions are expected to prevail across Punjab and Balochistan over the coming days. Lahore is set to experience mild autumn afternoons and comfortable nights. On nineteen October, the city will see clear skies, a high of twenty-nine degrees Celsius, and an overnight low of twenty degrees. The daytime peak will nudge up to thirty degrees Celsius on twenty October beneath partly cloudy skies, with the minimum remaining at twenty degrees. On twenty-one and twenty-two October, Lahore will continue to see highs of thirty degrees and lows of twenty degrees under clear skies, with the probability of rain remaining at zero percent throughout the forecast window.

Further south in Multan, warmer conditions will dominate the plains of southern Punjab. The city will record a high of thirty-three degrees Celsius on nineteen October, with the overnight minimum resting at twenty-one degrees. From twenty October through twenty-one October, daytime highs will touch thirty-four degrees Celsius with nighttime readings staying at twenty-one degrees under partly cloudy skies. By twenty-two October, temperatures are forecast to reach thirty-five degrees Celsius while the low holds at twenty-one degrees. Partly cloudy skies will continue on that day, accompanied by a slight two percent chance of rain.

Quetta will experience distinct highland weather marked by crisp afternoons and noticeably chilly nights. On nineteen October, the provincial capital of Balochistan will reach a high of twenty-five degrees Celsius with a low of eleven degrees under partly cloudy skies. Skies will clear on twenty October while the temperature range remains identical at twenty-five degrees high and eleven degrees low. Conditions will cool slightly on twenty-one and twenty-two October, with daytime maximums reaching twenty-four degrees and night minimums settling at twelve degrees under consistently clear skies with zero chance of rain.

Warm Skies and Late Fog in Karachi

Karachi will see persistent daytime heat alongside humid conditions over the next four days. On nineteen October, the port city will experience clear skies, an afternoon high of thirty-five degrees Celsius, and a nighttime low of twenty-five degrees Celsius. A slight uptick in heat is expected on twenty October, when the maximum temperature will reach thirty-six degrees Celsius while the overnight reading drops to twenty-four degrees under clear skies.

Clear conditions will persist into twenty-one October as temperatures peak at thirty-five degrees Celsius with an overnight low of twenty-five degrees. However, a change in atmospheric visibility is anticipated on twenty-two October. While daytime temperatures will again reach thirty-five degrees and nighttime readings will settle at twenty-four degrees, fog is forecast to develop over the metropolitan area. The chance of precipitation will remain at zero percent throughout all four days, maintaining dry ground conditions across the southern coastal belt.

Coastal Vulnerability and Environmental Changes

Alongside the immediate weather forecast, long-term environmental developments along the Arabian Sea coast have drawn renewed attention. According to Dawn, new satellite images from the years 2000 and 2025 released by the national space agency show a fundamental shift in land cover along the coastal zone of Karachi near the Mai Kolachi Bypass. The imagery documents how natural tidal creeks and mangrove stands have increasingly given way to reclaimed land and urban development over the past quarter century.

In a note on the development reported by Dawn, the Space and Upper Atmosphere Research Commission stated that areas once characterised by continuous mangrove cover and natural tidal creeks had progressively been converted into reclaimed land and urban plots. According to the agency, “This transformation reflects a transition from ecosystem-based coastal protection to hard, land-intensive development. Mangrove removal directly weakens the coastal system’s ability to respond to climate stress.”

As Dawn reported, the progressive loss of these natural buffers raises flood and erosion risks along the city coastline. Mangroves serve as a frontline shield against incoming storm surges and heavy sea action. When natural creek systems are filled in for construction, the capacity of the shoreline to absorb tidal surges diminishes, leaving coastal infrastructure more exposed to extreme weather events over time.

Implications for Agriculture and Field Work

The prevailing weather across the agricultural heartland of Punjab and Khyber Pakhtunkhwa will have direct consequences for field operations. The lack of rain in Lahore, Multan, and Peshawar provides stable conditions for ongoing harvesting routines and early land preparation for winter sowing. Farmers in southern Punjab can proceed with outdoor work under partly cloudy skies, though high afternoon temperatures reaching thirty-five degrees in Multan will require adequate hydration management for laborers and livestock.

In northern upland valleys such as Gilgit, intermittent drizzle and cloudiness across three consecutive days will produce damp surface conditions. Wet fields could temporarily slow down late-season orchard chores or crop collection until the weather clears on twenty-two October. In contrast, the plateau around Quetta offers ideal dry circumstances for agricultural transit, though the drop in night temperatures to eleven and twelve degrees indicates a steady shift toward late-autumn thermal conditions that growers must account for during irrigation scheduling.

Health Considerations and Travel Conditions Across the Country

Stable conditions across most major road networks will support uninterrupted transport during the coming days. In northern areas, drivers traveling around Gilgit may encounter wet road surfaces and reduced visibility during periods of drizzle between nineteen and twenty-one October. Regular vigilance is advisable for travelers using mountain routes until clear skies return on twenty-two October. On the national highways connecting Lahore, Islamabad, and Peshawar, clear to partly cloudy conditions with dry tarmac will make for predictable journeys.

Commuters and transport operators in Karachi should prepare for changes in early morning visibility toward the end of the four-day forecast period. The emergence of fog on twenty-two October could cause localized travel delays on coastal highways and major urban thoroughfares.
