Current Weather Overview for Pakistan

As the nation moves through the middle of November, the climate across the country remains largely stable and dry. According to data provided by Open-Meteo, for the period spanning November 17 to November 20, 2026, there is no rain expected in any major city. This pattern of clear or partly cloudy skies is likely to persist, offering consistent weather conditions for all provinces. While temperatures vary significantly between the northern mountainous regions and the southern coastal areas, the general trend is one of predictability.

For the residents of Karachi, the coming days will be warm. The city can expect daily highs hovering between 31 and 32 degrees Celsius, with nightly lows ranging from 20 to 22 degrees. The weather will remain mostly clear, save for a brief period of partial cloudiness on November 18. This sustained warmth is typical for the region at this time of year, allowing for normal outdoor activities without the interference of precipitation. Similarly, in Multan, the weather will remain warm and dry, with daily highs near 30 degrees Celsius and stable nighttime lows, ensuring that the southern belt remains free from climate-related disruptions.

Conditions in Punjab and the Capital

Lahore and Islamabad are entering a period of pleasant autumn weather. Lahore will see daytime temperatures reaching between 25 and 26 degrees Celsius, with nightly temperatures dipping to between 13 and 14 degrees. The city will experience clear skies for most of the duration, with only a touch of cloudiness expected by November 20. This is ideal for those planning travel or outdoor work, as the lack of rain ensures that road conditions will remain dry and manageable for commuters and commercial transport alike.

In Islamabad, the climate will be slightly cooler, with highs ranging from 22 to 23 degrees Celsius and lows between 12 and 13 degrees. The capital will experience a mix of clear skies and partial cloudiness as the week progresses. This mild weather is beneficial for the general public, as it avoids the extremes that often disrupt daily life. Residents in these areas can expect comfortable conditions for the next four days, provided they prepare for the cooler evenings that characterize the season. The absence of humidity and rainfall makes this a time of year where the transition between seasons is felt most clearly in the northern plains.

Northern and Western Regional Forecasts

Peshawar and Quetta are experiencing the cooling trend associated with their higher elevations. Peshawar will maintain highs around 22 to 23 degrees Celsius, while nighttime temperatures will stay low at 10 to 11 degrees. The sky will be partly cloudy for most of the period, with a clear day on November 18. This suggests that while the days are mild, the nights will require adequate warmth for those residing in the area. The consistent, dry air in the region is a hallmark of the current high-pressure system dominating the northern parts of the country.

Quetta faces the coldest conditions in the country during this period. Temperatures will start with a low of 5 degrees Celsius on November 17, gradually rising to 9 degrees by November 20. Daily highs will fluctuate between 17 and 21 degrees. The weather will transition from clear to partly cloudy throughout the four days. In the northern reaches, Gilgit will also see a steady climb in temperatures from 15 degrees to 22 degrees, though nighttime lows will remain chilly, starting at 4 degrees. These conditions are standard for the region, and inhabitants should remain mindful of the significant drop in temperature once the sun sets.

Agricultural and Daily Life Implications

The absence of rainfall across the country is a significant factor for the agricultural sector. Farmers who rely on predictable weather patterns for harvesting or planting will find these dry conditions advantageous, as there is no risk of water damage to crops from unexpected storms. The consistent temperatures also mean that there are no immediate climate-related threats to livestock or outdoor farming operations in the coming days. This stability allows for the uninterrupted movement of goods and agricultural products to markets across the provinces.

For the general population, the lack of precipitation means that travel by road is unlikely to be hampered by weather-related issues. Commuters and those traveling between cities can expect standard conditions. Health-wise, the transition from warm days to cool nights, particularly in the northern regions, may contribute to common seasonal illnesses. It is advisable for residents to dress in layers to accommodate these daily temperature swings, ensuring they remain protected as the evening chill sets in. Maintaining this awareness is a simple way for citizens to manage the health impacts of the current seasonal shift.

Updates from the Sports Field

In other news, sporting events are also being influenced by the climate. According to Dawn, the second and final Test match between Pakistan and Sri Lanka is scheduled to begin in Rawalpindi. The teams are looking for a definitive result following the first match, which was impacted by bad weather. Pakistan bowling coach Shaun Tait and Sri Lankan captain Kusal Mendis have both expressed a desire for a competitive outcome. The first Test concluded without a winner due to both weather interruptions and the nature of the pitch, which frustrated both sides.

Kusal Mendis noted that his team aims to apply pressure on the hosts. During the previous match, Sri Lanka held a strong position but could not capitalize on it, leading to a draw. The players are now focused on performing well on the pitch in Rawalpindi. With no rain forecasted for the region, there is hope that this match will proceed without the environmental disruptions that affected the earlier encounter, allowing the skills of the players to determine the outcome. Analysts may observe that the drier conditions in Rawalpindi could change the way the pitch behaves, potentially favoring a more decisive result compared to the previous outing.

Political Developments and Public Discourse

The political atmosphere is also seeing some shifts, as reported by Dawn. There is growing concern regarding the government's approach to handling dissent within the country. PPP Chairman Bilawal Bhutto-Zardari recently held a conversation with JUI-F leader Maulana Fazlur Rehman to discuss the political environment. During this call, the PPP chairman expressed strong disapproval of what he described as actions and political vengeance directed at the JUI-F. This interaction indicates a deepening rift within the coalition, as the PPP seeks to establish a distinct identity.

The discussion highlighted a desire by the PPP to distance itself from the policies of the ruling PML-N. Bilawal Bhutto-Zardari urged his coalition partners to adopt a more conciliatory approach toward the opposition, emphasizing that the opposition plays an extremely important role in democracy and the parliamentary system. He warned that the government should refrain from such actions that weaken democracy and parliament. Analysts may observe that these tensions within the coalition could have long-term consequences for the stability of the government, as the PPP seeks to clarify its stance on governance and the treatment of political rivals. Observers will be watching to see if the government adjusts its strategy in response to these internal pressures.
