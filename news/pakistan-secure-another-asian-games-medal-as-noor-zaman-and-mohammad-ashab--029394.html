Pakistan secure another Asian Games medal as Noor Zaman and Mohammad Ashab Irfan reach squash semi-finals

By Umer Javed | 26 September 2026 | 4 min read
Photo: Anthony Yates / Pexels

Pakistan guaranteed a squash medal at the Asian Games after Noor Zaman and Mohammad Ashab Irfan won their quarter-final matches on Friday. Both players advanced to the semi-finals at the Nagoya Kinjo Futo Arena.

Pakistan secured another medal at the Asian Games on Friday after Noor Zaman and Mohammad Ashab Irfan won their respective singles matches to reach the semi-finals of the squash competition.

Noor Zaman holds nerve against Hong Kong opponent

Third-seeded Noor Zaman, Pakistan's top-ranked player in the draw, faced a stern test from Hong Kong's Chi Hin Henry Leung at the Nagoya Kinjo Futo Arena. The match proved tighter than the seeding suggested, with Leung pushing Noor deep into the contest. However, the Pakistani player held his nerve during the important stages to secure a place in the last four. According to reports from the venue, Noor's experience in high-pressure situations proved decisive as he managed the closing stages of the match with composure.

Ashab Irfan produces strong performance against Indian rival

Fifth-seeded Mohammad Ashab Irfan delivered an impressive display to overcome India's Veer Chotrani, the fourth seed in the competition. The all-South Asian encounter carried additional significance given the sporting rivalry between the two nations. Ashab produced a strong performance against his higher-seeded opponent, recovering from difficult positions during the match to claim victory. The win not only secured a medal for Pakistan but also gave Ashab a psychological edge should the two players meet again in future competitions.

Pakistan's squash legacy at the Asian Games

Squash has historically been one of Pakistan's strongest disciplines at the Asian Games. The country has a rich tradition in the sport, dating back to the era of Jahangir Khan and Jansher Khan, who dominated world squash for decades. At the Asian Games, Pakistan has consistently featured on the podium in both individual and team events. The current generation, led by players like Noor Zaman and Mohammad Ashab Irfan, is attempting to revive that golden era. Their progress to the semi-finals continues a proud tradition and provides hope for a new chapter in Pakistan's squash history.

Path to the semi-finals

Both Pakistani players navigated the early rounds with authority before facing their quarter-final tests. Noor Zaman, entering as the third seed, had received a favourable draw in the initial stages but the quarter-final represented his first serious examination. Mohammad Ashab Irfan, seeded fifth, had similarly progressed steadily before the challenge of facing the fourth-seeded Indian player. The semi-finals will present a significant step up in quality, with the remaining four players all capable of winning gold. According to tournament schedules, the semi-final matches are expected to take place on Saturday, with the final scheduled for Sunday.

Venue and competition format

The squash competition is being held at the Nagoya Kinjo Futo Arena, a purpose-built facility that has hosted international tournaments previously. The venue features glass-backed courts that allow for spectator viewing and television broadcast. The competition follows a standard knockout format, with the top seeds receiving byes in the opening rounds. All matches are best-of-five games, ensuring that the better player typically progresses over the longer format. The semi-finals will follow the same format, with the winners advancing to the gold medal match and the losers contesting the bronze.

Medal implications for Pakistan contingent

This guaranteed squash medal adds to Pakistan's overall tally at the Asian Games. The country's delegation has been targeting medals across multiple disciplines, with squash traditionally viewed as a strong prospect. The confirmation of at least one medal, with the possibility of two if both players win their semi-finals, represents a significant boost for the contingent. Team officials have expressed satisfaction with the squash team's performance so far. The Pakistan Squash Federation has invested in developing these players through international exposure and training camps, and the results at Nagoya suggest those investments are bearing fruit.

Looking ahead to the semi-finals

Both Noor Zaman and Mohammad Ashab Irfan will need to recover quickly for their semi-final matches. The physical demands of squash at this level are immense, with matches often lasting over an hour. Recovery protocols, including physiotherapy, nutrition, and rest, will be critical over the next 24 hours. The coaching staff will also analyse potential opponents and devise game plans tailored to each player's strengths. Noor Zaman's experience in big matches could be an asset, while Ashab Irfan's confidence after defeating a higher-seeded opponent will serve him well. The entire Pakistan contingent will be watching closely as both players aim to reach the final and compete for gold.
