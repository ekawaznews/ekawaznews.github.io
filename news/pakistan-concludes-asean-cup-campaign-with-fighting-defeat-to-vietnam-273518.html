Pakistan Concludes ASEAN Cup Campaign with Fighting Defeat to Vietnam

By Umer Javed | 2 October 2026 | 6 min read
Photo: Đạt Hà / Pexels

Pakistan ended their FIFA ASEAN Cup journey with a 4-2 defeat against Vietnam in Jakarta on Friday. Despite a strong start and a goal from Mohibullah, Nolberto Solano’s side could not overcome their higher-ranked opponents.

Pakistan concluded their FIFA ASEAN Cup campaign with a fighting 4-2 defeat against Vietnam at the Gelora Bung Karno Stadium in Jakarta on Friday. The match saw Nolberto Solano’s side, ranked 99 places below Vietnam in the FIFA rankings, demonstrate moments of promise but ultimately succumb to a second-half resurgence from their opponents.

The encounter served as the final fixture for Pakistan in the tournament, offering valuable experience against a well-established regional football power. Despite the numerical disadvantage in rankings, the Pakistani squad displayed commendable spirit and tactical discipline for large portions of the game, particularly in the initial phases.

Early Promise and Mohibullah's Strike

The match began with Pakistan showing an unexpected assertiveness, pushing forward and creating opportunities. Their efforts were rewarded in the first half when Mohibullah scored a superb goal, giving Pakistan a significant and morale-boosting lead. This opening strike sent a clear message of intent and briefly put the higher-ranked Vietnamese team on the back foot.

Mohibullah's goal highlighted the potential within the Pakistani squad, particularly among its younger players, to compete at an international level. The lead, though temporary, highlighted the team's ability to execute their game plan and capitalize on scoring chances against formidable opposition. The initial period of the game was marked by disciplined defending and quick transitions from the Pakistani side.

The atmosphere in Jakarta, a major footballing hub in the region, provided an intense backdrop for the contest. Playing in such an environment against a strong opponent like Vietnam offers invaluable exposure for a developing football nation like Pakistan, allowing players to adapt to high-pressure situations and the demands of international competition.

Vietnam's Second-Half Onslaught

As the second half commenced, Vietnam intensified their offensive efforts, displaying the quality and depth expected of a team ranked significantly higher. The pressure eventually paid off as they managed to break through Pakistan’s defense. Long Hai Nguyen was the first to find the back of the net for Vietnam, leveling the score and shifting the momentum of the game.

Following Long Hai Nguyen’s equalizer, Vietnam continued to press their advantage. Duc Hoang Nguyen then scored to put Vietnam ahead, giving them a 2-1 lead. This was quickly followed by another goal from Bac Dinh Nguyen, extending Vietnam’s lead to 3-1. The rapid succession of goals in the second half demonstrated Vietnam’s attacking prowess and their capacity to turn a game around.

The Vietnamese offensive surge tested Pakistan’s defensive resilience and stamina. While the Pakistani side had managed to contain their opponents effectively in the first half, the relentless attacks in the second period proved difficult to withstand. The tactical adjustments made by Vietnam’s coaching staff also played a role in their ability to unlock Pakistan’s defense.

Late Comeback Attempt and Final Goal

Despite being 3-1 down, Pakistan refused to concede defeat easily. In the 88th minute, veteran striker Kaleemullah Khan pulled one back for his side, reducing the deficit to 3-2. This late goal injected a renewed sense of urgency and hope into the Pakistani camp, suggesting a possible dramatic finish to the match.

Kaleemullah Khan’s goal was the perseverance of the Pakistani team and the individual quality of its experienced players. His ability to find the net in a high-stakes moment provided a glimpse of the fighting spirit that characterizes Pakistani athletes on the international stage. The late goal created a tense final few minutes, with Pakistan pushing for an unlikely equalizer.

However, Vietnam quickly extinguished any hopes of a Pakistani comeback. Hoang Minh Williams restored Vietnam’s two-goal advantage shortly after Kaleemullah’s strike, making the score 4-2. This final goal secured Vietnam’s victory and concluded a dynamic match that saw both teams demonstrate periods of dominance and resilience.

Context of Pakistan's Football Journey

The match against Vietnam serves as a significant benchmark for Pakistan’s national football team. Playing against a team ranked 99 places higher in the FIFA standings, as mentioned, highlights the considerable gap in development and resources that Pakistan faces in comparison to more established footballing nations in Asia. However, such encounters are essential for the growth and development of the sport within the country.

Under the guidance of coach Nolberto Solano, the Pakistani squad is on a journey of rebuilding and improvement. Solano, with his extensive experience in international football, is working to instill a professional ethos and a more structured approach to the game. The ASEAN Cup campaign, regardless of the results, provides a important platform for the players to gain international exposure and apply their training in competitive scenarios.

For a nation like Pakistan, where football competes with other popular sports, consistent participation in international tournaments is vital. It offers players opportunities to hone their skills, understand different tactical approaches, and build camaraderie as a cohesive unit. The lessons learned from matches against stronger opponents, even in defeat, contribute significantly to the team’s overall progress.

The Road Ahead for Pakistani Football

The performance against Vietnam, characterized by an early lead and a late fightback, reflects the potential that exists within Pakistani football, alongside the challenges that need to be addressed. The ability to score against a strong opponent like Vietnam, and the determination shown until the final whistle, are positive indicators for the future.

Developing a competitive national team requires sustained investment in grassroots football, youth academies, and professional league structures. The experience gained by players such as Mohibullah and Kaleemullah in high-pressure international fixtures is invaluable for their individual growth and for the collective improvement of the squad. These matches highlight areas where the team has made progress and areas that require further attention and development.

The FIFA ASEAN Cup campaign, now concluded for Pakistan, represents another step in the national team’s ongoing journey to elevate its standing in Asian football. The focus will now shift to analyzing the performances, identifying key takeaways, and continuing the training and development programs initiated by Coach Solano. The objective remains to build a stronger, more consistent team capable of competing effectively on the regional and international stages.

The dedication of the players and the coaching staff remains undiminished. While the defeat against Vietnam marks the end of this particular campaign, it also signals the continuation of efforts to improve and achieve greater success in future international assignments. The experience gathered from playing against higher-ranked teams will undoubtedly contribute to the long-term strategic goals for Pakistani football.
