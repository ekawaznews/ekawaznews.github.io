Supreme Court Suspends PMDC Orders to Return Afghan Medical Students

By Umer Javed | 15 September 2026 | 4 min read
Photo: Elena / Pexels

Pakistan’s Supreme Court has suspended the Pakistan Medical and Dental Council’s orders to send Afghan medical students back to their homeland. The move follows a legal review of the council’s directive.

The Supreme Court of Pakistan has suspended the orders issued by the Pakistan Medical and Dental Council (PMDC) that required Afghan medical students to return to Afghanistan. The stay order was issued after a legal challenge was filed by the students and their representatives.

Background of Afghan Students in Pakistan

Since the early 2000s, thousands of Afghan nationals have sought medical education in Pakistan. Many enrolled in public and private universities, often with scholarships or financial aid from the Afghan government or international donors. The influx of Afghan students has been a subject of debate, with concerns about resource allocation and security. Despite these concerns, the students have contributed to the academic community and the health sector by engaging in research and clinical practice.

PMDC’s Return Orders

Earlier this year, the Pakistan Medical and Dental Council announced that Afghan medical students would be required to return to Afghanistan upon completion of their studies. The council cited a lack of accreditation agreements and regulatory oversight as reasons for the directive. The orders were communicated through official notices to universities and the students themselves. The notice also mentioned that the students would not be permitted to register for the licensing examination in Pakistan.

Supreme Court Decision

According to reports, the Supreme Court issued a stay on the PMDC orders after reviewing the legal merits of the case. The court’s decision was based on the principle that the council’s directive could potentially violate the rights of students who had invested years in their education. The stay allows the students to continue their studies and participate in the licensing examinations while the legal dispute proceeds.

Legal Context

The PMDC operates under the Pakistan Medical and Dental Council Act, which grants it authority over medical education and licensing. However, the council’s powers are subject to judicial oversight, especially when orders affect the fundamental rights of individuals. The Supreme Court’s intervention highlights the judiciary’s role in balancing regulatory authority with individual liberties. The court also emphasized that any directive must be consistent with the Constitution and international agreements on the status of refugees.

Impact on Afghan Students

The stay order provides relief to Afghan students who were facing uncertainty about their future. Many had already completed clinical rotations and were preparing for the licensing examination. The decision allows them to remain in Pakistan until the legal process concludes. It also preserves the financial investments made by the students and their families. The students expressed relief in informal statements to local media, though they remain cautious about the long‑term outcome of the case.

Reactions from Stakeholders

Universities across the country welcomed the Supreme Court’s decision. According to reports, several faculty members noted that the students had been valuable contributors to campus life and research projects. The PMDC, however, maintained that its orders were issued in the interest of maintaining standards in medical education. No official statement from the council was released following the court’s stay order. The Afghan Embassy in Islamabad has called for a diplomatic dialogue to address the concerns raised by the council.

Future Outlook

The Supreme Court has scheduled additional hearings to examine the legality of the PMDC’s directive. The court will consider arguments from both the council and the students. Depending on the outcome, the students may be allowed to take the licensing exam in Pakistan or may need to return to Afghanistan to complete their qualifications. The decision will also set a precedent for how professional councils handle foreign students in the future.

Meanwhile, the Afghan Ministry of Health has indicated its willingness to cooperate with Pakistani authorities to ensure that Afghan medical graduates receive proper accreditation. The Ministry has requested that the council provide clear guidelines for the registration of Afghan doctors in Pakistan. The government of Pakistan has reiterated its commitment to upholding the rights of students while maintaining regulatory standards. The case is likely to remain in the public eye for several months as the court reviews the evidence and arguments presented by both sides.

, the Supreme Court’s suspension of the PMDC orders marks a significant development in the ongoing debate over foreign medical students in Pakistan. The decision highlights the need for clear regulatory frameworks and safeguards that protect the rights of students while ensuring the integrity of medical education. The outcome will influence not only Afghan students but also the broader policy on foreign medical education in the country.
