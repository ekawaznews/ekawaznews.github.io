Federal Government Imposes Emergency and Governor Rule in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa

U
Umer Javed
International Correspondent
| 🕒 11 October 2026 at 03:19 pm | 📖 7 min read
Share: 💬 WhatsApp 📘 Facebook ✈️ Telegram 🐦 Twitter

Federal Cabinet Action and Presidential Sanction

The federal government moved to establish a state of emergency and enforce governor rule across Khyber Pakhtunkhwa following formal executive steps taken in the federal capital. According to Dawn, the president gave formal approval to these sweeping governance measures after receiving recommendations sent directly by the federal cabinet. The cabinet session that finalized the proposal took place under the leadership of Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif, who gathered senior ministers to evaluate the deteriorating security environment in the province. The move represents a major transition in the administrative relationship between the central government and the provincial executive.

As reported by ARY News, the decisions were anchored in specific constitutional mechanisms, specifically Article 232 and Article 234. These constitutional articles provide the central government with the authority to declare emergency conditions and place provincial administration under direct federal oversight when standard provincial machinery faces extraordinary instability. Following the cabinet discussion, the prime minister dispatched the formal advisory documents to the presidency. Dawn confirmed that the President House later issued a statement validating the implementation of both emergency measures and governor rule under the cited provisions.

The execution of the process involved high-level personal delivery of the executive advice. Federal Minister for Interior Mohsin Naqvi and Minister of State for Interior Talal Chaudhry traveled together to the President House carrying the cabinet recommendation. This direct procedural delivery highlighted the urgency with which the federal cabinet treated the matter. The rapid formal validation by the presidency effectively suspended the standard provincial executive authority, placing executive oversight directly into federal hands.

Federal Stance on Countering Regional Insecurity

The official justification for this constitutional intervention centered on internal peace and stability in the province. Dawn reported that the federal cabinet based its recommendation on what government officials described as deliberate disengagement from the fight against terrorism by the provincial administration. The federal cabinet determined that the security situation in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa had degraded to a point where federal intervention could no longer be deferred. The central leadership argued that regional administrative authorities were failing to meet their responsibilities in countering militant activities and managing security issues.

Speaking informally to media representatives, Interior Minister Mohsin Naqvi indicated that federal authorities had explored other alternatives before adopting this extreme step. He stated that there was no room left to avoid the decision, suggesting that extensive efforts to prevent governor rule had reached an impasse. The interior minister explained that the government had sought accommodations to avoid invoking emergency powers, but those initiatives ultimately failed to yield viable results.

To support his remarks regarding the extent of these efforts, Mohsin Naqvi referred directly to the head of the provincial ruling party. He suggested that reporters should ask Pakistan Tehreek-e-Insaf chairman Barrister Gohar how far he had gone in attempting to prevent the situation from reaching this outcome. Naqvi maintained that the central government had engaged in extensive dialogue to avert the enforcement of direct rule, but the ground realities and governance gaps left no other option.

Proposed Administrative Framework for Impacted Districts

Before finalizing the complete takeover of provincial executive powers, federal policymakers reviewed several specialized structural arrangements for handling the security challenges. ARY News reported that one proposal discussed during cabinet deliberations involved isolating twelve severely unrest-affected districts and placing them under direct federal administration. This model was examined as a potential means of isolating violence and applying concentrated federal resources without dismantling the broader provincial structure.

The twelve areas considered for this direct federal management plan included eight districts from the former Federally Administered Tribal Areas, alongside the settled districts of Lakki Marwat, Karak, and Kohat. Under this proposal, these specific locations would have operated under an entirely independent administrative system. The plan called for establishing a separate secretariat, designating an independent administrator, and allocating dedicated financial resources specifically targeted at managing civil and security issues in those troubled territories.

Another administrative choice brought before the cabinet involved delegating chief executive authority to Khyber Pakhtunkhwa Governor Faisal Karim Kundi. Sources cited by ARY News noted that the governor had maintained contact with the President House to examine various governance options for the province. Governor Kundi traveled between Islamabad and Peshawar to take part in high-level consultations in the federal capital before returning to the provincial headquarters, reflecting the fluid administrative talks that preceded the final declaration.

Political Outreaches and Consultations with the Awami National Party

In the lead-up to the formal declaration, federal leaders attempted to gather political backing across different political factions active in the province. ARY News reported that Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif and Interior Minister Mohsin Naqvi personally contacted Awami National Party chief Senator Aimal Wali Khan to discuss the impending imposition of governor rule and an emergency. The federal leadership sought to build a political consensus among regional stakeholders to justify and support the intervention.

The outreach, however, encountered firm resistance from the nationalist leader. Senator Aimal Wali Khan declined to endorse either the imposition of an emergency or the enforcement of governor rule in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa. According to sources cited by ARY News, the Awami National Party chief firmly advised the federal leadership that the provincial government led by Pakistan Tehreek-e-Insaf should be permitted to finish its constitutional term. His position reflected a refusal to endorse federal actions that unseat an elected provincial setup, regardless of political disagreements.

During these political talks, the central government also floated executive power-sharing proposals to win regional backing. ARY News revealed that Interior Minister Mohsin Naqvi offered to appoint former chief minister Amir Haider Khan Hoti as the governor of the province. Despite this offer to place a prominent Awami National Party figure in the top provincial office, the party leadership maintained its principled refusal to support the federal takeover.

Sharp Reactions from Regional and National Political Parties

The imposition of direct federal oversight prompted swift condemnation from political organizations with strong roots in the province. Dawn reported that Pakistan Tehreek-e-Insaf chairman Barrister Gohar strongly rejected the central government actions, denouncing the decision as completely undemocratic. The party leader stated that sustainable peace cannot be established by undermining provincial autonomy, arguing that removing provincial authority damages the democratic framework and disrupts local governance.

Resistance to the federal move was not limited to the provincial ruling party. Jamiat Ulema-e-Islam also issued strong warnings against the imposition of emergency measures and governor rule in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa. ARY News reported that the party characterized both measures as highly undesirable and dangerous for the political stability of the country. The religious-political party warned that taking direct control of the province would worsen existing grievances and deepen administrative dysfunction.

Appearing on the ARY News program Sawal Yeh Hai, Jamiat Ulema-e-Islam spokesperson Aslam Ghauri revealed that federal representatives had previously extended clear assurances to party leader Maulana Fazlur Rehman. Ghauri explained that a federal delegation had assured Fazlur Rehman during an earlier meeting that neither an emergency nor governor rule would be implemented. The spokesperson questioned why the government had initiated political discussions with their leadership if it ultimately intended to move forward with the very measures it had promised to avoid.

Administrative Realignment and Governance Under Governor Rule

Following the presidential approval, the administrative apparatus in Peshawar began to restructure under direct federal command. Dawn reported that Dr Muhammad Fakhr Alam was appointed as the new chief secretary for Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, establishing an administrative head to steer provincial operations during the emergency period. The appointment of the chief secretary marks a central step in restoring bureaucratic stability and organizing security coordination under federal supervision.

Governor Faisal Karim Kundi addressed the provincial public following the formal transfer of executive powers. According to Dawn, the governor called for collective unity across political and social divisions, stating that the new phase should be utilized to begin a new chapter in the development of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa. His public address sought to calm administrative tensions and encourage cooperation between state institutions and regional civil departments during the period of governor rule.

Analysts will closely monitor how local institutions, law enforcement bodies, and provincial departments adapt to direct federal control in the coming days. The central administration must demonstrate that emergency measures can stabilize the security environment in the unrest-affected districts while managing vocal political pushback from regional parties. The response of the provincial public, the actions of political parties in parliament, and the performance of the newly appointed chief secretary will indicate the path ahead for governance in the province.
