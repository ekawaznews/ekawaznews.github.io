Pakistan Women Miss Asian Games Cricket Final After Athapaththu Blitz

By Umer Javed | 20 September 2026 | 5 min read
Photo: Ricky Febrianto / Pexels

Pakistan's women's cricket team missed out on the Asian Games final, suffering an emphatic eight-wicket defeat against Sri Lanka in the semi-final at Korogi Sports Park in Nisshin, Japan. Sri Lanka captain Chamari Athapaththu's unbeaten 83 off 50 balls powered her side to chase down Pakistan's challenging target of 177 with 13 balls to spare.

Pakistan's women's cricket team has missed out on a spot in the Asian Games final, succumbing to an emphatic eight-wicket defeat at the hands of Sri Lanka in their semi-final encounter. The match, held at Korogi Sports Park in Nisshin, Japan on Sunday, saw Sri Lanka captain Chamari Athapaththu deliver a match-winning performance that steered her side to victory.

Fatima Sana's charges had initially appeared to set a challenging target, posting a commendable total of 177 for four after their allotted overs. Ayesha Zafar was the top-scorer for Pakistan, contributing a significant 71 runs off just 40 balls, providing the backbone for what seemed like a competitive score on the board. The innings showcased periods of strong batting, with Pakistani batters aiming to establish a formidable total that would test the Sri Lankan chasing side.

However, Sri Lanka's response was swift and decisive. Chasing 178 for victory, the Sri Lankan team reached their target with 13 balls to spare, ultimately finishing on 178 for two. The architect of this successful chase was undoubtedly Chamari Athapaththu, who remained unbeaten on a blistering 83 runs from 50 deliveries. Her innings was a display of aggressive and calculated stroke play, featuring seven boundaries and an impressive six maximums, effectively dismantling the Pakistani bowling attack.

Athapaththu's dominance was a significant factor in the semi-final's outcome. Her ability to score freely and maintain a high strike rate put Pakistan's bowlers under constant pressure, making it difficult for them to create opportunities for breakthroughs. She was ably supported by Harshitha Samarawickrama, who remained unbeaten on 17 runs, ensuring that Sri Lanka maintained their momentum and crossed the finish line comfortably.

The loss marks a significant moment for the Pakistani contingent at the Asian Games, as the team had harboured ambitions of reaching the final and competing for the gold medal. The semi-final stage of any major tournament presents immense pressure, and while Pakistan's batting performance set a strong foundation, the Sri Lankan chase, particularly Athapaththu's individual brilliance, proved too much to overcome on this occasion.

The Asian Games Cricket

The inclusion of cricket in multi-sport events like the Asian Games provides a unique platform for the sport, particularly for women's cricket, to gain wider recognition and exposure across the continent. For many nations in Asia, cricket holds a prominent place in their sporting culture, and participation in the Asian Games offers an opportunity to compete for national pride on a larger stage.

The women's cricket tournament at the Asian Games brings together some of the strongest teams from the region, fostering intense competition and showcasing the growing talent pool in women's cricket. These tournaments are not just about winning medals; they are also about promoting the sport, inspiring new generations of athletes, and providing valuable international experience to players.

For teams like Pakistan and Sri Lanka, who are well-established cricketing nations, the Asian Games represent a chance to assert their regional dominance and benchmark their progress against their continental rivals. The format of T20 cricket, known for its fast-paced nature and potential for dramatic shifts in momentum, adds an exciting dimension to these competitions, making every match a high-stakes affair.

The Korogi Sports Park in Nisshin, Japan, served as the venue for these important matches, offering a professional setting for the athletes to compete. The conditions, while not explicitly detailed, would have played a role in how both teams approached their game plans, from pitch behavior to outfield speed, all factors that impact T20 strategies.

The presence of strong individual performers, such as Chamari Athapaththu, often dictates the flow and outcome of these high-pressure encounters. Her performance was the impact a single player can have in the shortest format of the game, turning the tide decisively in her team's favour when it mattered most.

Pakistan's Campaign and Reflections

Pakistan's journey to the semi-finals of the Asian Games women's cricket tournament reflected their consistent efforts and skill throughout the competition. Reaching the semi-final stage itself is an accomplishment, demonstrating the team's ability to compete at a high level against some of Asia's best cricketing sides. The team, led by Fatima Sana, had shown glimpses of their potential in previous matches.

The match against Sri Lanka served as a significant test, highlighting both the strengths and areas for improvement within the squad. While Ayesha Zafar's innings was a notable highlight, providing the impetus required to set a challenging total, the team's bowling and fielding efforts could not contain the powerful Sri Lankan batting lineup, especially given Athapaththu's form.

In T20 cricket, even a target of 177 can be chased down if the opposition bats aggressively and maintains a high scoring rate. The ability to defend such totals often hinges on early breakthroughs and containing key batters. On this occasion, Sri Lanka's top order, spearheaded by their captain, managed to negate Pakistan's bowling plans effectively.

For the Pakistani team, this semi-final loss will undoubtedly be a moment for reflection and learning. Analyzing the performance, particularly the strategies employed in defending a competitive score, will be essential for future tournaments. Identifying areas where improvements can be made, both individually and as a unit, is a standard practice for any professional sports team.

The experience gained from playing in a high-stakes semi-final at a major multi-sport event like the Asian Games is invaluable. It exposes players to different pressures and competitive environments, which aids in their development and prepares them for future international challenges. The lessons learned from such encounters often contribute to long-term growth and resilience within the squad.

Looking ahead, the team will focus on regrouping and continuing their efforts to elevate women's cricket in Pakistan. The competitive nature of the Asian Games women's cricket tournament highlights the importance of continuous development, tactical innovation, and consistent performance at all levels of the game. The commitment to excellence remains a guiding principle for the team as they move forward from this particular result.
