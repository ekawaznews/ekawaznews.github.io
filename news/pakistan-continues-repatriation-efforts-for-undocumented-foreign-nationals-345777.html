Pakistan Continues Repatriation Efforts for Undocumented Foreign Nationals

By Umer Javed | 2 October 2026 | 5 min read
Photo: Helena Lopes / Pexels

Pakistan is actively engaged in the process of returning undocumented foreign nationals to their home countries, a policy that has gained national attention. This initiative affects hundreds of thousands of individuals, primarily from Afghanistan, and involves complex logistical and humanitarian considerations.

The process of undocumented foreign nationals returning from Pakistan to their home countries is a significant national undertaking currently underway across the country. This large-scale movement, described as 'homeward bound' by various reports, reflects a determined policy by the Pakistani government to address the long-standing issue of unregistered residents within its borders.

Authorities have initiated this process with a stated aim of regularising the presence of all foreign nationals and ensuring national security. The directive primarily affects individuals who do not possess valid documentation or legal status to reside in Pakistan, with a substantial number being Afghan nationals.

Government Policy and Stated Rationale

The Pakistani government formally announced its plan for the repatriation of undocumented foreign nationals several months ago, setting a deadline for voluntary departure before enforcement measures would commence. This policy was framed as a necessary step for Pakistan's sovereignty and national interest, citing concerns related to security, economic strain, and the proper management of its population.

Officials have consistently stated that the policy is not targeted at any specific nationality but applies to all individuals residing in Pakistan without legal documentation. However, due to historical circumstances and geographical proximity, a vast majority of those affected are Afghans.

The government has emphasised that the repatriation process is being conducted in a phased manner, aiming for an organised and systematic approach. It has established transit camps and facilitated transport for those voluntarily returning.

Historical Context of Afghan Presence in Pakistan

Pakistan has hosted millions of Afghan refugees and migrants for over four decades, primarily since the Soviet-Afghan war in 1979. Successive waves of conflict and instability in Afghanistan led to large-scale displacement, with Pakistan becoming one of the largest refugee-hosting nations globally.

Many Afghan families have lived in Pakistan for generations, with children born and raised on Pakistani soil. Over time, the lines between registered refugees, unregistered individuals, and economic migrants became blurred, creating a complex demographic situation.

While Pakistan has extended hospitality for decades, the government has recently highlighted the socio-economic burden and security challenges associated with a large unregistered population. These concerns have been central to the current policy shift.

Logistical and Humanitarian Challenges of Repatriation

The scale of the repatriation effort presents considerable logistical challenges. Managing the movement of hundreds of thousands of individuals requires extensive coordination among various government agencies, including law enforcement, border management, and humanitarian organisations.

Transit points have been established near border crossings to process individuals and facilitate their return. These facilities aim to provide temporary shelter, food, and basic medical aid before individuals cross into Afghanistan.

Humanitarian organisations and international bodies have expressed concerns regarding the conditions of return, particularly for vulnerable populations such as women, children, the elderly, and those with disabilities. Calls have been made for the process to adhere to international human rights standards and ensure dignified and humane treatment for all individuals.

Reports from the border areas indicate a continuous flow of individuals making their way back. Many families are seen carrying limited belongings, often reflecting years of their lives spent in Pakistan. The emotional toll on these families, uprooted from communities they have known for decades, is palpable.

Impact on Pakistan and Regional Dynamics

The repatriation drive has significant implications for Pakistan's internal dynamics and its relationship with Afghanistan. Domestically, the government hopes to alleviate pressure on public services, improve law and order, and better manage its resources.

Economically, the presence of a large unregistered workforce has been a subject of debate, with arguments made about its impact on local employment and the informal economy. The government believes that a more regulated population will contribute to better economic planning.

Diplomatically, the policy has strained relations between Pakistan and the interim Afghan government. Afghan authorities have called for a more gradual and coordinated approach, expressing concerns about their capacity to absorb such a large number of returnees and provide them with adequate support and reintegration opportunities.

Pakistan has maintained that the decision is an internal matter, driven by national security and economic considerations, and has urged international partners to assist Afghanistan in managing the returnee population.

International Response and Calls for Support

International organisations and several countries have acknowledged Pakistan's long-standing role in hosting Afghan refugees. However, they have also urged Pakistan to ensure that the repatriation process is conducted in a voluntary, safe, and dignified manner, in line with international principles.

The United Nations High Commissioner for Refugees (UNHCR) and other aid agencies have been actively involved in monitoring the situation and providing assistance where possible. They have called for increased international support for Afghanistan to help it cope with the influx of returnees, many of whom face uncertain futures in a country already grappling with humanitarian crises.

The international community's focus remains on ensuring that humanitarian considerations are prioritised throughout this complex process. There is a general consensus that sustainable solutions require regional cooperation and strong support from global partners.

Looking Ahead: The Path Forward

The 'homeward bound' movement is an ongoing process, with authorities continuing their efforts to ensure compliance with the policy. The long-term impact of this large-scale repatriation on Pakistan's socio-economic and regional relations will unfold over time.

For the thousands of individuals returning to Afghanistan, the journey back signifies the beginning of new challenges related to resettlement, access to livelihoods, and reintegration into a country that has been a stranger to many of them for decades. The complexities of this situation demand continued attention and support from all stakeholders.

The government's resolve to implement its policy remains firm, while simultaneously acknowledging the humanitarian aspects involved. This balance between national interest and human welfare will remain a central theme as the repatriation process progresses.
