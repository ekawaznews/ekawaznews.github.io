Foul Smell Grips Karachi as Residents Seek Answers

By Umer Javed | 17 September 2026 | 5 min read

Residents across Karachi reported an unpleasant foul smell this week, raising concerns about air quality and environmental conditions. Authorities and environmental experts have been called upon to investigate the source of the odor.

Residents across Karachi woke up to an unusual and unpleasant foul smell on Thursday, prompting concerns about the city's air quality and environmental conditions. The odor, reported by people in several neighborhoods, spread quickly and led many to question its source. According to reports, the smell was noticed in various parts of the city, though the exact areas most affected have not been confirmed.

What Could Be Causing the Smell

Karachi, the largest city in Pakistan and one of the most densely populated in the world, faces a range of environmental challenges. Industrial activity in and around the city, combined with heavy traffic congestion and inadequate waste management systems, often contributes to poor air quality. These factors can produce strong and unpleasant odors that affect residents on a regular basis.

The city is home to major industrial zones, including SITE and Korangi, where factories and manufacturing units release various pollutants into the air. The Port of Karachi, one of the largest and busiest ports in South Asia, also generates significant emissions and odors from shipping activity and cargo handling. In addition, large-scale solid waste dumping sites around the city have long been a source of foul smells, particularly during warmer months when heat intensifies the odor.

Previous Incidents of Bad Odor

Karachi has experienced similar episodes in the past, where residents reported a thick, unpleasant smell hanging over the city for hours or even days. On several occasions, environmental authorities attributed such incidents to industrial emissions, open burning of waste, or emissions from passing cargo ships. However, in many cases, a definitive source was never officially confirmed, leaving residents frustrated and concerned about long-term health effects.

The Sindh Environmental Protection Agency has in the past issued warnings about air pollution levels in Karachi, particularly during the monsoon and winter seasons when atmospheric conditions trap pollutants closer to the ground. According to reports, these pollution spikes have been linked to increased cases of respiratory illness among the city's population.

Health Concerns Among Residents

The foul smell has raised alarm among residents, many of whom reported headaches, nausea, and irritation in the eyes and throat after exposure. People living near industrial areas and major roads said the odor was stronger in their localities. Some residents took to social media to express their frustration, questioning why authorities had not yet identified or addressed the source of the smell.

Medical experts have previously warned that prolonged exposure to polluted air can lead to serious health problems, including asthma, bronchitis, and other respiratory diseases. Children and the elderly are considered most at risk. According to reports, several hospitals in Karachi have recorded a rise in patients with breathing difficulties during periods of heavy pollution in the past.

What Authorities Are Doing

Officials from the Karachi Port Trust and the Sindh Environmental Protection Agency were approached for comment regarding the current incident. According to reports, no formal statement has been issued so far explaining the cause of the foul smell. Environmental activists and civic organizations have repeatedly called on provincial and federal authorities to take concrete steps to monitor and control pollution in the city.

The Karachi Municipal Corporation has also been urged to improve waste collection and disposal systems, which many experts say are a major contributor to the city's odor problems. According to reports, large portions of Karachi's waste end up in open dumpsites, where they decompose and release harmful gases. These dumpsites are often located close to residential areas, making the impact on public health even more severe.

A City Facing Environmental Challenges

Karachi's environmental record has been a growing concern in recent years. Rapid urbanization, unchecked construction, deforestation in the surrounding areas, and a lack of green spaces have all worsened the city's ability to manage pollution. The coastline, which could serve as a natural buffer, has been increasingly encroached upon, reducing its capacity to absorb pollutants.

The city also faces seasonal challenges. During the summer, temperatures in Karachi regularly exceed 40 degrees Celsius, which accelerates the release of volatile compounds from waste and industrial sources. Strong winds can carry these pollutants across large distances, affecting areas far from the original source. According to reports, the situation tends to deteriorate further during the rainy season when stagnant water and waste mix to produce additional odors.

Environmentalists have stressed that a comprehensive air quality monitoring system is needed across Karachi to identify pollution sources in real time. Currently, the city has very few operational air quality monitoring stations, which makes it difficult to pinpoint the exact cause of incidents like the foul smell reported recently. According to reports, authorities have so far not announced any plans to expand the monitoring network.

Residents hope that the recent episode will prompt a stronger response from the relevant authorities. For now, many are advised to stay indoors when the smell becomes unbearable and to use masks if they must go outside. The incident serves as a reminder of the environmental challenges that Karachi continues to face, and the need for urgent action to protect public health and the city's air quality.
