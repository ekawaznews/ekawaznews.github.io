Pakistan Opens Treasury Bills and Bonds to Public via Stock Market

By Umer Javed | 30 September 2026 | 4 min read
Photo: Walls.io / Pexels

The government announced that the general public can trade government securities through the stock market, a move linked to the IMF programme and a new local currency bond market plan.

The government announced that the general public would be allowed to trade government securities, including treasury bills and bonds, through the stock market as part of efforts to improve compliance under the International Monetary Fund programme currently being reviewed by a visiting staff mission for the disbursement of around $1.2 billion.

Opening the Market to the Public

On Tuesday, Finance Minister Muhammad Aurangzeb revealed that citizens will soon be able to purchase and sell treasury bills and bonds on the Karachi Stock Exchange. The decision follows a meeting with an IMF staff mission led by Iva Petro, during which the strategic action plan for a Local Currency Bond Market was discussed.

The IMF Connection and Fiscal Implications

Pakistan’s engagement with the IMF is a key element of the country’s broader economic strategy. The $1.2 billion disbursement is contingent on meeting certain fiscal and monetary targets, and the new bond market is seen as a tool to help the government meet those targets by expanding its funding options.

Why a Local Currency Bond Market Matters

Allowing the public to trade government securities directly introduces a new channel for raising capital. It reduces the need for foreign borrowing and can help keep the exchange rate more stable. By issuing bonds in Pakistani rupees, the state can align debt maturities with domestic cash flows, easing pressure on the balance of payments.

Benefits of a Public Bond Market

For investors, the market offers a low‑risk, government‑backed investment that can diversify portfolios. For the government, it provides a way to spread debt over a broader base, potentially lowering borrowing costs. A deeper market also signals transparency and can attract foreign institutional investors who prefer a regulated domestic platform.

Regulatory and Institutional Requirements

To make the market functional, the Securities and Exchange Commission of Pakistan must tighten rules on disclosure, pricing, and settlement. The stock exchange will need to upgrade its trading platform to handle bond transactions and ensure that settlement cycles are compatible with bond maturities.

Challenges Ahead

Pakistan still faces a limited pool of qualified investors. Many citizens lack experience with fixed‑income instruments, and there is a risk that liquidity will be low in the early stages. The government will need to run education campaigns and perhaps offer incentives to attract retail investors.

Potential Impact on the Fiscal Deficit

By tapping a new source of domestic funding, the state can reduce reliance on external borrowing, which often carries higher interest rates and exchange‑rate risk. If the bond market grows, it could ease the fiscal deficit and improve the country’s credit profile over time.

Investor Base and Market Depth

Institutional investors such as pension funds and insurance companies are likely to be the first buyers. Their participation could provide the depth needed to keep spreads narrow and the market liquid. The role of mutual funds and unit trust schemes will also grow as they seek safe, long‑term assets.

Risk of Default and Confidence

Pakistan’s debt sustainability has been a concern for years. The success of the bond market depends on the government’s ability to meet its repayment obligations. A single default could erode confidence and stall the market’s development.

Need for Investor Education

Retail investors need clear information about bond yields, tax treatment, and maturity structures. The Securities and Exchange Commission is expected to publish guidelines and host seminars to bridge the knowledge gap.

Role of the Stock Exchange

The Karachi Stock Exchange will become the hub for bond trading, and its systems must support both electronic and over‑the‑counter transactions. Market makers will need to provide liquidity and maintain orderly pricing.

Comparison with Other Markets

Countries like Bangladesh and Sri Lanka have already opened their bond markets to the public. Pakistan can learn from their regulatory frameworks and the incentives they offered to early adopters.

Potential for Foreign Institutional Investors

Foreign investors who prefer a domestic currency exposure may view the new market as an attractive option. However, they will assess the legal framework, tax regime, and liquidity before committing large sums.

Timeline and Implementation Steps

The plan is expected to roll out in phases. Initial steps will involve setting up the necessary legal provisions, followed by a pilot issuance of a few treasury bills. Full market operations could be operational by the end of the next fiscal year.

Looking Forward

Pakistan’s move to open its treasury market to the public signals a willingness to deepen financial markets and improve fiscal resilience. The success of the plan will hinge on transparent regulation, investor confidence, and the government's continued commitment to fiscal prudence. If the bond market develops as intended, it could become a cornerstone of Pakistan’s long‑term economic strategy, providing a stable source of domestic capital and a platform for responsible growth.
