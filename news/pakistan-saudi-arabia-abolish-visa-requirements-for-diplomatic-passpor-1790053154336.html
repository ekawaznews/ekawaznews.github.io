Pakistan, Saudi Arabia Abolish Visa Requirements for Diplomatic Passports

U
Umer Javed
Political Reporter
| 🕒 22 September 2026 at 09:59 am | 📖 4 min read
Share: 💬 WhatsApp 📘 Facebook ✈️ Telegram 🐦 Twitter
📷 Photo: Pixabay / Pexels | ekawaznews.github.io

Pakistan and Saudi Arabia signed an agreement on Tuesday to abolish visa requirements for holders of diplomatic, official and special passports. The decision was reached in New York on the sidelines of the United Nations General Assembly.

Signing Ceremony

Deputy Prime Minister Ishaq Dar met Saudi Foreign Minister Prince Faisal Bin Farhan at the UN headquarters to formalise the accord. Both officials exchanged the signed document before a small gathering of diplomats and press. The event was brief but carried significant symbolic weight, as it took place at the heart of the international community.

Official Statement

In a post on X, Deputy Prime Minister Dar stated that the step would "further facilitate high‑level engagement and institutional exchanges, and contribute to advancing our enduring and brotherly strategic partnership across all areas of mutual interest." He added that the agreement would simplify diplomatic travel and strengthen ties between the two governments.

Background of Visa Policy

Until now, Pakistani diplomatic staff, officials and holders of special passports required a visa to enter Saudi Arabia. The new agreement eliminates that requirement, allowing them to travel without prior clearance. Saudi Arabia had previously maintained a similar policy for Pakistani nationals holding diplomatic credentials, but the formal abolition marks a new stage in bilateral cooperation.

Implications for Bilateral Relations

The removal of visa barriers is expected to ease the movement of diplomats, aid workers and business delegations. It will likely reduce administrative delays and foster quicker response times in areas such as trade negotiations, cultural exchanges and security cooperation. The agreement signals a deepening of the strategic partnership that has existed since the early 2000s.

Impact on Trade and Investment

Saudi Arabia is a major investor in Pakistan, with significant projects in energy, infrastructure and agriculture. The visa waiver will allow Pakistani trade officials to visit Saudi Arabia more frequently, potentially accelerating project approvals and investment flows. Likewise, Saudi business leaders will find it easier to conduct visits to Pakistan, encouraging joint ventures and market expansion.

Historical Context

Pakistan and Saudi Arabia share a long history of cooperation rooted in shared religious and cultural ties. The two nations have collaborated on defense, education and humanitarian aid. The current agreement builds on past initiatives, such as the 2019 memorandum of understanding on trade and the 2020 pact on energy cooperation.

Regional Implications

The agreement may influence other Gulf Cooperation Council countries to review their visa policies toward Pakistani officials. It also aligns with Saudi Arabia's broader strategy of engaging with South Asian partners to diversify diplomatic and economic relationships in the region.

Procedural Aspects

Under the terms of the accord, both governments will issue formal notifications to their respective immigration authorities. The implementation will involve updating electronic visa systems and training border officials to recognize the new status of diplomatic, official and special passports. The process is expected to be straightforward, as similar arrangements exist within the Gulf region.

Implementation Timeline

While the agreement was signed on Tuesday, the actual removal of visa requirements is slated to take effect within the next quarter. Both sides have agreed to monitor the rollout and address any operational challenges promptly. Officials from Pakistan’s Ministry of Foreign Affairs have indicated that the necessary legal amendments will be introduced in the coming weeks.

Reactions and Expectations

Pakistani diplomats in Riyadh welcomed the decision, noting that it would simplify routine visits for consular staff and aid workers. Members of the Pakistani diaspora in Saudi Arabia also expressed optimism, as the new policy will reduce bureaucratic hurdles for family visits and official duties. Saudi officials have not yet released a formal statement, but the presence of the Foreign Minister at the signing suggests strong support.

Comparative Perspective

Several other countries, including the United Arab Emirates and Qatar, have already abolished visa requirements for Pakistani diplomatic passports. The new agreement places Saudi Arabia in line with these Gulf partners, reinforcing its image as a facilitator of diplomatic mobility in the region.

Future Prospects

Beyond visa waivers, both governments have hinted at exploring further cooperation in areas such as education, technology transfer and counterterrorism. The agreement sets a foundation for deeper engagement, with the potential to open new avenues for collaboration in the coming years. The two sides have expressed a commitment to continue dialogue on strengthening institutional ties and expanding mutual benefits.

Conclusion

The abolition of visa requirements for diplomatic, official and special passports marks a clear step toward greater cooperation between Pakistan and Saudi Arabia. By simplifying travel for high‑level officials, the agreement is poised to enhance diplomatic engagement, simplify institutional exchanges and support the ongoing development of a strategic partnership that spans multiple sectors. As both countries move forward, the new arrangement will likely serve as a catalyst for further collaboration across the political, economic and social spectrum.
