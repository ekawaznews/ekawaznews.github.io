Naqvi had no objection to India's trophy refusal, claims BCCI official

By Umer Javed | 15 September 2026 | 5 min read
Photo: Sayantan Dhar / Pexels

BCCI Vice President Rajeev Shukla stated that Asian Cricket Council Chairman Mohsin Naqvi did not object to the Indian women’s team declining the Asia Cup trophy. This statement follows mounting criticism regarding interactions between Indian cricket officials and Naqvi during the final match.

Asian Cricket Council (ACC) Chairman Mohsin Naqvi had no objection to the Indian women’s team refusing to collect the Asia Cup trophy from him, according to Board of Control for Cricket in India (BCCI) Vice President Rajeev Shukla. Shukla made this assertion on Monday, addressing a situation that has drawn considerable attention within cricketing circles.

The remarks by the BCCI Vice President come as discussions intensified over the interaction, or lack thereof, between Shukla himself and BCCI Secretary Devajit Saikia with Mohsin Naqvi during the Women's Asia Cup final held in Dubai on Sunday. The incident has cast a spotlight on the often-sensitive relationship between the cricket boards of India and Pakistan, particularly when their officials are present at the same international sporting events.

India emerged victorious in the final match, defeating Sri Lanka by a margin of 72 runs to secure the Women’s Asia Cup title. However, the Indian team chose not to receive the trophy from Naqvi, who holds multiple significant positions. Besides his role as the ACC Chairman, Mohsin Naqvi also serves as Pakistan’s Interior Minister and is the Chairman of the Pakistan Cricket Board (PCB). These combined roles lend a distinct political and administrative dimension to any interaction involving him, especially in a cross-border context.

Shukla’s statement indicates an effort to clarify the situation and perhaps mitigate the perception of a diplomatic snub. While the specific reasons for India’s decision to decline receiving the trophy from Naqvi were not fully elaborated in the provided context, Shukla's comments focused on Naqvi's alleged lack of objection to the Indian team's stance. This suggests an attempt to frame the incident as something not perceived negatively by the Pakistani official involved.

The Roles of Key Officials

Mohsin Naqvi’s presence at the Women's Asia Cup final in his capacity as ACC Chairman is standard protocol for the head of the regional cricketing body. His additional roles as Pakistan’s Interior Minister and PCB Chairman, however, introduce layers of complexity. The position of Interior Minister carries significant political weight, and the PCB Chairman is the highest authority in Pakistani cricket. These roles combined mean that any action or interaction involving him is often viewed through both a sporting and a geopolitical lens, particularly in the context of India-Pakistan relations.

Rajeev Shukla, as the Vice President of the BCCI, is a prominent figure in Indian cricket administration. His statements carry authority within the Board and are often reflective of its official stance. Similarly, Devajit Saikia, as the BCCI Secretary, plays a key administrative role. The presence of such high-ranking officials from both the BCCI and the ACC at an important regional final highlights the significance of the tournament and the broader cricketing in Asia.

The Asian Cricket Council, which organises the Asia Cup, aims to promote and develop cricket across the Asian continent. Its chairmanship rotates among member nations, and Naqvi's current tenure places him at the helm of regional cricket governance. The ACC’s mandate includes fostering cooperation and goodwill among its members, a task that can sometimes be challenging given the political dynamics between certain nations, most notably India and Pakistan.

Context of India-Pakistan Cricket Relations

Cricket matches between India and Pakistan are among the most highly anticipated sporting events globally, drawing immense viewership and passionate support from both sides of the border. However, bilateral cricketing ties between the two nations have been largely suspended for several years due to political tensions. The last bilateral series between India and Pakistan took place in 2012-13, and since then, the teams have only met in multi-nation tournaments such as the Asia Cup and ICC events.

These multi-nation tournaments, therefore, become rare occasions where players and officials from both countries share the same platform. Such encounters are frequently scrutinised for any signs of diplomatic interaction or lack thereof. The historical context of strained political relations often overshadows the sporting spirit, leading to heightened sensitivity around gestures, statements, and even ceremonial activities like trophy presentations.

The decision by the Indian women’s team not to collect the trophy from Mohsin Naqvi, irrespective of the stated lack of objection from Naqvi himself, will be viewed by many through the prism of this complex history. It reflects the ongoing cautious approach taken by both sides in official interactions, a pattern that has become characteristic of India-Pakistan sporting engagements.

The Women's Asia Cup and its Significance

The Women's Asia Cup is a premier regional tournament that plays a vital role in promoting women's cricket in Asia. India has a dominant record in the tournament, having won multiple titles, which highlights its strength in the women's game. The event provides a important platform for emerging talent and helps raise the profile of women's cricket across the continent.

The final match, where India secured a comfortable victory over Sri Lanka, was the growing competitiveness and skill in women's cricket. While the on-field action celebrated the sport, the off-field events surrounding the trophy presentation have unfortunately drawn significant attention. Such incidents, even if minor, can sometimes detract from the sporting achievements and the broader message of unity that sports often aim to convey.

The incident highlights the delicate balance that cricket administrators must maintain, particularly when political sensitivities intersect with sporting protocols. While the BCCI Vice President has sought to clarify Naqvi’s reaction, the episode adds another layer to the ongoing narrative of India-Pakistan cricketing relations, where even ceremonial acts can carry significant weight and be interpreted in various ways by the public and media.

The ACC, under Naqvi's chairmanship, is expected to continue its efforts to foster cricket development in the region. However, incidents like the one at the Women's Asia Cup final serve as a reminder of the challenges that remain in separating sports from the broader political, especially when officials holding governmental positions are involved in cricketing events.
