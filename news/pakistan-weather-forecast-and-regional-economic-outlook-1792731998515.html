Moving into Saturday, 24 October 2026, the high temperature in Karachi will edge upward slightly to thirty-five degrees Celsius while the low rests at twenty-four degrees Celsius. Conditions stay clear with zero chance of precipitation.

For Sunday, 25 October 2026, and Monday, 26 October 2026, the high climbs further to thirty-six degrees Celsius each day. Broader Economic Context and Regional Projections

In broader economic reporting, Dawn reported that the International Monetary Fund forecasted a contraction of 1.3 percent in economic output for Pakistan, the Middle East, and North Africa in 2026. According to Dawn, this drop is attributed by the IMF to the war against Iran involving the United States and Israel, which has impacted energy production, shipping, tourism, trade, and confidence.

Dawn further noted that the IMF projected a subsequent rebound to 6.6 percent growth in 2027, though regional output would generally remain below pre-war projections. The IMF stated that oil-exporting economies facing direct disruptions to energy production and shipping were hit harder, while oil importers proved more resilient, as reported by Dawn.

This economic outlook provides essential context for understanding financial planning across the country. Observers may evaluate how international developments intersect with domestic stability as institutions monitor these projected shifts in national output.
