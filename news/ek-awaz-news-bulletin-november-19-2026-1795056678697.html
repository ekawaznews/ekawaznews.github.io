Commission Formed to Investigate Illegal Gold Extraction

According to Dawn, the Khyber Pakhtunkhwa governor has established a special eight-member inquiry commission to investigate illegal placer gold mining within the province. A formal notification released by the provincial home and tribal affairs department outlines that retired Justice Asadullah Khan Chamkani will lead this newly formed investigative body. Additional members appointed to the commission include Barrister Mohammad Yaseen Raza, legal adviser Sher Akbar Khan, Deputy Inspector General of Police Wasif Rahman, and Health Department Additional Secretary Engineer Ziarat Khan, alongside representatives from various intelligence agencies.

The notification details that the commission is tasked with examining the complete scale, history, and nature of unauthorized placer gold operations across the province. Particular focus will be directed toward affected regions such as Kohat, Nowshera, and Karak. Authorities intend to establish the exact duration, locations, and overall magnitude of these illegal activities. Observers suggest that this probe could eventually lead to stricter regulatory oversight and accountability measures in the mining sector.

What happens next with the findings of this investigative commission remains to be seen as the newly appointed members begin their assessment of the affected districts.

Pakistan Sentences Citizen Over Foreign Threat Plot

According to Dawn, a Pakistani individual has received a twenty-seven-year prison sentence in connection with a conspiracy directed at plotting an assassination attempt against Donald Trump. The legal proceedings concluded with this significant custodial sentence for the accused party involved in the transnational security plot.

Details regarding the mechanics of the trial and the specific jurisdictions involved were noted briefly in the reporting. Such international legal cases often draw considerable attention from diplomatic circles and security analysts monitoring cross-border security concerns. Observers might watch for any subsequent diplomatic communications or legal appeals following the sentencing.

The ultimate implications of this judicial outcome on bilateral relations and security protocols will become clearer as further developments unfold in the coming weeks.

Gandhara Strategic Dialogue Opens in Islamabad

According to ARY News, the Gandhara Strategic Dialogue 2026 officially began at the Ministry of Foreign Affairs in Islamabad, operating under the core theme of focusing on a shared future. The inauguration brought together international experts, diplomats, parliamentarians, senior government officials, and Buddhist monastics representing eight different countries. Discussions centered around promoting, preserving, and gaining international recognition for the historical Gandhara heritage located within Pakistan.

Federal Minister for National Heritage and Culture Aurangzeb Khan Khichi stated that Gandhara represents one of the most illustrious eras in the ancient history of the region, evolving into a major center for Buddhist philosophy, art, and culture. Imran Shauket serves as the coordinator for the Prime Minister Task Force on Gandhara. The federal minister emphasized that Pakistan takes great pride in safeguarding this heritage as a responsibility toward the international community and future generations, while Foreign Secretary Ambassador Asim Iftikhar Ahmad also welcomed the international delegates.

Future steps for this initiative involve implementing the Gandhara strategy and developing meditation centers and university corners as proposed by visiting monastics.

Child Labor and Education Challenges Highlighted in New Report

According to The Statesman, a recent report indicates that twenty-eight percent of children remain out of school in Pakistan. The publication notes that widespread poverty is actively driving millions of young people into the labor market. This situation presents a persistent challenge for social welfare systems across the country.

Educational advocates and policymakers frequently review such data to gauge the effectiveness of ongoing schooling initiatives. Financial pressures on families often force minors to seek employment rather than attend classes, compounding systemic socio-economic difficulties. Analysts may monitor whether upcoming budgetary allocations address these educational gaps directly.

Addressing the root causes of school dropouts remains a critical talking point for civil society organizations and government planners alike.

Tragic Incident Involves Fallen Power Line in Turbat

According to Dawn, a woman lost her life and her husband along with their son suffered critical burn injuries when a high-voltage power line snapped and fell onto their residence. The incident occurred during the early hours of the day in the Wasto Bazaar area of Turbat within Balochistan. Hospital officials confirmed that the victims were urgently transported to the Teaching Hospital Turbat for medical attention.

Medical staff and attendants reported that the injured family members faced significant hurdles in receiving adequate treatment due to a non-functional burns unit at the local hospital. Local residents pointed out that snapping power lines have developed into a recurring hazard across Turbat and surrounding localities. Previous accidents involving live wires during active power supply hours have been documented in areas such as Malikabad and Shahi Tump.

Local authorities may face increased pressure from residents to repair electrical infrastructure and restore functionality to medical burn facilities in the region.

Minister Reaffirms Pledge to Protect Children from Violence

According to ARY News, Federal Minister for Human Rights Senator Azam Nazeer Tarar spoke at the second Global Ministerial Conference on Ending Violence Against Children held in Manila, Philippines. He reiterated the country's international dedication to eliminating violence against minors, stressing that practical action planning is necessary for child safety. He reminded attendees that the national Constitution mandates free and compulsory education for every child between the ages of five and sixteen.

The federal minister outlined that Pakistan is actively fulfilling commitments made at the Bogota Conference by introducing concrete steps. Last month, the government launched the initial National Strategy to End Violence Against Children covering the period up to the next decade, alongside the Islamabad Capital Territory Child Protection Policy. also, the official noted that child protection units have expanded from forty-two districts at the time of the previous global conference to eighty-one districts currently, with an ultimate target of reaching one hundred districts by the year 2030.

Continuous monitoring and multisectoral coordination will determine the success of these newly established child protection frameworks moving forward.
