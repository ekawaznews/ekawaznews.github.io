Pakistan Wins Volleyball Match at Asian Games, Squash Playoff Adds Bronze

By Umer Javed | 27 September 2026 | 3 min read
Photo: Pavel Danilyuk / Pexels

Pakistan’s men’s volleyball team secured a 3-1 win against Kazakhstan in their opening match at the Asian Games, while squash player Noor Zaman claimed a bronze in an all‑Pakistan playoff.

Pakistan’s men’s volleyball team opened their Asian Games campaign with a 3-1 victory over Kazakhstan, securing a promising start at the Aichi-Nagoya Games.

The 2026 Asian Games, held in the twin cities of Aichi and Nagoya, bring together athletes from across the continent in a showcase of sporting excellence. Pakistan has entered the event with high hopes across multiple disciplines.

In the Pool “A” opener at the Okazaki Chuo Sogo Park Gymnasium, the Pakistani side fought hard to win the first set 25‑16, followed by a 25‑22 win in the second. After a 17‑25 loss in the third, they rallied to take the final set 25‑20, completing a 3‑1 scoreline that lifted the home crowd.

Key Player Performance

Murad Khan emerged as the top scorer, contributing 21 points across the match. His consistent attacks and sharp serve reception were instrumental in maintaining Pakistan’s momentum after the third set loss.

Volleyball has a long tradition in Pakistan, with the national team first competing in the Asian Games in the 1960s. Over the decades, the sport has faced challenges in infrastructure and funding, but recent years have seen a resurgence in youth participation.

Winning against a seasoned Kazakh squad marks a significant milestone for the team. The victory not only boosts morale but also signals that Pakistan can compete against higher ranked opponents in the region.

Next Fixtures

The team will face Uzbekistan on Monday, a match that will test their adaptability after a day of recovery. Their final group encounter will be against the host nation Japan, a formidable opponent that has dominated the tournament in recent years.

Squash Success

While the volleyball triumph added excitement, the day also saw Pakistani squash player Noor Zaman defeat compatriot Mohammad Ashab Irfan in an all‑Pakistan playoff. The win secured a bronze medal for Pakistan in the sport.

With the addition of the squash bronze, Pakistan’s medal count at the Games rises to four bronze medals, reflecting a steady performance across disciplines.

Officials at the Pakistan Sports Board praised the athletes for their discipline and teamwork. “The players have shown great resilience and focus,” a spokesperson said to reporters.

Preparation and Training

Coaches have highlighted the rigorous training schedule that began six months before the Games. The team trained in high altitude camps in the northern region to build stamina and refine techniques.

Head coach Saad Ali emphasized the importance of mental preparation. “We worked on strategies to handle pressure and maintain composure during tight sets,” he explained during a press briefing.

Success on the court carries wider implications for sports development in Pakistan. The government has announced increased funding for volleyball academies and plans to host more international friendly matches to expose local talent to global standards.

As the Games progress, Pakistan’s athletes will aim to build on this early success, striving for higher honors and inspiring a new generation of players across the country.
