Two Italians Killed, Pakistani Injured in Kuwait Radar Site Drone Strike

Fatal Drone Strike on Kuwaiti Air Defence Station Leaves Technicians Dead

A drone strike targeting a military installation in Kuwait on Thursday resulted in the deaths of two Italian technicians and injured two others, including a Pakistani citizen, according to international media reports. The attack occurred against the backdrop of ongoing hostilities between the United States, Israel, and Iran that began in late February 2026. Official reports indicated that the strike targeted an air defence detection facility where the foreign contractors were servicing equipment.

The Italian technicians were identified in news reports as 65-year-old Antonio Saccoccio from Formia and 36-year-old Emanuele Lo Russo from Rome. Both men were working for Rheinmetall Italia, the local unit of the German defence manufacturer Rheinmetall. They were performing maintenance work on detection antennae for the Skyguard air defence system operated by the armed forces of Kuwait. Falling shrapnel from intercepted drones caused the fatal injuries, while a Kuwaiti citizen and a Pakistani national sustained injuries in the incident.

The event marks the first instance of European civilian deaths connected to the broader regional conflict since hostilities broke out early in the year. Although a French soldier lost his life during a drone strike in Iraq in March, European non-combatants had not suffered fatal casualties until this strike in Kuwait. News outlets including BBC, Dawn, and Al Jazeera reported that the attack has triggered sharp diplomatic friction between Gulf nations, European leaders, and Tehran.

Diplomatic Reaction from Italy, Kuwait, and the Gulf Cooperation Council

Following the strike, high-ranking officials in Rome and Kuwait City issued swift condemnations. Italian Foreign Minister Antonio Tajani informed the Italian Senate that the drones used in the assault were of Iranian origin. Tajani held direct consultations with his Kuwaiti counterpart to review the situation. Both governments jointly condemned the strike, expressing grave concern over the targeting of defensive infrastructure and civilian workers.

Kuwait's Foreign Ministry issued an official statement describing the action as a sinful attack against its territory. The ministry emphasized that the state reserves its full rights to implement necessary measures to safeguard its national sovereignty, as well as the protection of citizens and foreign residents. The military command of Kuwait initially designated the targeted location as a vital site before further operational details were shared by Italian authorities.

Regional multilateral organizations also responded swiftly to the escalation. The Secretary-General of the Gulf Cooperation Council, Jasem Albudaiwi, issued a statement strongly condemning the strike, characterizing it as a brutal attack against a member state. Officials across the region expressed concern over the safety of critical security installations that maintain air defence readiness during ongoing regional turbulence.

Tehran Issues Categorical Denial and Proposes Joint Investigation

In response to accusations from Rome and Kuwait, Iranian officials forcefully rejected any involvement in the Thursday drone strike. Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi stated on social media that the incident appeared suspicious and resembled a pre-cooked recipe for war. Araghchi initiated telephone calls with the foreign ministers of Italy and Kuwait to personally deny Iranian responsibility and proposed establishing a joint investigation to determine the origin of the attack.

Iranian military spokespersons also rejected the allegations. Abolfazl Shekarchi, speaking on behalf of both the regular armed forces of Iran and the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps, asserted that Iranian units conducted no military or offensive operations in the area. Shekarchi described the attack as a false flag operation, while a separate military statement accused the United States and Israel of attempting to use the event to justify continued aggressive actions in the region.

The diplomatic representation of Iran in Italy reinforced this position. The Iranian embassy in Rome stated that it categorically rejected all allegations attributing the strike to Tehran. Iranian diplomats suggested that the attack might have been deliberately staged by third parties aiming to draw European nations into the active hostilities surrounding Iran.

Pattern of Military Claims and History of Denials During Hostilities

The absolute denial from Tehran stands out in comparison to its previous public statements during the current conflict. Throughout November, Iranian authorities issued five separate claims of responsibility for military strikes against neighboring countries, including four operations aimed at targets in Kuwait and Bahrain. Tehran has consistently argued that its strikes are directed strictly at hosted United States assets as an act of self-defence, rather than attacks against the host nations themselves.

However, Thursday was not the first occasion on which Iranian officials denied a fatal strike inside Kuwait. On March 30, a strike hit a power and desalination facility in Kuwait, resulting in the death of an Indian worker. On that occasion, Iran similarly rejected responsibility and attributed the incident to Israeli forces. Other civilian infrastructure in Kuwait has experienced repeated disruptions since February, including strikes on oil facilities, power networks, and transportation hubs.

Prior attacks in Kuwait have previously led to civilian casualties and significant disruption. On June 3, a drone strike on Kuwait International Airport resulted in one Indian traveler losing his life and 63 people suffering injuries. Despite Kuwait suffering some of the heaviest hits among Gulf states throughout the months of fighting, the attribution of individual strikes continues to create intense debates between military analysts and regional authorities.

Analyst Debates Surrounding Launch Locations and Potential Suspects

Security experts quoted by Al Jazeera offered differing views on who executed the strike. Hassan Ahmadian, a professor of Middle East studies at the University of Tehran, noted that Iran regularly claims its military actions. Ahmadian suggested that if Iranian forces had intended to strike assets linked to Western defence contractors, they would have publicly claimed responsibility as they have done with strikes on American facilities. He argued that third parties, including Israel, might seek to complicate regional diplomacy by targeting infrastructure in Kuwait.

Ahmadian also noted that Iranian officials suspect possible involvement by other actors seeking to increase their strategic alignment with Washington. He pointed to previous discussions in March when Ukraine offered anti-drone expertise to the United States as American interceptor stockpiles faced heavy demand from mass-produced Shahed drones. However, other political experts dismissed suggestions of Western or allied involvement. Paolo von Schirach, president of the Global Policy Institute in Washington, stated that it would be illogical for the United States to coordinate an attack on an allied partner like Kuwait. Von Schirach observed that Italy has refrained from joining the active war effort against Iran, making theories of coordinated provocations unreasonable.

Muhanad Seloom, a senior fellow at the Middle East Council on Global Affairs, emphasized the need to differentiate between Iranian-built weaponry and strikes directly ordered or launched by Tehran. Seloom explained that while the drones were identified as Iranian models, such technology is also deployed by regional armed factions in Iraq. He suggested that an Iraqi armed group represents a primary alternative suspect, given past claims by such groups regarding strikes on Gulf bases hosting foreign forces. Seloom noted that Yemen's Houthis represent a less likely alternative, while claims regarding direct Western or Ukrainian involvement lack physical evidence. He stressed that only radar data, missile debris, and launch point tracking can definitively resolve the issue.

Impact on Overseas Workers and Broader Regional Security

The injury of a Pakistani worker in the Kuwait strike highlights the direct risks facing foreign workers stationed across the Arabian Peninsula. Millions of South Asian expatriates, including hundreds of thousands of Pakistani citizens, reside and work in Kuwait and neighboring Gulf countries. Threats to critical infrastructure such as air defence sites, industrial plants, and transport centers create ongoing security concerns for foreign labor forces serving in essential operational and technical roles.

Analysts observe that the incident could lead to expanded air defence precautions across the Gulf. If attacks on radar stations and military sites persist, host governments may increase defensive alert levels around key civilian and military installations. The involvement of European defence firms in maintaining regional air defence systems could also prompt European governments to review security protocols for technical personnel working in active conflict zones.

Observer attention will now focus on whether Kuwait or international investigators publish radar tracking logs and hardware debris recovered from the strike site. Diplomatic exchanges between Rome, Kuwait City, and Tehran are expected to continue as regional governments assess the findings of ongoing security evaluations. The resolution of attribution for the attack will remain a key factor in determining whether European states alter their diplomatic posture regarding the ongoing Middle East conflict.
