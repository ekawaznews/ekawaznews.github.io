Pakistan Opens Public Access to Treasury Bills and Bonds

By Umer Javed | 30 September 2026 | 4 min read
Photo: Walls.io / Pexels

The government announced that citizens can now trade treasury bills and bonds on the stock market, a move aimed at boosting IMF compliance and expanding the local currency bond market.

The government on Tuesday announced that the general public would be allowed to trade government securities, including treasury bills and bonds, through the stock market as part of efforts to improve compliance under the International Monetary Fund (IMF) programme currently being reviewed by a visiting staff mission for the disbursement of around $1.2 billion.

IMF Programme Context

Pakistan’s current IMF programme is designed to address fiscal deficits and strengthen macroeconomic stability. The visiting staff mission, led by Iva Petro, is assessing the country’s progress and determining the timing of the next disbursement. The release of $1.2 billion is contingent on the government meeting specific reform targets and improving transparency in public debt management.

Local Currency Bond Market Development

The local currency bond market (LCBM) has long been identified as a tool to diversify funding sources and reduce reliance on external borrowing. Prior attempts to broaden participation in the LCBM have faced limited uptake due to regulatory constraints and a lack of market infrastructure tailored to retail investors.

Public Access Initiative

Under the new plan, retail investors will be able to purchase treasury bills and bonds through the existing stock market platform. This step removes a major barrier that previously restricted such transactions to institutional buyers. The government stated that the move will align with IMF expectations for broader market participation and increased transparency.

Compliance and Market Confidence

Expanding public access is intended to demonstrate Pakistan’s commitment to sound debt management practices. By opening the market to ordinary citizens, the authorities aim to create a more inclusive financial that supports the IMF’s focus on sustainable fiscal reforms.

Kick‑Off Meeting Highlights

Finance Minister Muhammad Aurangzeb met with the IMF staff mission in a customary kick‑off session that set the agenda for the LCBM strategy. The meeting confirmed that the plan will be implemented in phases, with initial roll‑out targeting high‑yield treasury instruments before expanding to longer‑dated bonds.

Strategic Action Plan Details

The action plan outlines steps to upgrade market infrastructure, including the integration of electronic trading systems and the introduction of standardized bond denominations suitable for retail investors. It also calls for the development of educational programs to inform the public about the risks and benefits associated with fixed‑income securities.

Potential Market Impact

Opening the market to the general public is expected to increase liquidity and broaden the investor base. A larger pool of buyers could reduce price volatility and lower borrowing costs for the government. The plan also aims to create a more resilient debt market that can better absorb external shocks.

Investor Benefits

Retail investors gain access to a new asset class that offers predictable returns and lower risk compared to equities. The inclusion of treasury bills and bonds in the stock market allows for easier portfolio diversification and improved asset allocation for individuals seeking stable income streams.

Challenges Ahead

Implementing the plan will require significant regulatory adjustments, including amendments to securities laws and the establishment of new compliance frameworks. There is also a need to ensure that market participants are adequately protected from potential fraud and market manipulation.

Regulatory Adjustments

The Securities and Exchange Commission will review existing rules to facilitate the trading of government securities by retail investors. This review will cover licensing requirements for brokers, disclosure obligations, and the creation of a dedicated trading segment for treasury instruments.

Timeline for Roll‑Out

The government intends to launch the pilot phase within the next quarter, with a full roll‑out scheduled for the following fiscal year. The IMF staff mission will monitor progress and provide feedback to ensure that the initiative aligns with the broader programme objectives.

Next Steps

Following the announcement, the Ministry of Finance will issue detailed guidelines for market participants. The plan will be subject to periodic review by the IMF staff mission, and adjustments will be made based on market performance and investor response.

Looking Ahead

By broadening access to treasury securities, Pakistan aims to strengthen its financial markets and improve its standing with international lenders. The initiative reflects a broader strategy to deepen domestic capital markets and foster a culture of investment among ordinary citizens.
