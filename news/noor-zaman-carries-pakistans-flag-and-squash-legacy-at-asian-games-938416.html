Noor Zaman Carries Pakistan's Flag and Squash Legacy at Asian Games

By Umer Javed | 18 September 2026 | 5 min read
Photo: Yassir Abbas / Pexels

Noor Zaman will carry Pakistan's flag at the Asian Games opening ceremony in Nagoya, symbolizing national pride. The young squash player also bears the hopes of reviving Pakistan's glorious past in the sport.

Noor Zaman will walk into the opening ceremony of the Asian Games in Nagoya on Saturday, carrying Pakistan’s flag, a moment of immense national pride and symbolism for the 22-year-old from Peshawar. This responsibility extends beyond merely representing his country in the parade; he also carries the significant weight of a sport's past glory, a memory of a time when Pakistan stood at the pinnacle of international squash.

His presence at the Games is the aspirations of a nation that cherishes its sporting heroes and looks to a new generation to reclaim former triumphs. For a young athlete, this dual role as flag bearer and a standard-bearer for a sport’s revival is a profound honour and a considerable challenge.

Noor Zaman has articulated his clear ambition regarding his mission. “I want to bring Pakistan on top in squash, make my country champions again,” Noor told Dawn on Thursday. He added, “And I am working towards that goal, doing my best.” These words encapsulate the determination required to confront the high expectations associated with Pakistan’s squash heritage.

This declaration is not just a personal aspiration; it is a direct echo of a mission that once defined an entire era for Pakistani sports. That mission belonged to legendary figures like Jahangir Khan and Jansher Khan, two names that became synonymous with dominance in global squash for nearly two decades. Their era was a golden age, a period when Pakistan’s name was uttered with reverence in every squash court around the world.

The Shadow of Giants: Jahangir and Jansher Khan

The legacy of Jahangir Khan and Jansher Khan remains unparalleled in the annals of squash history. Jahangir Khan, with his record ten British Open titles and an unbroken winning streak of 555 matches, set a benchmark of excellence that many consider unbreakable. His sheer athleticism, mental fortitude, and relentless pursuit of victory captivated audiences globally and inspired a generation of Pakistani youth.

Following Jahangir’s reign, Jansher Khan emerged to continue Pakistan’s dominance, securing eight World Open titles and six British Open titles. His rivalry with Jahangir, though brief, and his subsequent control over the sport solidified Pakistan's position as the undisputed powerhouse in men's squash. These two individuals did not just win tournaments; they established a culture of winning and set an incredibly high standard for future generations.

For Noor Zaman, born into a time after the peak of this golden era, the stories of Jahangir and Jansher Khan are part of his sporting folklore. He is old enough to have heard the tales of their incredible feats, the sheer scale of their achievements, and the pride they brought to Pakistan. While he did not witness their reign firsthand, the echoes of their dominance resonate strongly, shaping the context in which he now competes.

The task of following such giants is immense. The memory of that past glory acts as both an inspiration and a heavy expectation. It reminds current players of what is possible, yet also highlights the distance that current Pakistani squash must cover to return to that exalted status. This historical context is a constant companion for any Pakistani squash player aspiring for international success.

The Asian Games: A Platform for Revival

The Asian Games represent a significant regional multi-sport event, offering a prime stage for athletes to showcase their talent and for nations to assert their sporting prowess. For Pakistan, participation in such events is always met with enthusiasm and hope, particularly in sports where the nation has a storied past.

Noor Zaman's role as flag bearer at these Games elevates his participation beyond individual competition. It transforms him into a symbol of Pakistan's sporting ambitions and its enduring spirit. The opening ceremony is a moment when all eyes are on the representatives of each nation, and Noor will be at the forefront, carrying the national flag with honour.

His performance in the squash competitions will be watched with keen interest, not just for individual medals but for signs of a broader resurgence in Pakistani squash. Success at the Asian Games, even if not replicating the global dominance of the past, can provide a much-needed morale boost and a foundation for future development.

The Asian Games offer an opportunity for young athletes like Noor to gain invaluable international experience, test their skills against top regional competitors, and make their mark on the continental stage. Such exposure is vital for development, helping to bridge the gap between national and international standards.

Challenges and the Path Ahead for Pakistani Squash

The decline from the peak of squash dominance in Pakistan is a complex issue, often attributed to various factors including a lack of sustained investment, inadequate infrastructure development, and a perceived reduction in the depth of the talent pool. While the raw talent has always been present in Pakistan, the structured support systems required to consistently produce world-beaters have faced challenges.

The professional of squash has also evolved significantly since the days of Jahangir and Jansher. The sport has become more globalized, with talent emerging from various countries, and the level of competition has intensified. Modern training methodologies, sports science, and consistent international exposure are now for athletes aiming for the top ranks.

For Pakistan to reclaim its position, even partially, a concerted effort is required from sports federations, government bodies, and private sector patrons. This includes investing in grassroots development programs, establishing modern training academies, ensuring access to experienced coaches, and providing financial support for promising young players to compete internationally.
