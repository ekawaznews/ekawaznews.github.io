JI Chief Announces Islamabad March on Sept 20 Against Petroleum Levy

By Umer Javed | 15 September 2026 | 4 min read
Photo: Ann H / Pexels

JI chief has called for a protest in Islamabad on September 20 to oppose the federal petroleum levy, raising concerns about security and economic impact.

JI chief announced a march in Islamabad on September 20 to protest the petroleum levy.

Background on the Petroleum Levy

The federal government introduced a 1 per cent levy on petroleum products in 2023 as part of a broader fiscal strategy to increase revenue and reduce fiscal deficits. The levy applies to all fuels sold in the country, including petrol, diesel, and natural gas. Critics argue that the additional cost will exacerbate inflation and place a heavier burden on consumers, particularly those in low‑income brackets.

Opposition parties have repeatedly called for the repeal of the levy, citing its impact on transportation costs and the overall price level. The government maintains that the levy will help finance essential public services and infrastructure projects, but the policy remains controversial across the political spectrum.

JI's History of Protests

Jamaat-ud-Dawa (JI) is a banned organization in Pakistan, designated as a terrorist group by the state. Despite its status, the group continues to organize protests and rallies, often on issues related to state policies that it views as unjust. In the past, JI has staged demonstrations against foreign intervention, government corruption, and perceived infringements on religious freedoms.

Previous protests organized by JI have drawn large crowds, sometimes resulting in clashes with security forces. The group's ability to mobilise supporters remains a concern for law enforcement agencies, who monitor its activities closely.

Government Response and Security Measures

The announcement of the planned march has prompted the Islamabad Metropolitan Police to review security protocols for the capital. According to reports, the police will deploy additional personnel at key intersections and main roads that are expected to be used by the protestors. The federal government has stated that it will ensure public safety while respecting the right to peaceful assembly.

Law enforcement officials have also warned that any attempt to disrupt traffic or damage property will be met with legal action. The government has reiterated its commitment to maintaining order and preventing any escalation that could affect the city's infrastructure.

Potential Impact on Islamabad

The march is scheduled to begin at 10 a.m. from the National Press Club and move towards the Parliament House. If the protest proceeds as planned, it could cause significant disruptions to traffic flow, affecting commuters and businesses in the vicinity. Several local vendors and transport operators have expressed concerns about potential losses due to road closures.

The protest could also influence the daily routine of the city's residents, especially those who rely on public transport. Authorities are expected to coordinate with transportation authorities to manage the situation and minimize inconvenience.

Public Sentiment and Economic Concerns

Public opinion on the petroleum levy remains divided. While some segments of society support the measure as a necessary step to strengthen the national treasury, others view it as an additional tax that will strain household budgets. The upcoming protest may amplify these divisions, as citizens rally either in support of or against the policy.

Economic analysts warn that large‑scale demonstrations could affect investor confidence and the overall business climate in Islamabad. However, they also note that the government has mechanisms in place to address public grievances and mitigate economic fallout.

Legal Status of JI and Implications

The banned status of JI complicates the legal surrounding the protest. While the group claims to be exercising its constitutional right to assembly, the government maintains that its activities are prohibited. This tension could lead to legal challenges and potential arrests of protestors or organizers.

Lawyers and civil society representatives have called for a clear legal framework to handle the situation, emphasizing the importance of upholding the rule of law while ensuring public safety. The judiciary may be called upon to interpret the legality of the protest in light of the ban on JI.

Upcoming Dates and Next Steps

The protest is slated for September 20, a date that coincides with the annual celebration of Pakistan Day. The timing may be strategic, as the government will be on high alert due to the significance of the day. Security agencies are expected to coordinate with the Election Commission and other stakeholders to manage the event.

In the days leading up to the march, the government is likely to issue statements clarifying its position and outlining measures to prevent any disruption. JI is expected to release a manifesto detailing the objectives of the protest, though details remain sparse at present.

Conclusion

The planned march by JI on September 20 highlights the ongoing friction between the government and groups that oppose its economic policies. The protest highlights the broader debate over the petroleum levy and its impact on Pakistan’s economy and society. As Islamabad prepares for the event, authorities will balance the enforcement of law with the maintenance of public order, while the public watches closely to see how the situation unfolds.
