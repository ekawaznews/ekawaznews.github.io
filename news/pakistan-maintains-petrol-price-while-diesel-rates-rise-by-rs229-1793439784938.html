Pakistan Maintains Petrol Price While Diesel Rates Rise by Rs2.29

Latest Petroleum Adjustment in Pakistan

The federal authorities in Islamabad have issued an official notification announcing a revised pricing structure for petroleum products across the nation. Based on reports published by ARY News and Geo News, the government has chosen to keep the price of motor gasoline, commonly known as petrol, unchanged for the current pricing cycle. However, the official notification indicates an upward revision in the rate of high-speed diesel, reflecting a modest increase aimed at balancing current supply arrangements and official pricing calculations.

This pricing adjustment comes into effect on October 31 and is scheduled to remain applicable through November 2. According to ARY News, the government designated a three-day window for these specific rates. The decision directly impacts commercial transport, logistics companies, personal commuters, and agricultural operators who rely heavily on fuel for daily operations. Observers note that short-term price notifications reflect ongoing monitoring by the Oil and Gas Regulatory Authority alongside the Ministry of Energy.

Reporting from Dawn highlights that official ex-depot pricing adjustments are coordinated through notifications released by the regulatory body and energy ministry. By maintaining motor gasoline prices while increasing high-speed diesel, the official notification establishes a clear distinction between consumer transportation fuels and industrial or commercial transit fuels. Citizens and commercial sector operators across Pakistan are monitoring these changes closely to assess potential immediate effects on transport fares and commodities.

Public transport operators, freight movers, and individual consumers often react swiftly to shifts in government fuel pricing notifications. Even small adjustments in petroleum rates can ripple through various sectors of the economy, affecting everyday expenditure and freight logistics. The government routinely reviews these figures based on regulatory recommendations, domestic inventory levels, and international energy market movements.

Breakdown of New Fuel Rates

Under the newly announced rate structure detailed by ARY News, the selling price for petrol remains fixed at Rs377.91 per litre. Motorists and commercial drivers utilizing petrol-powered vehicles will experience no change at the pump during this three-day period. The maintenance of this rate provides temporary stability for urban commuters, small business owners, and private vehicle owners who rely on motor gasoline for daily travel.

In contrast, the price of high-speed diesel has been raised by Rs2.29 per litre. As reported by Geo News and Daily Ausaf, this increase brings the new high-speed diesel rate to Rs402.96 per litre. Diesel is a central input for heavy machinery, freight trucks, intercity passenger buses, and agricultural water pumps across the country. Consequently, even an incremental upward shift of Rs2.29 per litre can influence operational expenditures for goods transporters and agricultural producers.

As noted by Dawn, these ex-depot prices serve as the benchmark figures determined by the Ministry of Energy and regulatory channels. Outlets including Geo News and Daily Ausaf confirmed that while motor gasoline stayed completely static, high-speed diesel was the sole category subject to an immediate increase in this administrative notification. The disparity between petrol and diesel prices highlights varying demand dynamics and cost structures associated with different refined products.

Transport sector representatives and logistics analysts often monitor these price notices to evaluate operational margins. A rise in diesel rates could lead freight managers to adjust transit charges for long-distance transport. Meanwhile, the unchanged petrol rate offers temporary predictability for passenger vehicles, two-wheelers, and lighter commercial vans operating within metropolitan areas.

National Production and Consumption Figures

Alongside the pricing notification, details regarding domestic fuel manufacturing and overall consumption were recently presented to lawmakers. According to ARY News, a written response submitted to the Senate outlined comprehensive production and sales metrics for petrol and high-speed diesel covering the first quarter of the fiscal period, from July to September 2026.

The written reply revealed that Pakistan maintained an average daily petrol production of 7,035 metric tonnes during the July to September 2026 period. However, national consumption far exceeded domestic refining capability. Total daily sales of petrol were recorded at 21,583 metric tonnes across the country during the same three-month span. This substantial difference between domestic output and market demand highlights the reliance on external supply chains to meet nationwide motor gasoline requirements.

For high-speed diesel, domestic refining facilities recorded an average daily production of 14,065 metric tonnes between July and September 2026. On the demand side, nationwide daily sales of high-speed diesel reached 16,866 metric tonnes over the same period, as detailed in the parliamentary submission cited by ARY News. Although the gap between production and sales for diesel is narrower than that of petrol, local output still falls short of total domestic consumption.

The data presented in the Senate indicates that domestic refineries process both locally extracted crude oil and imported crude oil to manufacture finished petroleum products. The reliance on imported crude oil means domestic supply stability and pricing formulas remain closely tied to international supply movements, refinery capacities, and trade logistics.

Provincial Production and Demand Disparities

The official data presented in the Senate provides a granular breakdown of how production and consumption vary significantly across different provinces. According to figures reported by ARY News, Punjab recorded the highest volume of daily petrol production, averaging 4,670 metric tonnes between July and September 2026. Despite this substantial output, daily petrol sales in Punjab reached 14,827 metric tonnes, reflecting the province's dense population and high volume of vehicular traffic.

Regarding high-speed diesel, Punjab reported a daily average production of 7,608 metric tonnes during the same quarter. Sales of high-speed diesel in Punjab averaged 10,779 metric tonnes per day. These statistics indicate that Punjab relies on inter-provincial fuel transfers or imported refined products to fulfill its heavy agricultural and industrial diesel consumption demands.

In Sindh, daily petrol production reached 1,592 metric tonnes while daily sales were recorded at 4,567 metric tonnes, according to the Senate reply cited by ARY News. In terms of high-speed diesel, Sindh produced a daily average of 4,419 metric tonnes, against daily provincial sales of 3,662 metric tonnes. Sindh's diesel figures show a localized production surplus relative to its immediate provincial sales volume during this specific quarter.

The Senate submission also outlined figures for Khyber Pakhtunkhwa and Balochistan. In Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, daily petrol sales averaged 2,127 metric tonnes, while daily high-speed diesel sales stood at 2,355 metric tonnes. In Balochistan, daily petrol production was recorded at 773 metric tonnes, compared to daily sales of 62 metric tonnes. Daily diesel production in Balochistan reached 2,038 metric tonnes against daily sales of 70 metric tonnes, reflecting localized refining facilities paired with lower recorded provincial distribution sales.

Economic Impact and Operational Dynamics

The interaction between localized refining output and national sales volumes shapes Pakistan's energy logistics. Refineries operating across the provinces process a blend of domestic crude oil reserves and imported crude shipments to generate motor gasoline and high-speed diesel. When local production lags behind demand, fuel marketing companies must rely on imported finished products or redistribute stock across provincial borders to prevent shortages.

Changes in fuel pricing notifications directly touch key economic sectors. High-speed diesel powers much of the country's public transit, heavy goods haulage, and agricultural machinery such as tractors and irrigation pumps. An upward shift in diesel costs could lead to minor adjustments in transport freight charges. Transporters may pass on added fuel expenses to shippers, which could eventually influence retail prices for everyday consumer goods and food commodities.

Analysts may point out that stable petrol prices offer temporary relief to urban households reliant on personal transport, two-wheelers, and ride-hailing services. Maintaining petrol at Rs377.91 per litre keeps routine commuter costs steady for the immediate three-day period. However, market observers note that price adjustments over short intervals require commercial operators to remain agile in managing their operating expenses.

Agricultural producers and industrial units that depend on diesel generators or heavy machinery may also re-evaluate operational budgets following the rise to Rs402.96 per litre. While the Rs2.29 per litre increase is relatively small, cumulative operational costs over extended periods represent a factor for large-scale operations and supply chain management.

Factors to Observe in Coming Days

As the current notification covers the period from October 31 to November 2, industry stakeholders and consumers are focusing on what policy decisions may follow. The short three-day applicability window specified in the notification means that officials will evaluate fuel rates again in the near future. Observers will watch closely to see whether future notifications maintain this selective pricing approach or revise rates across all refined categories.

Future pricing determinations are expected to depend heavily on international crude oil price trends, local refining throughput, and currency exchange rates. Regulatory authorities like the Oil and Gas Regulatory Authority and the Ministry of Energy regularly assess these variables before issuing updated price schedules. Any movement in global oil markets could influence whether domestic rates remain unchanged, increase, or decrease in subsequent announcements.

Separately, lawmakers and policy experts will likely continue analyzing the production and consumption metrics submitted to the Senate. Addressing the gap between domestic refinery output and high national fuel demand remains a key subject for long-term energy planning. Increasing domestic refining capacity or optimizing crude imports could serve as strategies to meet growing transportation and industrial needs.

For now, market participants across Pakistan will adapt to the revised rates of Rs377.91 per litre for petrol and Rs402.96 per litre for high-speed diesel. As November 2 approaches, fuel distribution networks, public transport associations, and consumers will remain attentive to upcoming regulatory announcements from the federal government.
