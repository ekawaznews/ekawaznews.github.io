High-Level Pakistani Delegation in Riyadh for Makkah Defence Pact Meeting

By Umer Javed | 5 October 2026 | 7 min read
Photo: ِAMR-MANSOUR / Pexels

A meeting of the Makkah Joint Defence Agreement's Strategic Political and Defence Committee is being held today in Riyadh. Pakistan's delegation includes Deputy Prime Minister Ishaq Dar, Chief of Army Staff Field Marshal Asim Munir, and Defence Minister Khawaja Asif.

A meeting of the Makkah Joint Defence Agreement's Strategic Political and Defence Committee (SPDC) is being held on Monday in Riyadh, as confirmed by the Foreign Office (FO). This significant gathering will focus on regional developments and ongoing cooperation among the member states, addressing shared security interests and strategic challenges facing the region.

The Foreign Office, in a statement released on the social media platform X, detailed the composition of Pakistan’s high-level delegation participating in the meeting. The delegation is led by Deputy Prime Minister and Foreign Minister Ishaq Dar, signifying the diplomatic and political importance Pakistan attaches to this regional security framework. He is joined by Chief of Defence Forces and Chief of Army Staff Field Marshal Asim Munir, highlighting the military dimension of Pakistan's engagement. Defence Minister Khawaja Asif is also a key member, ensuring representation across the full spectrum of defence and strategic policy.

The presence of such a senior and comprehensive delegation highlights Pakistan's commitment to regional stability and its active role in collective defence initiatives. The meeting is also being attended by the respective Ministers of Foreign Affairs and other high-ranking officials from the member states, indicating a concerted effort towards coordinated policy and action.

Understanding the Makkah Joint Defence Agreement

The Makkah Joint Defence Agreement represents a significant framework for cooperation among its member states, primarily aimed at fostering regional security and stability. While specific details of its charter are often discussed among member nations, its overarching objective is to establish a collective defence mechanism and promote strategic alignment on matters of shared concern. This agreement serves as a platform for dialogue, coordination, and collaborative action in response to various security threats, ranging from conventional military challenges to non-traditional security issues such as terrorism and extremism.

Such defence pacts are typically designed to enhance interoperability among the armed forces of member countries, facilitate intelligence sharing, and coordinate defence policies. The goal is to create a unified front against potential aggressors and to strengthen the collective capacity of the region to manage and mitigate security risks. For participating nations, these agreements provide a structured avenue for joint military exercises, capacity-building programmes, and the exchange of strategic insights, all contributing to a more secure and stable regional environment.

The establishment of the Makkah Joint Defence Agreement reflects a growing recognition among regional powers of the need for collaborative security frameworks in an increasingly complex geopolitical. It signifies a move towards greater self-reliance in defence and a shared responsibility for maintaining peace within their geographical sphere. These agreements often evolve over time, adapting their focus and mechanisms to address emerging threats and changing strategic priorities, ensuring their continued relevance and effectiveness.

Pakistan's Strategic Engagement

Pakistan's active participation in the Makkah Joint Defence Agreement is its long-standing foreign policy emphasis on regional cooperation and its commitment to the security of its allies. As a nation with significant military capabilities and extensive experience in counter-terrorism operations, Pakistan brings considerable strategic value to such defence pacts. Its armed forces are widely regarded for their professionalism and operational readiness, making Pakistan a valuable partner in any collective defence endeavour.

The inclusion of Deputy Prime Minister and Foreign Minister Ishaq Dar in the delegation highlights the diplomatic gravitas Pakistan attaches to this meeting. His presence ensures that Pakistan's foreign policy objectives and strategic vision are articulated at the highest diplomatic level, allowing for effective coordination with other foreign ministers on regional issues. This integrated approach, combining diplomatic engagement with defence planning, is characteristic of Pakistan's comprehensive strategy for national and regional security.

also, the participation of Chief of Army Staff Field Marshal Asim Munir and Defence Minister Khawaja Asif signifies the unified and comprehensive nature of Pakistan's representation. Field Marshal Munir's involvement provides important military insights and ensures that any defence-related discussions are grounded in practical operational considerations. Defence Minister Asif’s presence, overseeing the broader defence policy, reinforces the government’s commitment to the pact and ensures that decisions made at the meeting align with Pakistan’s national defence strategy. This high-level attendance from both the diplomatic and military spheres projects a strong image of national resolve and collective leadership.

Pakistan's historical ties with many of the member states, particularly Saudi Arabia, form a strong foundation for its engagement in such defence agreements. These relationships are often built on shared cultural values, economic partnerships, and a common vision for regional peace and prosperity. Participation in the Makkah Joint Defence Agreement allows Pakistan to reinforce these bilateral relationships within a multilateral framework, strengthening overall regional cohesion.

Regional Dynamics and Cooperation Agenda

The meeting's focus on "regional developments" suggests a broad agenda that will likely encompass a range of security and political issues impacting the member states. The Middle East and South Asia regions have witnessed considerable geopolitical shifts and security challenges in recent years, including persistent threats from extremist groups, regional conflicts, and evolving power dynamics. Discussions at the SPDC meeting are expected to address how these developments affect the collective security interests of the member nations and what coordinated responses might be necessary.

Ongoing cooperation among member states is a central theme of the meeting, indicating a continuous effort to enhance collaboration across various domains. This cooperation typically includes intelligence sharing, which is vital for anticipating and countering threats effectively. Joint military exercises are another common form of collaboration, designed to improve interoperability, refine tactical procedures, and build mutual trust among participating forces. Capacity-building initiatives, where member states share expertise and resources to strengthen each other's defence capabilities, also form a significant part of this ongoing cooperation.

The Strategic Political and Defence Committee provides a forum for member states to harmonise their foreign and defence policies concerning regional issues. This alignment is essential for presenting a united front on international platforms and for ensuring that individual national actions contribute to the broader collective security objectives. By discussing and agreeing upon common positions, the member states can amplify their influence and more effectively address external pressures or internal destabilising factors.

The deliberations are expected to cover strategies for counter-terrorism, border security, maritime security, and potentially cyber defence, reflecting the diverse nature of contemporary threats. Effective cooperation in these areas requires a clear understanding of each member state's capacities and vulnerabilities, fostering an environment of mutual support and shared responsibility. The meeting therefore serves as a critical mechanism for reviewing existing cooperative frameworks and exploring new avenues for collaboration to adapt to the changing security.

Historical Ties and Future Prospects

The Makkah Joint Defence Agreement is deeply rooted in the historical and strategic ties that bind the participating nations. For Pakistan, its relationship with Saudi Arabia, a key player in the agreement, has been particularly strong and enduring, marked by extensive cooperation in defence, economy, and culture. This historical context provides a stable foundation for ongoing defence cooperation, ensuring that strategic partnerships are built on trust and mutual understanding. Such long-standing relationships are often reinforced through formal agreements like the Makkah Pact, which institutionalise security cooperation and provide a structured platform for continued engagement.

Looking ahead, the outcomes of the SPDC meeting are anticipated to reinforce existing cooperation mechanisms and identify new areas for collaboration. The continuous dialogue among high-level diplomatic and military officials is essential for adapting to emerging threats and ensuring the pact remains relevant and effective. Potential future initiatives could include enhanced joint training programmes, greater integration of defence technologies, and more frequent strategic consultations to address evolving regional dynamics. The aim is to build a resilient and responsive collective defence posture that can effectively deter aggression and maintain peace.

The Makkah Joint Defence Agreement represents a commitment to collective security and regional stability, fostering an environment where member states can pursue their national development objectives without undue external interference. Pakistan's active role in this framework reflects its dedication to promoting peace and security not only within its borders but also across the wider region. The Riyadh meeting is thus a critical step in the ongoing effort to strengthen these vital partnerships and ensure a coordinated approach to the complex security challenges that lie ahead, contributing to the broader stability of the region.
