LHC Moved Against PMDC's Repatriation Directives for Afghan Students

U
Umer Javed
Staff Reporter
| 🕒 11 September 2026 at 07:42 pm | 📖 6 min read
Share: 💬 WhatsApp 📘 Facebook ✈️ Telegram 🐦 Twitter
📷 Photo: https://kaboompics.com/ / Pexels | ekawaznews.github.io

The Lahore High Court (LHC) has been moved against directives issued by the Pakistan Medical and Dental Council (PMDC) concerning the repatriation of Afghan students. This development marks a significant legal challenge to an administrative decision by the country's apex regulatory body for medical and dental education, bringing the matter into judicial scrutiny.

The petition, filed before the High Court, seeks to contest the legality and implications of the PMDC's directives, which reportedly mandate the return of Afghan students currently enrolled in various medical and dental colleges across Pakistan. While the specific details of the directives have not been fully disclosed in public reports, the core issue revolves around the students' continued stay and study in Pakistan.

Understanding the Pakistan Medical and Dental Council's Role

The Pakistan Medical and Dental Council (PMDC) serves as the primary regulatory authority for medical and dental professions and education in Pakistan. Established under an act of parliament, its mandate includes setting and maintaining uniform standards of medical and dental education, registering medical and dental practitioners, and ensuring the quality of healthcare services throughout the country. The council is responsible for overseeing admissions criteria, curriculum development, examination standards, and the accreditation of medical and dental institutions.

The PMDC's directives carry significant weight within the medical education sector. Its decisions impact both educational institutions and students, dictating operational procedures for colleges and eligibility requirements for individuals aspiring to or currently pursuing medical and dental degrees. Any directive issued by the PMDC is intended to uphold the integrity and standards of medical education in Pakistan.

The council's authority extends to various aspects of student enrollment, including those pertaining to foreign nationals. It ensures that all students, irrespective of their nationality, meet the prescribed academic and regulatory benchmarks for studying medicine or dentistry in Pakistan. This oversight is considered essential for maintaining the quality and international recognition of Pakistani medical qualifications.

The Context of Afghan Students in Pakistan

Pakistan has historically hosted a large population of Afghan nationals, including a considerable number of students seeking educational opportunities. Many Afghan students have pursued higher education in Pakistan, including in professional fields such as medicine and dentistry, due to geographical proximity, cultural similarities, and the availability of educational institutions.

These students often enroll through various channels, including self-financing or under specific scholarship programmes. Their presence in Pakistani educational institutions has been a long-standing feature of the bilateral relationship, contributing to both the academic and the broader socio-economic fabric.

The decision to pursue medical or dental education is a significant commitment, involving years of rigorous study and substantial financial investment. For Afghan students, studying in Pakistan has often represented a pathway to professional careers and improved livelihoods, both within Pakistan and potentially upon their return to Afghanistan.

The directives for repatriation, therefore, introduce a layer of uncertainty and potential disruption for these students, raising concerns about the continuity of their education and their future prospects. The implications extend beyond individual students to their families and the institutions where they are enrolled.

Legal Grounds for the Challenge

The petition filed in the Lahore High Court is expected to challenge the PMDC's directives on several legal grounds. In Pakistan, administrative decisions made by government bodies and regulatory authorities are subject to judicial review by the High Courts. Petitioners can argue that a directive is ultra vires (beyond the powers) of the issuing authority, arbitrary, discriminatory, or in violation of fundamental rights.

Legal challenges often invoke principles of natural justice, which include the right to be heard (audi alteram partem) and the requirement for a reasoned decision. Petitioners may contend that the PMDC's directives were issued without adequate consultation, without providing affected students an opportunity to present their case, or without sufficient justification.

The concept of legitimate expectation could also be a basis for the petition. Students who have been admitted, are actively pursuing their studies, and have invested considerable resources might argue that they had a legitimate expectation to complete their degrees under the existing regulations. Any sudden change in policy that disrupts this expectation without compelling reasons could be challenged in court.

also, the petition may also raise humanitarian concerns, considering the current situation in Afghanistan. The legal arguments could highlight the potential hardships and risks that students might face if compelled to return to Afghanistan before completing their education, especially if safe and stable conditions for their continued studies or professional practice are not assured in their home country.

Implications for Students and Institutions

The directives for repatriation, if upheld, would have profound implications for the Afghan students currently enrolled in medical and dental programmes. Such a move would disrupt their academic careers, potentially forcing them to abandon their studies midway. This could lead to significant financial losses for the students and their families, who have invested heavily in their education.

The uncertainty surrounding their status can also have a severe psychological impact on the students, affecting their academic performance and overall well-being. The prospect of having to leave Pakistan before completing their degrees raises questions about the transferability of their credits, the recognition of their partial qualifications, and their ability to continue their education elsewhere.

For the medical and dental colleges, the directives could result in a sudden reduction in their student body, particularly affecting those institutions with a significant enrollment of foreign students. This might lead to financial strains, as tuition fees from international students often contribute substantially to institutional revenues. Colleges might also face administrative challenges in managing the departure of students and adjusting their academic planning.

The episode also highlights the need for clear, consistent, and well-communicated policies regarding foreign students by regulatory bodies. Sudden changes in policy can create instability and damage the reputation of Pakistani educational institutions on an international level, potentially deterring future foreign applicants.

The Role of the Lahore High Court

The Lahore High Court, as a superior court, plays a vital role in upholding the rule of law and safeguarding the rights of citizens and individuals within its jurisdiction. When moved against administrative actions, the LHC examines whether the executive or regulatory bodies have acted within their legal parameters and in accordance with established principles of justice and fairness.

The court's intervention in such matters ensures that administrative decisions are not arbitrary or capricious but are based on sound legal principles and due process. The judicial review process allows for a thorough examination of the facts, the legal framework, and the potential impact of the directives on the affected parties.

The outcome of this petition will be closely watched by the academic community, foreign students, and human rights advocates. The court's decision will not only determine the fate of the Afghan students but may also set important precedents for how regulatory bodies like the PMDC formulate and implement policies that affect foreign nationals studying in Pakistan. The proceedings are expected to clarify the extent of PMDC's authority in issuing such directives and the legal protections available to students.

As the legal process unfolds, the focus will remain on the arguments presented by both the petitioners and the PMDC. The High Court's ultimate judgment will provide clarity on the matter, ensuring that justice is served and the principles of fair administration are upheld in the country's higher education sector.
