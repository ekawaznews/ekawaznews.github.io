Weekly Inflation Drops 0.46% as Fuel Prices Decline

Umer Javed

Decline in National Weekly Inflation Rates

Official figures released on Friday indicate that weekly inflation across the country experienced a downward movement during the tracking period ending on November 12, 2026. According to details shared by the Pakistan Bureau of Statistics, the Sensitive Price Indicator for the combined consumption group dropped to 372.90 points. This represented a shift from the previous reading of 374.61 points recorded during the preceding week. The primary driver behind this relief in the overall index was a notable decrease in petroleum costs.

Despite the week-on-week reduction, a comparison with the corresponding period of the previous year reveals an upward trajectory in general price levels. On a year-on-year basis, weekly inflation registered an increase of 11.21 percent. This wider annual metric highlights ongoing economic pressures experienced by households over a longer duration, even as short-term weekly figures show some relief.

Varying Impact Across Income Brackets

The impact of weekly price movements was not uniform across different financial segments of the population. Data from the Pakistan Bureau of Statistics shows that the Sensitive Price Indicator for the lowest consumption group, which comprises individuals with a monthly income of up to Rs17,732, actually increased by 0.03 percent. This pushed the index for this vulnerable segment to 361.12 points compared to 361.00 points in the prior week.

Conversely, higher income brackets experienced relief during the same weekly cycle. Consumption groups with monthly earnings ranging from Rs17,733 to Rs22,888 saw a decline of 0.09 percent. Groups earning between Rs22,889 and Rs29,517 recorded a decrease of 0.17 percent. Segments with incomes from Rs29,518 to Rs44,175 noted a drop of 0.32 percent, while the highest income bracket with monthly earnings above Rs44,175 experienced the largest reduction at 0.68 percent.

Commodity Price Fluctuations During the Week

Market dynamics during the monitored week displayed a mixed pattern of increases and decreases across fifty-one essential commodities. Official reports indicate that prices for nineteen items, constituting 37.25 percent of the basket, moved upward. Meanwhile, nine items representing 17.65 percent of the monitored goods registered a decline in their market rates. Prices for the remaining twenty-three items, making up 45.10 percent of the total basket, remained stable throughout the week.

Fuel items led the downward trend on a week-on-week basis. Petrol prices recorded the largest single decrease at 7.42 percent, while diesel followed closely with a reduction of 6.10 percent. Additional decreases were observed in food and household items, including onions at 1.70 percent, bananas at 1.03 percent, gur at 0.59 percent, sugar at 0.43 percent, powdered milk at 0.36 percent, pulse moong at 0.32 percent, and pulse gram at 0.08 percent.

Notable Increases in Food and Household Goods

While fuel costs provided relief, several food items and essentials became more expensive during the week under review. Tomatoes recorded the steepest weekly price increase, surging by 12.06 percent. Eggs followed with a rise of 5.98 percent, and potatoes became 2.42 percent more expensive. Other commodities registering higher rates included garlic at 1.76 percent, chicken at 1.32 percent, firewood at 1.10 percent, wheat flour at 0.94 percent, pulse masoor at 0.64 percent, beef at 0.47 percent, and liquefied petroleum gas at 0.30 percent.

On an annual basis, the shifts were even more pronounced for certain goods. Liquefied petroleum gas recorded the highest year-on-year increase at 111.35 percent, followed by tomatoes at 78.94 percent, and first-quarter electricity charges at 52.38 percent. Other significant annual increases were noted in wheat flour at 36.14 percent, diesel at 30.77 percent, petrol at 26.23 percent, onions at 19.67 percent, powdered chillies at 15.55 percent, mutton at 14.67 percent, plain bread at 12.35 percent, beef at 11.30 percent, and chicken at 10.98 percent.

Fuel Price Reductions Across National Outlets

Recent policy adjustments regarding petroleum products have been a focal point of public attention. Geo News reported that the government reduced the price of petrol by Rs3 per litre and diesel by Rs4 per litre. Dawn noted official ex-depot prices through notifications from the Oil and Gas Regulatory Authority and the Ministry of Energy, confirming the downward adjustments for high-speed diesel and petrol. Daily Pakistan reported that the petrol price in the country dropped to Rs332, while diesel slipped to Rs360 for the period starting November 13, 2026.

These fuel price adjustments align with the broader national data showing petroleum as the primary catalyst for the recent dip in the weekly inflation index. ARY News noted that the overall weekly inflation decline was anchored by these reductions in petroleum prices as tracked by the Pakistan Bureau of Statistics. Observers might monitor whether subsequent weeks will sustain these trends or if seasonal factors will alter the pricing landscape for essential commodities.

Annual Price Reversals for Select Goods

Despite rising annual figures for energy and select food categories, certain commodities registered notable price decreases compared to the previous year. Potatoes recorded the largest annual price reduction, falling by 36.41 percent. Sugar prices dropped by 24.54 percent on a year-on-year basis, while eggs declined by 23.29 percent.

Additional annual decreases were recorded in powdered salt down by 12.51 percent, pulse masoor down by 7.21 percent, pulse moong down by 6.83 percent, gur down by 6.63 percent, and bananas down by 3.35 percent. These variations demonstrate the complex interplay of supply and demand factors affecting different sectors of the economy over a twelve-month period, providing mixed signals for household budgeting.

Future Outlook and What to Watch

As consumers adjust to the revised fuel tariffs and shifting costs of daily essentials, analysts may watch how upcoming weekly Sensitive Price Indicator releases reflect broader economic shifts. The disparity in impact between lower-income households and higher-income brackets highlights ongoing economic vulnerabilities. Observers will likely track future decisions by regulatory bodies regarding petroleum pricing and utility tariffs to gauge their potential effect on inflation metrics in the coming weeks.

Stakeholders across various sectors remain attentive to international market trends and local supply chain updates that could influence domestic fuel costs and food prices. Continued reporting from official statistical agencies will remain essential for understanding the trajectory of living costs and consumer purchasing power throughout the remainder of the year.
