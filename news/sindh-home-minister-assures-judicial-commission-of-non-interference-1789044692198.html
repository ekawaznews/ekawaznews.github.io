Sindh Home Minister Assures Judicial Commission of Non-Interference

U
Umer Javed
Political Reporter
| 🕒 10 September 2026 at 05:51 pm | 📖 5 min read
Share: 💬 WhatsApp 📘 Facebook ✈️ Telegram 🐦 Twitter
📷 Photo: TREEDEO.ST / Pexels | ekawaznews.github.io

Sindh Home Minister Ziaul Hassan Lanjar on Thursday assured the judicial commission probing possible negligence in the Mir Raza Ali murder case that the provincial government did not intend to “impede” its proceedings. The minister's statement came after the commission summoned him to clarify the government's position, following an interruption by the Sindh advocate general during a previous hearing.

On Tuesday, Sindh Advocate General (AG) Jawad Dero interrupted the commission's proceedings. His intervention occurred when he objected to the lawyer representing the victims’ family asking questions during police surgeon Dr Summaiya Syed’s testimony. This interruption led the commission to summon Minister Lanjar, seeking a clear understanding of the provincial government's stance on the commission's independent functioning.

The AG's specific objection centred on the right of the victims' family's counsel to cross-examine a witness integral to the investigation. Such an intervention from a senior government legal official during a judicial inquiry raises important questions about the autonomy of the commission and the perception of governmental influence over its operations, prompting the commission to seek formal clarification from the Home Minister.

The judicial commission was established to investigate the Mir Raza Ali murder case, specifically examining any potential negligence that may have occurred during the investigation or related processes. Cases involving allegations of negligence or misconduct often lead to the formation of such commissions, aiming to ensure transparency and accountability beyond standard police inquiries.

Understanding Judicial Commissions in Pakistan

Judicial commissions in Pakistan are typically formed to inquire into matters of public importance, often involving significant public interest, allegations of misconduct, or complex events requiring an impartial and thorough investigation. They are usually headed by a serving or retired judge of a High Court or the Supreme Court, granting them a degree of judicial authority and independence in their fact-finding mission.

The primary purpose of these commissions is to ascertain facts, identify responsible parties, and recommend measures to prevent future occurrences or improve existing systems. Their findings are critical in shaping public perception of justice and accountability, particularly in cases where there are doubts about the fairness or thoroughness of initial investigations. The integrity of such commissions relies heavily on their ability to operate without external pressure or interference.

The Role of the Advocate General

The Advocate General is a constitutional office, serving as the chief legal advisor to the provincial government. The AG represents the government in various legal matters, including court cases and inquiries where the provincial government's interests are involved. Their role is to ensure the government's legal position is presented and protected.

An intervention by the Advocate General in the proceedings of a judicial commission, particularly one probing potential governmental negligence, can be viewed from multiple perspectives. While the AG has a duty to represent the government, the manner and timing of such interventions are scrutinized for their potential impact on the commission's independence. Objections to the questioning by victims' counsel can raise concerns about impeding the search for truth and justice.

The delicate balance lies in the AG fulfilling their duty to the government while respecting the independent nature of a judicial inquiry. Any action perceived as an attempt to control or direct the commission's investigative process can undermine public confidence in the commission's ability to deliver impartial findings. This is why the commission found it necessary to summon the Home Minister for a definitive clarification of the government's intent.

Victims' Rights and Due Process

The right of victims' families to legal representation and to participate actively in proceedings is a fundamental aspect of due process and the pursuit of justice. Lawyers representing victims play a vital role in ensuring that all pertinent questions are asked, evidence is thoroughly examined, and their clients' perspectives are heard. This active participation enhances the transparency and credibility of the inquiry.

Any attempt to restrict a victim's family lawyer from questioning witnesses can be seen as an obstruction to justice and a denial of their right to a fair hearing. Such actions can deepen the sense of injustice among affected families and the wider public, fostering distrust in the legal and governmental systems. The ability of all parties, especially those directly impacted, to present their case without hindrance is essential for a just outcome.

Government's Stance and Future Implications

Home Minister Lanjar's assurance on Thursday is a significant development, as it publicly reaffirms the provincial government's commitment to allowing the judicial commission to proceed without obstruction. This statement is important for restoring confidence in the commission's independence and signaling that all parties involved are expected to cooperate fully with its mandate.

The clarification from a senior provincial minister is essential for the commission to continue its work effectively and without perceived external pressure. It provides a clear directive that the government respects the judicial process and will not tolerate actions that may be interpreted as an attempt to influence or impede the inquiry into the Mir Raza Ali murder case. This stance is critical for upholding the rule of law and ensuring accountability.

The government's explicit commitment to non-impediment is particularly important in the context of high-profile cases where public scrutiny is intense. It sets a precedent for how provincial authorities intend to interact with independent judicial inquiries, emphasizing transparency and cooperation. Such assurances are vital for maintaining public trust in the administration of justice and the government's dedication to fair governance.

The public, along with the family of Mir Raza Ali, will now expect the judicial commission to proceed with its investigation swiftly and impartially, free from any further interruptions. The Home Minister's statement places the responsibility firmly on all government functionaries to respect the commission's autonomy and facilitate its work towards delivering justice and accountability in the case. This commitment is a cornerstone of a functioning democratic system.

The integrity of the judicial process remains vital, and statements from high-ranking officials confirming respect for independent inquiries are essential. The Mir Raza Ali murder case commission's ability its findings without hindrance will be a test of the commitment articulated by the provincial government.
