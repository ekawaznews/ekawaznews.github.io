M-1 Motorway Reopens Amidst Political Tensions and PTI Long March Postponement

By Umer Javed | 28 September 2026 | 6 min read
Photo: Igor Passchier / Pexels

The M-1 Motorway connecting Islamabad and Peshawar has been fully reopened to all traffic, following an indefinite closure last week. This development comes as restoration efforts continue on other key routes, set against the backdrop of an anticipated political long march.

The M-1 Motorway, a vital artery linking Islamabad with Peshawar, has been fully reopened to all types of traffic in both directions, the National Highways & Motorway Police (NH&MP) confirmed on Monday. This reopening marks a significant restoration of connectivity after a period of indefinite closure that impacted major transport routes across the region.

The decision to reopen the Islamabad-Peshawar motorway provides much-needed relief to commuters, transporters, and businesses that rely heavily on this important corridor for daily movement and trade. Its closure had led to considerable disruption, forcing traffic onto alternative, often less efficient, routes and causing widespread inconvenience.

Last week, the M-1, along with sections of the M-14 (Islamabad-Dera Ismail Khan Motorway) and the Grand Trunk Road (N-5), was closed for an indefinite period. These preemptive measures were implemented by the authorities amidst heightened political tensions and in anticipation of a planned long march towards Islamabad by the Pakistan Tehreek-e-Insaf (PTI) political party.

The PTI's long march, a significant political demonstration, was initially scheduled for September 27. However, it was later postponed to October 4. The government's pushback, which included the closure of these major highways, was widely seen as an attempt to manage potential large-scale gatherings and maintain law and order in the capital and surrounding areas.

The closure of such extensive sections of the national highway network had far-reaching implications. For residents of Peshawar and its adjoining areas, access to the federal capital was severely restricted, affecting inter-city travel for work, education, and personal reasons. Similarly, commercial traffic, including the movement of essential goods and commodities, experienced significant delays and increased operational costs due to rerouting and extended travel times.

The M-1 Motorway, inaugurated in 2007, is a 155-kilometer-long six-lane controlled-access highway that serves as a primary link between Khyber Pakhtunkhwa province and Punjab. Its strategic importance extends beyond mere transportation, playing a critical role in the economic and social integration of the regions it connects. Any disruption to its operations invariably impacts a broad spectrum of daily life and commerce.

According to a Motorway Police spokesperson, while the M-1 is now fully operational, restoration work is still underway on other affected sections. Specifically, efforts are continuing on the M-14 motorway between Hakla and Kundal. The M-14, also known as the Hakla-D.I. Khan Motorway, provides a important link to southern Khyber Pakhtunkhwa and parts of Balochistan, and its full restoration remains a priority.

also, restoration work is also in progress on sections of the historic GT Road (N-5). The N-5 is Pakistan's longest and most important national highway, stretching from Karachi to Torkham, and its partial closure had a cascading effect on local and inter-provincial traffic, particularly for those unable or unwilling to use the motorways.

The National Highways & Motorway Police, responsible for enforcing traffic laws and ensuring safe passage on Pakistan's motorways and national highways, played a central role during both the closure and the subsequent reopening phases. Their personnel were deployed extensively to manage traffic flow, guide commuters, and facilitate the restoration efforts. The spokesperson's announcement highlights the agency's commitment to restoring normalcy on the country's road network.

The government's decision to close major roads ahead of significant political events is a measure often employed in Pakistan to preempt potential unrest or to control the movement of large protesting crowds towards sensitive areas, particularly the capital. While intended to maintain public order, such actions frequently lead to considerable public inconvenience and economic disruption, drawing mixed reactions from various segments of society.

The indefinite nature of the closure last week created uncertainty among the public and business community. Many transporters expressed concerns over the financial losses incurred due to stalled logistics and perishable goods being delayed. The reopening of the M-1, therefore, is being welcomed as a step towards alleviating these pressures and allowing economic activity to resume its normal pace.

The political remains charged, with the PTI's postponed long march still on the horizon for October 4. The reopening of the M-1 could be interpreted in several ways: perhaps as a temporary easing of restrictions, or an adjustment in strategy by the authorities as they prepare for the rescheduled protest. The coming days are expected to reveal more about the government's approach to managing the upcoming political demonstration.

For the average citizen, the ability to travel freely on major national arteries like the M-1 is fundamental to daily life. The period of closure served as a stark reminder of how deeply political developments can impact basic freedoms of movement and commerce. With the M-1 now open, and ongoing work on M-14 and N-5, the focus shifts to the full restoration of the entire network and the unfolding political developments that continue to shape the national discourse.

Impact on Commuters and Commerce

The closure of the M-1 and other main routes had a substantial impact on both daily commuters and commercial activities. Businesses relying on timely delivery of goods, such as fresh produce, industrial supplies, and consumer products, faced considerable challenges. Supply chains were disrupted, leading to potential shortages in some areas and increased costs due to longer transit times and higher fuel consumption on alternative routes.

Individuals traveling for work, medical appointments, or family visits between Islamabad, Rawalpindi, Peshawar, and other cities found their plans severely hampered. Many were forced to cancel or reschedule journeys, while others endured significantly extended travel durations. The psychological impact of uncertainty surrounding road access also contributed to a sense of unease among the populace.

Role of Motorway Police in Restoration

The NH&MP played a important role throughout the period of closure and during the subsequent reopening. Beyond their primary duties of traffic enforcement, their personnel were instrumental in coordinating the logistics of reopening the motorway, ensuring that road surfaces were clear and safe for travel. Their public announcements and guidance were vital for keeping the public informed about the status of the roads.

The spokesperson for the Motorway Police emphasized the diligent efforts of their teams in facilitating the reopening and managing the flow of traffic once the restrictions were lifted. This swift action was essential to minimize further economic losses and restore public confidence in the accessibility of national infrastructure.

Broader Political Context

The decision to close major roads is a reflection of the intense political climate currently prevailing in Pakistan. The PTI's long march represents a significant challenge to the incumbent government, aiming to mobilize public support and exert pressure on key political issues. Such demonstrations often lead to a standoff between the protesting party and state authorities, with measures like road closures becoming a part of the broader strategy to manage or contain the protest movement.

As the new date for the long march approaches, the reopening of the M-1 may also signal a tactical shift, potentially allowing authorities to focus resources on specific entry points to Islamabad, rather than maintaining widespread road blockades. The coming days will be critical in observing how both the protesting party and the government navigate this evolving political situation, with the nation's infrastructure once again playing a key role in the unfolding events.
