FO Confirms Makkah Defence Pact Defensive, Expansion Not Planned

By Umer Javed | 10 September 2026 | 6 min read
Photo: ِAMR-MANSOUR / Pexels

The Foreign Office on Thursday clarified that the Makkah Defence Alliance, involving Pakistan, Saudi Arabia, and Turkiye, is solely defensive. Spokesperson Sajjad Haider Khan stated that expansion of the pact is currently not under consideration.

The Foreign Office (FO) announced on Thursday that the Makkah Defence Alliance, a pact involving Pakistan, Saudi Arabia, and Turkiye, is fundamentally “defensive” in nature, clarifying that any expansion of the alliance is currently “not on the cards.” This statement aims to provide clarity regarding the objectives and future trajectory of the trilateral arrangement.

FO spokesperson Sajjad Haider Khan made these remarks while addressing a weekly press briefing in Islamabad. He elaborated that the alliance is primarily conceived as a “defensive alliance to ensure regional security primarily through deterrence.” This articulation highlights a strategic intent focused on stability and the prevention of aggression rather than any offensive posturing.

Nature of the Makkah Defence Alliance

The spokesperson’s comments on the character of the Makkah Defence Alliance, positioning it as a mechanism for collective security among its member states. A defensive alliance, in international relations, typically commits its signatories to mutual defence in the event of an attack by an external party. Such pacts are often formed to pool resources, share intelligence, and coordinate military strategies to enhance the security of member nations.

Khan’s emphasis on “regional security” suggests that the alliance is designed to address common threats and challenges faced by Pakistan, Saudi Arabia, and Turkiye within their respective geopolitical spheres. These threats can range from terrorism and extremism to conventional military aggression or destabilising regional conflicts. The alliance’s defensive posture is intended to reassure international partners while sending a clear message to potential adversaries.

The concept of “deterrence” is central to the FO’s explanation of the alliance’s purpose. Deterrence, in military and strategic terms, refers to the strategy of discouraging an adversary from taking an action by making the costs of that action outweigh the benefits. By forming a collective defensive front, Pakistan, Saudi Arabia, and Turkiye aim to present a formidable combined capability that would dissuade any party from undertaking aggressive actions against any of the member states.

Focus on Consolidation and Cooperation

Regarding the potential expansion of the alliance, Sajjad Haider Khan was explicit, stating that it was “not on the cards” at present. He added that “Pakistan, Saudi Arabia and Turkiye must first further consolidate their organisation and cooper” before considering any new members. This indicates a pragmatic approach, prioritising the internal strengthening and operationalisation of the existing framework.

The need for consolidation implies a phase of intense coordination and development among the founding members. This might involve establishing clear command structures, defining operational protocols, conducting joint military exercises, and fostering greater interoperability between their respective armed forces. Such steps are essential to ensure that the alliance can function effectively and cohesively in times of need.

Building a strong defensive alliance requires significant effort in harmonising defence policies, intelligence sharing mechanisms, and logistical support systems. The FO spokesperson’s remarks suggest that the current focus is on laying a strong foundation for the alliance, ensuring that the cooperation among Pakistan, Saudi Arabia, and Turkiye is deeply ingrained and highly efficient before contemplating broader membership.

Pakistan's Strategic Partnerships

Pakistan maintains deep historical and strategic ties with both Saudi Arabia and Turkiye. Its relationship with Saudi Arabia is complex, encompassing religious, economic, and defence cooperation. Pakistan has historically contributed to Saudi Arabia’s defence capabilities through military training and advisory roles, reflecting a long-standing partnership in security matters.

Similarly, Pakistan and Turkiye share a bond rooted in common cultural heritage, religious affinity, and a growing convergence of strategic interests. In recent years, defence cooperation between Islamabad and Ankara has seen an upward trajectory, with joint ventures in defence production, military exercises, and exchanges of expertise becoming more common. This deepening relationship forms a natural basis for their participation in a collective defence mechanism.

Pakistan’s foreign policy has consistently sought to foster regional peace and stability through various bilateral and multilateral engagements. Its participation in the Makkah Defence Alliance aligns with this broader strategy of strengthening partnerships with key Muslim nations to address shared security concerns and promote collective defence capabilities.

Regional Security Dynamics

The formation of such an alliance comes against a backdrop of complex and often volatile regional security dynamics. The Middle East and South Asia continue to grapple with a range of challenges, including geopolitical rivalries, non-state actors, and internal conflicts that have broader regional implications. These challenges necessitate a coordinated response from countries committed to maintaining stability.

For Saudi Arabia, ensuring the security of its borders, vital infrastructure, and holy sites remains a vital concern. Turkiye, with its expansive geopolitical interests stretching from the Eastern Mediterranean to the Caucasus and Africa, also seeks to safeguard its strategic autonomy and project stability in its neighbourhood. Pakistan, situated at the crossroads of South Asia, Central Asia, and the Middle East, faces its own set of security imperatives, including counter-terrorism and maritime security.

A defensive alliance involving these three nations can serve as a significant bulwark against potential external aggression and contribute to a more balanced security architecture in the wider region. It sends a signal that these countries are prepared to work together to protect their sovereignty and territorial integrity, thereby enhancing their collective security posture.

The Role of Deterrence in Regional Stability

The FO spokesperson's emphasis on deterrence highlights a core principle of international security. By pooling military and strategic resources, the Makkah Defence Alliance aims to create a credible threat of retaliation that would deter any potential aggressor. This approach seeks to prevent conflicts by making the cost of initiating hostilities prohibitively high.

Effective deterrence relies not only on military capability but also on the clear communication of intent and a strong demonstration of unity among alliance members. The FO's public clarification regarding the defensive nature and current non-expansion stance of the alliance contributes to this clarity, ensuring that its objectives are understood by both allies and potential adversaries.

In a region where geopolitical tensions can escalate rapidly, a well-defined defensive alliance can act as a stabilising force. It provides a framework for consultation and coordinated action, which can be important in de-escalating crises and managing regional security challenges effectively. The commitment to consolidation before expansion further strengthens the credibility of the alliance as a serious and long-term security commitment.
