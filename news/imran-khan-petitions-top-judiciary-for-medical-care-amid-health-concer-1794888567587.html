Imran Khan Petitions Top Judiciary for Medical Care Amid Health Concerns

Judicial Petitions and Medical Appeals from Incarceration

Imran Khan, the incarcerated founder of Pakistan Tehreek-e-Insaf and former prime minister, has made an urgent request for medical intervention for both himself and his wife, Bushra Bibi. According to a report by Dawn, this plea was conveyed through an open letter sent to the chief justices of the Federal Constitutional Court, the Supreme Court, and the Islamabad High Court. The communication was made public by Khan’s legal counsel, Barrister Salman Safdar, who shared the text on the social media platform X after visiting the former premier at Adiala jail.

The legal representative reported meeting Khan at the prison facility on two separate occasions, specifically on November 2 and November 11. Dawn reported that during the second interaction on November 11, Khan instructed his counsel to deliver his direct statements to the heads of the superior courts. Safdar confirmed in the correspondence that he had transcribed and transmitted the text faithfully following those specific directives. The released document bore the lawyer’s formal signature alongside the personal statements dictated by the former head of government.

As reported by BBC News, Khan raised grave apprehensions about his ongoing physical state in the written document. In his statement, the 74-year-old former prime minister remarked: "At 74, this unrelenting state is taking me to a place from which I may not return alive." Through this petition, Khan has asked the senior members of the judiciary to step in and direct that proper healthcare provisions be extended immediately to address the physical deterioration he says he is enduring.

Specific Health Conditions and Claims Regarding Medical Neglect

The correspondence detailed several medical emergencies that Khan asserts are threatening his life within the penitentiary system. According to BBC News, Khan wrote that medical practitioners have informed him that he remains highly vulnerable to a brain haemorrhage and cardiac arrest. He added that his heart has already suffered damage under the prevailing conditions of his detention. These assertions highlight severe cardiovascular and neurological dangers that his legal team insists need comprehensive, independent clinical evaluation.

Khan also brought forward claims regarding his eyesight, which his family has highlighted over the course of the preceding year. BBC News reported that his family maintains he has lost most of the vision in his right eye, attributing the issue to the failure of correctional authorities to act promptly when symptoms first presented. In the letter, Khan stressed that his ability to face pending legal challenges is directly tied to his physical survival. He wrote: "I will only remain able to prove my innocence if I stay alive."

Beyond his personal medical situation, Khan raised serious alarms regarding the welfare of his spouse, Bushra Bibi, who is also imprisoned. As BBC News detailed from the letter, Khan stated that his wife suffered a detached retina in April and later had to undergo a surgical procedure conducted in secrecy. He expressed deep distress that she risks losing her eyesight entirely if follow-up medical operations are not authorized. These health issues have formed the core of the petition submitted before the senior judges.

Detention Conditions and Claims of Solitary Confinement

The former prime minister also utilized his written message to air strong grievances concerning his daily treatment inside the correctional facility. According to BBC News, Khan reported that he is kept in solitary confinement for as much as 22 hours each day. He claimed that within this restricted setting, he is forbidden from receiving personal visits from relatives and is denied basic recreational and intellectual materials, including access to reading books and television programming.

Khan stated that these daily restrictions have exacted a heavy toll on his mental well-being over the extended duration of his detention. He noted that his interactions with legal advisors have also been restricted severely by prison officials. The letter indicated that he was granted permission to meet with his defense attorneys on only four occasions throughout the past year. Such limits, his team argues, impede his capacity to prepare an adequate defense across his numerous judicial cases.

Expressing frustration with his legal circumstances, Khan criticized the operational functioning of the justice system in his letter. As reported by BBC News, the former premier stated: "I have fought impossible legal battles in countless cases, and I have not stopped. But the system has stopped giving me justice." This assessment forms the foundation of his direct appeal to the country's senior judicial leadership, asking the court heads to intervene on humanitarian and procedural grounds.

Government Rebuttals and Official Medical Assessments

Federal authorities in Islamabad have offered a sharply contrasting description of Khan’s custodial environment and medical welfare. According to BBC News, administrative officials in the capital assert that the former prime minister receives weekly evaluations by qualified medical doctors. These regular examinations, according to the official accounts, have consistently concluded that Khan maintains a satisfactory level of general health while in state custody.

The government has pushed back against suggestions of mistreatment or inadequate provisions. Pakistan’s Parliamentary Affairs Minister Tariq Fazal Chaudhry publicly disputed the claims made in Khan’s letter during an interaction with reporters. As BBC News reported, Chaudhry stated that Khan is "provided with all facilities in prison, including food of his own choosing." The minister went on to tell media representatives that the former premier "is currently the most well-accommodated prisoner in Pakistan."

Official sources referenced by BBC News also outlined specific medical steps taken recently by state authorities to address his health needs. The authorities reported that Khan was escorted to a government-run hospital one week prior to receive an injection for his eye condition. Following that clinical visit, government medical staff assessed that Khan was clinically stable. These assertions present a direct challenge to the narrative of immediate physical jeopardy advanced by the defense counsel.

The Broader Legal Battles and Imprisonment Timeline

Khan’s current incarceration is the result of a protracted series of legal actions initiated against him and his family. BBC News reported that the former prime minister faces more than 100 legal charges, with allegations spanning from official corruption to the improper sale of state gifts. He has remained incarcerated continuously since August 2023, while his wife Bushra Bibi was placed behind bars alongside him during the preceding year.

Khan has consistently rejected every charge brought by state prosecutors, maintaining that the legal actions against both him and his spouse are politically motivated. Prior to his legal difficulties, Khan led the federal government as prime minister of Pakistan from 2018 until 2022, having entered politics after a distinguished international sports career during which he captained the national cricket team. His transition from sporting figure to head of government remains central to his lasting public profile.

The standoff over his incarceration has produced intense friction within the country's institutional and political arenas. Supporters of the former premier argue that the wide array of court cases is intended to remove him permanently from public life, while governmental officials maintain that all court proceedings reflect the standard application of judicial accountability. These competing perspectives continue to dominate public discussion concerning his confinement.

Public Demonstrations, Regional Unrest, and Future Developments

The detention of Imran Khan has repeatedly triggered widespread civil friction across urban centers and regional strongholds. As BBC News documented, his initial apprehension in May 2023 set off extensive and violent demonstrations by his followers. Protesters attacked the corps commander residence in Lahore, where individuals ransacked the property, damaged chandeliers, and took away household furniture, art paintings, and even peacocks. At least eight individuals lost their lives during that wave of civil unrest, compelling authorities to deploy army units to restore civil order.

Despite his continuous physical isolation, Khan remains a major force across Pakistan's political spectrum. In early October, thousands of his political supporters mobilized for a dayslong march through city streets to demand his immediate release. In response to the swelling street presence, administrative authorities launched a crackdown against demonstrations and declared a state of emergency within Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, recognized as a primary stronghold of Khan's political party.

The escalating debate over Khan’s well-being has also resonated in the international sporting community. Former international cricket captains have submitted multiple appeals directly to Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif, urging the government to guarantee adequate medical treatment for the former sports star. Observers will be closely monitoring how the chief justices of the Federal Constitutional Court, the Supreme Court, and the Islamabad High Court respond to Safdar's filing, as well as whether state authorities permit independent medical consultations in the coming days.
