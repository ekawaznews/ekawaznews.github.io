Razaullah Calls Pakistan Cricket a Lost Soul

By Umer Javed | 13 September 2026 | 3 min read

Former cricketer and commentator Razaullah has publicly declared that Pakistan cricket has lost its soul, warning that the national team is drifting further from its former glory.

Razaullah’s Critique

In his recent commentary, Razaullah highlighted a series of leadership failures and administrative missteps that have eroded the team’s confidence. He pointed to inconsistent selection policies, a lack of clear vision, and a culture that rewards short‑term results over long‑term growth.

Background of the Commentator

Razaullah, who played first‑class cricket in the 1990s and later turned to commentary, has long followed the sport’s highs and lows in Pakistan. He has been a regular voice on television and radio, offering analysis that blends technical insight with cultural context.

Reasons for Decline

Today, the national side faces a range of challenges. Selection panels have been criticised for favouring senior players over emerging talent, while coaching appointments have been viewed as politically motivated. Players report limited access to quality training facilities, and the media often highlights a lack of accountability within the board.

Historical Triumphs

Pakistan’s cricketing history is marked by landmark triumphs. The 1992 World Cup victory under Imran Khan and the 2009 ICC T20 World Cup win under Shahid Afridi remain symbols of national pride. Those victories were built on strong leadership, cohesive squads, and a belief that the team could compete with the best.

Present Challenges

Current issues include inconsistent selection, politicised appointments, and inadequate infrastructure. The board’s recent decisions have sparked debate about the direction of the sport.

Call for Overhaul

Razaullah urges the Pakistan Cricket Board to undertake a comprehensive overhaul. He stresses the need to invest in grassroots academies, improve coaching standards, and establish a transparent selection process that rewards merit. He also calls for the removal of political influence from cricket administration.

Reactions from the Cricket Community

While the article did not include direct reactions from other players or officials, several former cricketers have echoed similar concerns in interviews over the past months. Many fans have voiced their frustration on social media, demanding reforms that would bring the national side back to its winning ways.

PCB’s Silence

There has been no official statement from the PCB in response to Razaullah’s remarks. The board has, however, announced a new coaching contract for the upcoming season, though critics argue that this move does not address deeper systemic issues.

Fan Sentiment

Cricket remains the most popular sport in Pakistan, and the national team’s performance has a profound effect on the public psyche. Razaullah’s comments resonate with a generation that grew up watching legendary matches, and his critique has sparked renewed debate about the sport’s future.

Proposed Solutions

Experts suggest that a multi‑tiered strategy is required to restore confidence. This includes creating a national academy that offers world‑class training, establishing a clear pathway for young talent, and ensuring that coaching appointments are based on expertise rather than external pressures.

Looking Forward

Razaullah’s declaration that Pakistan cricket has lost its soul is a stark warning to stakeholders. It signals that without decisive action, the country’s cricketing heritage could continue to erode. The path forward will demand courage, transparency, and a commitment to nurturing talent at every level.

Final Thoughts

The debate ignited by Razaullah’s commentary may well serve as a catalyst for change. Whether the PCB and other governing bodies will heed these demands remains to be seen, but the call for reform has already taken root in the hearts of many Pakistanis who cherish the sport.
