Pakistan Hosts SCO Summit in Islamabad, 28-30 September

By Umer Javed | 27 September 2026 | 4 min read
Photo: iram shehzad / Pexels

Pakistan chaired the Shanghai Cooperation Organisation meeting in Islamabad from 28 to 30 September, focusing on regional cooperation, economic projects and security matters. The summit marked a significant diplomatic milestone for the host nation.

Pakistan hosted the Shanghai Cooperation Organisation meeting in Islamabad from 28 to 30 September, with the country serving as chair for the first time in its history.

SCO Overview

The Shanghai Cooperation Organisation, founded in 2001, is a Eurasian political, economic and security alliance that includes China, Russia, India, Pakistan, and several Central Asian republics. The group aims to promote stability, counterterrorism, and economic integration across its member states. Since its inception, the SCO has grown to include eight full members and several observer states, making it a key platform for diplomatic engagement in the region.

Pakistan's Chairmanship

Pakistan’s appointment as chairship reflects its growing influence in regional affairs. The role carries responsibilities such as setting the agenda, facilitating consensus, and representing the collective interests of the membership. This marks a first for Islamabad, as previous chairmanships were held by China, Russia, and India. The host nation aims to use the position to strengthen ties with both neighboring and distant partners.

Preparation and Logistics

Ahead of the summit, Islamabad prepared a comprehensive security plan, coordinated with local law enforcement and intelligence agencies. The venue, the Islamabad International Convention Centre, was upgraded to meet the technical and hospitality requirements of the delegation. Invitations were sent to all full members, observers, and partner states, with travel arrangements and accommodation handled by the Ministry of Foreign Affairs and the Pakistan Tourism Development Corporation.

Agenda Highlights

The summit’s agenda covered a broad spectrum of issues, from economic cooperation and infrastructure development to security and counterterrorism. Participants debated the expansion of trade corridors, the role of the SCO in global governance, and the need for greater collaboration on maritime security. The chairmanship sought to align these discussions with Pakistan’s national priorities, including the China-Pakistan Economic Corridor and regional connectivity projects.

Economic Cooperation

Economic integration remained a central theme. Delegates examined ways to deepen trade ties, reduce tariff barriers, and promote investment in key sectors such as energy, transport, and telecommunications. Pakistan highlighted the Belt and Road Initiative, stressing its potential to enhance trade routes across the region. The country also proposed joint ventures to upgrade ports, rail links, and road networks, aiming to boost intra‑SCO commerce.

Security Cooperation

Security cooperation was a focal point of the discussions. Participants reviewed joint counterterrorism operations, intelligence sharing mechanisms, and strategies to secure maritime routes. Pakistan reiterated its commitment to regional security, citing its experience in counterinsurgency operations. The summit also addressed cyber threats and the protection of critical infrastructure, with member states agreeing to share best practices and technical expertise.

Cultural and Humanitarian Initiatives

Beyond politics and economics, the summit also touched on cultural exchanges and humanitarian cooperation. Delegates discussed plans to promote language and cultural studies, support educational exchanges, and coordinate disaster relief efforts. Pakistan proposed a scholarship program for Central Asian students to study in Islamabad, fostering people‑to‑people ties and building long‑term goodwill.

Key Projects Discussed

Several specific projects received attention. The China‑Pakistan Economic Corridor, a flagship project under the Belt and Road Initiative, was highlighted as a model for regional connectivity. Delegates also examined the Trans‑Central Asian Railway, which could link Pakistan with Central Asian markets through a network of rail lines. Discussions included joint investment funds, technology transfer agreements, and the establishment of a regional logistics hub near the Afghan border.

Member States' Responses

Statements from member states reflected a shared commitment to the SCO’s objectives. Representatives from China, Russia, and India emphasized the importance of collective security and economic growth. Delegates from Central Asian republics expressed enthusiasm about the proposed connectivity projects, noting the potential to reduce transit times and costs. Observers from the United Arab Emirates and the United Kingdom welcomed the focus on counterterrorism and regional stability.

Implications for Pakistan

Hosting the SCO summit provides Pakistan with an opportunity to showcase its diplomatic capabilities and strengthen its regional standing. By steering the agenda, Islamabad can push for policies that align with its national interests, particularly in trade and security. The event also signals Pakistan’s readiness to play a larger role in shaping regional policy, potentially attracting further investment and cooperation.

Looking Ahead

Looking forward, the outcomes of the summit are expected to shape the agenda for future SCO meetings. Pakistan intends to follow up on the agreed initiatives, focusing on implementation and monitoring. The next chairmanship will likely rotate among member states, offering a chance for other countries to set new priorities. For Pakistan, the experience gained during this summit will inform its approach to multilateral diplomacy in the years ahead.
