Govt Raises Petrol Price by Rs4.10, HSD by Rs6.41 Per Litre

By Umer Javed | 16 September 2026 | 6 min read
Photo: Fahad Puthawala / Pexels

The government on Tuesday increased petrol prices by Rs4.10 per litre and high-speed diesel by Rs6.41 per litre, continuing a trend of adjustments tied to global oil markets and currency movements.

The government on Tuesday increased the price of petrol by Rs4.10 per litre and that of high-speed diesel (HSD) by Rs6.41 per litre. The notification was issued following a review of international oil prices and the prevailing exchange rate of the Pakistani rupee against the US dollar. This latest round of adjustments reflects the ongoing practice of aligning domestic fuel prices with global market trends.

Background of the Price Adjustment

Fuel prices in Pakistan are revised on a regular basis under a mechanism overseen by the Oil and Gas Regulatory Authority (OGRA). The government reviews global crude oil prices, currency exchange rates, and applicable taxes before announcing any change in retail prices. These adjustments typically take place at the beginning of each month, though timing can shift depending on market conditions.

The decision to raise petrol and diesel prices comes amid persistent volatility in international energy markets. Global crude oil prices have shown upward movement in recent weeks, driven by supply constraints and fluctuating demand patterns across major economies. The depreciation of the Pakistani rupee against the dollar has also contributed to higher import costs for refined petroleum products.

Details of the Increase

According to the official notification, petrol prices have been raised by Rs4.10 per litre. High-speed diesel, widely used in transportation and agriculture across the country, has seen an increase of Rs6.41 per litre. The new rates will apply nationwide at all petrol pumps and fuel stations once the notification takes effect.

Previous rates for petrol and diesel had already been adjusted several times over the past year. The government has maintained a policy of periodic price revisions to ensure that domestic fuel pricing remains broadly aligned with international benchmarks. Officials familiar with the matter stated that the latest increases were calculated after a thorough review of current market data and exchange rate movements.

Impact on Consumers and Transport Sector

The increase in fuel prices is expected to have a direct impact on consumers, particularly those who rely on personal vehicles for daily commuting. Transport operators, including bus and van services, are likely to adjust their fare structures in response to the higher cost of diesel and petrol. The agricultural sector, which depends heavily on diesel for tractors and irrigation pumps, may also face increased operational costs.

Small business owners who run fleets of vehicles for deliveries and logistics have expressed concern over the rising cost of fuel. Many stated that they have no choice but to pass on the increased expenses to customers through higher service charges. The rise in diesel prices is especially felt in rural areas where alternative modes of transport are limited and fuel consumption for agricultural purposes remains high.

Common citizens across major cities have voiced frustration over the continuous rise in fuel prices. Many told reporters that the increases add to the overall cost of living, affecting household budgets and everyday expenses. The ripple effect of fuel price hikes is often felt across multiple sectors, including food distribution, public transport, and general trade.

How Fuel Prices Are Determined in Pakistan

The pricing of petroleum products in Pakistan follows a structured formula. The government, through OGRA, takes into account the landed cost of oil, which includes the international price of crude, shipping charges, insurance, and taxes. The exchange rate of the rupee plays a significant role, as Pakistan imports the majority of its petroleum products.

Taxes and levies imposed by the federal and provincial governments also form a substantial portion of the retail price of fuel. Excise duty, general sales tax, and other surcharges are added on top of the base price, which can significantly affect the final amount paid by consumers at the pump.

Officials confirmed that the latest price revision was carried out in accordance with the established formula. The government stated that it strives to keep fuel prices as stable as possible while responding to external economic pressures. Any deviation from the formula, they stated, could result in financial losses for the oil refining and distribution infrastructure.

Historical Context of Fuel Price Revisions

Pakistan has a long history of adjusting fuel prices in response to global economic conditions. During periods of falling international oil prices, the government has reduced retail prices as well. However, in recent years, the trend has largely been upward, with prices increasing more frequently than they have decreased. This pattern has drawn criticism from opposition parties and consumer rights groups alike.

The Pakistan Peoples Party, the Pakistan Tehreek-e-Insaf, and other political parties have repeatedly questioned the rationale behind frequent price hikes. Opposition leaders stated that the government should provide relief to the common man instead of imposing additional burdens. They called on the administration to reconsider its approach to fuel pricing and explore ways to reduce the tax component on petroleum products.

The Pakistan Standardised and Quality Control Authority, along with OGRA, continues to monitor fuel quality and pricing across the country. Reports indicate that the regulatory bodies are keeping a close watch on market reactions following the latest increase. According to reports, consumer associations have also called for a review of the pricing mechanism to ensure greater transparency.

Broader Economic Implications

Higher fuel prices have a cascading effect on the broader economy. Increased transportation costs push up prices of essential goods, including food items, textiles, and construction materials. Inflationary pressures intensify as the cost of production and distribution rises across industries.

The International Monetary Fund has previously advised Pakistan to move toward market-based pricing for fuel. The government has in part followed this direction, though it also considers the social impact of such decisions. Officials stated that maintaining a balance between economic sustainability and public welfare remains a key priority for the administration.

The latest increase in petrol and diesel prices is expected to be reflected in the next inflation report from the Pakistan Bureau of Statistics. Economists believe that the move will contribute to a rise in the Consumer Price Index, particularly in the transport and food categories. According to reports, several market analysts have already revised their inflation forecasts upward in light of the announcement.

Government Response and Forward Outlook

The government has so far not announced any relief package or subsidy to offset the impact of the price increase on vulnerable groups. Officials stated that the decision was taken after careful consideration of all available data and in line with international practices. They further stated that the government remains committed to ensuring a stable and uninterrupted supply of fuel across the country.

The Ministry of Energy has been approached for additional comments regarding future price revisions. According to reports, no further adjustments are expected in the immediate term, though global market conditions could prompt another review earlier than scheduled. Consumers and industry stakeholders alike will be watching closely for any developments on this front in the coming weeks.
