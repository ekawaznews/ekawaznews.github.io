PM Shehbaz Orders Measures to Elevate Pakistani Exports to Global Standards

By Umer Javed | 15 September 2026 | 4 min read
Photo: Kavindu Kaushalya Perera / Pexels

Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif has directed authorities to implement effective measures to bring Pakistani product quality up to international standards, aiming to boost exports and accelerate economic growth.

Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif on Tuesday directed that effective measures be taken to bring the quality of Pakistani products up to international standards to enhance national exports. He issued the directives while chairing a review meeting in Islamabad on enhancing exports and accelerating economic growth, according to a statement by the Prime Minister’s Office (PMO).

Quality Standards and Compliance Emphasis

The prime minister emphasized the importance of improving the quality assurance system and ensuring full compliance with international standards and regulations. The directives came during a high-level meeting where officials reviewed the current state of Pakistani exports and identified key areas requiring immediate attention.

According to the PMO statement, the meeting was attended by senior government officials and representatives from relevant ministries. The focus remained on actionable steps that could be implemented across sectors to improve the competitiveness of Pakistani goods in global markets.

Export Growth and Economic Objectives

Enhancing exports has been a central goal of the current government’s economic strategy. Pakistan has long faced challenges in maintaining consistent export volumes, partly due to concerns over product quality and adherence to international norms. The directives issued by Prime Minister Sharif signal a renewed push to address these bottlenecks through institutional reforms and policy support.

Officials present at the meeting stressed the need for coordination between provincial and federal authorities to simplify certification processes and remove barriers that hinder export-oriented industries. The PMO noted that several sectors, including textiles, pharmaceuticals, and agro-based industries, were specifically discussed during the review session.

Industrial and Trade Sector Response

Trade associations and industry leaders have repeatedly called for government intervention to upgrade domestic manufacturing capabilities. Many exporters have expressed concerns regarding the lack of standardized quality control mechanisms, which they say limits their access to premium international markets. The prime minister’s directives are expected to provide much-needed policy clarity to businesses seeking to expand their export footprint.

According to reports, the government plans to establish dedicated task forces in key export sectors to monitor progress and ensure timely implementation of reforms. These initiatives may include partnerships with international certification bodies and the introduction of incentives for companies that meet global quality benchmarks.

Challenges Facing Pakistani Exports

Pakistan’s export performance has fluctuated over the past decade, influenced by factors such as energy shortages, limited access to finance, and evolving global trade dynamics. Despite these obstacles, certain sectors have shown resilience, particularly in textile manufacturing and surgical goods. However, sustaining growth requires a consistent framework for quality management.

The review meeting highlighted the role of small and medium enterprises (SMEs) in driving export diversification. Officials acknowledged that many SMEs lack the resources to independently meet international standards, suggesting that targeted support programs may be necessary to level the playing field.

Way Forward and Implementation Timeline

The Prime Minister’s Office stated that follow-up meetings will be convened on a regular basis to assess progress in implementing the outlined measures. A monitoring committee comprising representatives from the ministries of commerce, industry, and finance has reportedly been constituted to oversee the execution of reforms.

Analysts say that improving export quality is not merely about meeting standards but also about building long-term trust with international buyers. The initiative aligns with broader efforts to stabilize the economy and reduce reliance on external borrowing. For now, businesses and stakeholders are awaiting detailed policy announcements that will translate today’s directives into concrete actions.

Historical Context and Past Initiatives

This latest directive builds upon previous government efforts to revitalize Pakistan’s export sector. Past administrations have introduced export promotion schemes and duty exemptions, but results have been mixed. Experts argue that sustainable growth depends on systematic improvements in infrastructure, workforce skills, and regulatory consistency.

The current government’s approach appears to focus on strengthening institutional capacity rather than offering short-term subsidies. By prioritizing quality and compliance, policymakers aim to shift the narrative from quantity-based exports to value-added products that command higher prices internationally.

Stakeholder Expectations and Future Outlook

Industry chambers have welcomed the prime minister’s initiative, describing it as a step in the right direction. However, they caution that success will depend heavily on the speed and transparency of implementation. Delays or bureaucratic hurdles could undermine the intended benefits of the reforms.

As Pakistan continues to grapple with economic pressures, the export sector remains a vital source of foreign exchange and employment. With the right mix of policy support and private sector engagement, there is optimism that Pakistani products can gain stronger footholds in global markets. The coming months will be critical in determining whether these ambitious goals can be achieved.
