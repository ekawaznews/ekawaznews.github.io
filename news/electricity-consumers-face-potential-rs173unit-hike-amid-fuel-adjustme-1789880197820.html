Electricity Consumers Face Potential Rs1.73/Unit Hike Amid Fuel Adjustment Request

U
Umer Javed
Political Reporter
| 🕒 20 September 2026 at 09:56 am | 📖 5 min read
Share: 💬 WhatsApp 📘 Facebook ✈️ Telegram 🐦 Twitter
📷 Photo: Helena Lopes / Pexels | ekawaznews.github.io

Electricity consumers across Pakistan may face a Rs1.73 per unit increase in tariffs, following a request submitted to adjust power tariffs under the monthly fuel adjustment mechanism for August.

The Central Power Purchasing Agency (CPPA) has officially filed this application with the National Electric Power Regulatory Authority (Nepra). The regulatory body is scheduled to conduct a hearing on this significant request on September 29, which will determine the fate of electricity costs for millions of households and businesses nationwide.

According to the details provided in the application, the proposed increase is not limited to consumers served by the distribution companies (Discos) operating under CPPA's purview. It will also be applicable to consumers of K-Electric (KE), indicating a broad impact across the country's power.

The CPPA's request outlines that a total of 14.464 billion units of electricity were supplied to various distribution companies during the month of August. The average cost for these units stood at Rs8.82 per unit. The proposed adjustment seeks to reconcile the actual fuel costs incurred during power generation in August with the reference fuel costs already built into the existing tariffs.

Understanding the Fuel Adjustment Mechanism

The monthly fuel adjustment mechanism (FAM) is a standard component of Pakistan's power tariff structure. It allows power distribution companies to pass on variations in the cost of fuel used for electricity generation to consumers. This mechanism is designed to ensure that the actual cost of power generation, which fluctuates based on international fuel prices, exchange rates, and the generation mix, is recovered. Without such adjustments, the financial stability of the power sector could be jeopardized, potentially leading to increased circular debt.

These adjustments are a recurring feature of the power sector, reflecting the dynamic nature of energy markets. When global oil or gas prices rise, or if the country relies more on expensive imported fuels for power generation, the FAM typically results in an increase in tariffs. Conversely, a reduction in fuel costs can lead to a decrease, though such instances are often less frequent or smaller in magnitude compared to increases.

Nepra's role in this process is to act as an independent regulator. It meticulously reviews the applications filed by CPPA and other entities, scrutinizing the data on fuel consumption, generation mix, and costs. The Authority ensures that the calculations are accurate and that only legitimate costs are passed on to the consumers. This oversight is intended to protect consumer interests while allowing the power sector to remain financially viable.

The Regulatory Process Ahead

The hearing scheduled for September 29 is a critical step in this process. During the hearing, Nepra invites stakeholders, including CPPA, representatives from Discos, consumer groups, and the general public, to present their arguments and provide feedback on the proposed tariff adjustment. This transparent process allows for a thorough examination of the request and its potential implications.

Consumer advocacy groups frequently voice concerns during these hearings about the cumulative burden of such tariff increases on ordinary citizens. They often highlight the impact on household budgets, especially for low-income families already grappling with high inflation and rising living costs. The continuous upward trend in utility charges has been a consistent point of contention for many years.

Nepra's decision following the hearing will involve a careful balancing act. The Authority must consider the financial health of the power sector, which requires adequate cost recovery, against the need to ensure affordability for consumers. Any approved increase is typically notified after the hearing and then implemented in the subsequent billing cycles.

Potential Impact on Consumers and Economy

A Rs1.73 per unit increase, if approved, would represent a significant additional burden on electricity consumers across Pakistan. For an average household consuming several hundred units per month, this translates into hundreds or even thousands of rupees added to their monthly electricity bill. This comes at a time when the broader economic environment in Pakistan is already challenging, with persistent inflationary pressures impacting the purchasing power of citizens.

Industries, too, would feel the pinch of higher electricity costs. Energy is a substantial input cost for many manufacturing sectors. Increased power tariffs can erode the competitiveness of Pakistani products in both domestic and international markets. This can lead to higher production costs, which are often passed on to consumers in the form of higher prices for goods, further fueling inflation.

The government faces a complex challenge in managing the power sector. While it aims to provide affordable electricity, it also needs to ensure the financial sustainability of generation companies and distribution entities. The continuous need for tariff adjustments highlights underlying structural issues within the power sector, including inefficiencies, transmission and distribution losses, and the reliance on expensive imported fuels.

The monthly fuel adjustment mechanism, while technically a pass-through cost, often becomes a focal point of public frustration regarding electricity prices. It highlights the sensitivity of Pakistan's energy economy to global market fluctuations and domestic operational efficiencies. The government's energy policy and its ability to secure cheaper and more sustainable energy sources play a direct role in mitigating the frequency and magnitude of such tariff hikes.

As the date for Nepra's hearing approaches, consumers and businesses will be closely watching the proceedings. The decision made by the regulatory authority will have direct implications for household budgets and industrial operational costs, shaping the economic outlook for the coming months. The anticipated increase reflects the ongoing pressures within Pakistan's energy sector and the broader economic environment.
