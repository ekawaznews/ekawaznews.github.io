Musadik Malik to Represent Pakistan at SCO Civilisational Dialogue in New Delhi

Official Nomination and High-Level Representation in New Delhi

The Ministry of Foreign Affairs has formally confirmed that Minister for Climate Change Musadik Malik will represent Pakistan at the Shanghai Cooperation Organisation Civilisational Dialogue taking place in New Delhi. The event, which is set to run from November 20 to November 22, will bring together delegates from across the region to examine historical, social, and cultural themes. According to an official statement released by the Foreign Office on Saturday, the gathering serves as an important platform for member countries to convene and share perspectives on cultural cooperation.

The decision to send a federal minister highlights the intention of the government to maintain an active presence at regional multilateral gatherings. By sending a senior cabinet representative to the event hosted in the Indian capital, the leadership is taking steps to ensure that the voice of the nation is included in conversations regarding regional understanding. The Foreign Office indicated that the delegation was finalized after careful internal consultations regarding the schedule of international engagements.

As preparations for the event in New Delhi progress, officials within the diplomatic framework are aligning their priorities to reflect the core objectives of the alliance. The presence of Musadik Malik at this specific dialogue demonstrates a commitment to participating in discussions that extend beyond traditional political or security matters. Observers note that such gatherings offer a chance for member states to engage on topics that build shared understanding, creating avenues for broader institutional engagement over time.

Scope and Objectives of the Civilisational Dialogue

The scheduled gathering in New Delhi is designed to bring together all member states of the regional framework. Reports from the Indian news agency ANI indicate that ten member nations, including Pakistan, will be participating in the discussions. The primary agenda of this summit centers on civilisational dialogue, promoting mutual appreciation of different traditions, and building avenues for improved cultural understanding among the participating countries.

The event provides an established platform where representatives can discuss shared history, social cooperation, and the preservation of cultural heritage across member nations. According to reporting by Dawn, the gathering aims to explore how diverse societies can work together to address modern challenges while maintaining respect for their unique historical backgrounds. The discussions are expected to focus heavily on practical measures that can bring the populations of these countries closer together.

For the government of Pakistan, participating in these sessions allows the country to present its perspective on international cooperation. The Foreign Office noted that such discussions are intended to build a constructive environment where member nations can exchange ideas freely. By addressing cultural topics, the delegates hope to build trust and strengthen the foundations of regional partnerships within the framework of the Shanghai Cooperation Organisation.

Leadership Responsibilities and Strategic Context

Providing broader background on the upcoming visit, Deputy Prime Minister and Foreign Minister Ishaq Dar shared details regarding the regional commitments of the country. Writing on the social media platform X, he confirmed that Pakistan will actively participate in the New Delhi dialogue. He also highlighted that the country is designated to chair the Council of Heads of State for the Shanghai Cooperation Organisation during the 2026 to 2027 period.

This upcoming leadership role places added responsibility on the government to remain fully engaged with all activities sponsored by the regional organization. By participating in meetings organized across different member states, the administration demonstrates its willingness to fulfill its obligations leading up to its tenure as chair. Experts suggest that maintaining a consistent presence in current events will help the country shape the future priorities of the group when it assumes the chairmanship of the Council of Heads of State.

The statement by Ishaq Dar reflects the ongoing effort by the foreign policy establishment to balance regional commitments with broader international goals. The government views its upcoming chairmanship as an opportunity to guide discussions on regional economic development, security, and cultural exchanges. Participating in the New Delhi conference is therefore viewed as a necessary step in maintaining momentum within the organization as the transition to the 2026 to 2027 leadership period approaches.

Promoting People-to-People Contacts and Mutual Respect

A central theme of the position presented by the government involves the emphasis on public interaction across regional borders. In its official announcement, the Foreign Office stated that "The government of Pakistan remains committed to promoting dialogue among civilisations and fostering people-to-people contacts within the SCO framework in a spirit of mutual respect." This position highlights the belief that sustainable regional ties require direct engagement between citizens and civil society groups.

Establishing strong contacts between populations is seen by officials as a way to reduce misunderstandings and build long-term stability in the region. By advocating for increased cultural interaction, the government hopes to create opportunities for educational exchanges, artistic collaborations, and academic dialogue among member states. The conference in New Delhi provides a setting where such proposals can be discussed among official delegates and policy advisors.

The emphasis on mutual respect within the regional grouping reflects a consistent principle in the foreign policy approach of the state. Officials maintain that constructive engagement is most effective when member nations acknowledge and honor the diverse cultural realities of one another. The delegation headed by Musadik Malik will aim to reiterate this perspective during the sessions scheduled between November 20 and November 22.

The decision to assign the Minister for Climate Change to this cultural summit highlights the varied nature of current diplomatic assignments. According to reporting from The Nation, the Prime Minister has concurrently been highlighting climate concerns at the COP31 summit. This distribution of responsibilities shows how cabinet members are tasked with handling key international engagements across different global venues simultaneously.

While Musadik Malik oversees the climate change portfolio in his daily responsibilities, his representation at the civilisational dialogue in India shows that senior government figures are called upon to carry out high-level diplomacy when required. Managing environmental priorities alongside regional cultural engagements requires coordination across various government ministries. The participation of the minister in New Delhi comes at a time when the administration is actively involved in multiple high-profile international meetings.

The overlap between environmental priorities and regional diplomacy illustrates the expanding scope of international relations for developing nations. As climate change remains a dominant issue for the leadership, participating in regional organizations like the Shanghai Cooperation Organisation provides additional channels to discuss shared environmental and social challenges. The attendance of the climate minister in New Delhi could potentially allow for informal conversations on environmental cooperation alongside the planned civilisational discussions.
