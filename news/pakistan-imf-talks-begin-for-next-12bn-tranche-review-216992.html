Pakistan-IMF Talks Begin for Next $1.2bn Tranche Review

By Umer Javed | 28 September 2026 | 5 min read

Formal negotiations between the government of Pakistan and a visiting International Monetary Fund (IMF) staff mission are expected to begin today, Monday. These discussions are aimed at conducting the fourth review of the $7 billion Extended Fund Facility (EFF) and the third review of the Resilience Support Facility (RSF).

Official sources have confirmed that the mission, led by Iva Petrova, is scheduled to remain in Pakistan for approximately two weeks, with their stay extending until the first week of October. The primary objective of this visit is to assess Pakistan's economic performance and policy implementation under the ongoing IMF programmes.

Context of the Extended Fund Facility

Pakistan is currently operating under a 37-month IMF programme, known as the Extended Fund Facility. This comprehensive programme was initiated with the overarching goal of stabilising the nation's economy. Its core principles revolve around fostering fiscal discipline, implementing structural reforms, and addressing macroeconomic imbalances that have historically challenged Pakistan's economic resilience.

The EFF is designed to provide financial assistance to countries experiencing serious balance of payments problems because of structural weaknesses. For Pakistan, the facility has been a critical lifeline, offering support to manage external financing needs, bolster foreign exchange reserves, and restore investor confidence amidst persistent economic volatility.

Under the terms of the EFF, the government of Pakistan committed to a series of policy actions and reforms. These typically include measures aimed at increasing revenue collection, rationalising expenditures, reforming state-owned enterprises, and improving the energy sector's financial health. Each review by the IMF assesses the progress made against these agreed-upon benchmarks and conditions.

The Current Review and its Significance

The fourth review of the EFF is a significant juncture for Pakistan. A successful completion of this review is essential for unlocking the next tranche of approximately $1.2 billion, which is vital for the country's external financing requirements. The funds are disbursed in tranches, contingent upon the satisfactory completion of these periodic reviews.

These reviews are not merely financial transactions; they represent an assessment of the government's commitment to economic reforms. The IMF's stamp of approval often influences other international financial institutions and bilateral creditors, potentially opening avenues for further financial support and investment.

The discussions will encompass a wide array of economic indicators and policy areas. These typically include fiscal performance, particularly revenue collection and expenditure control, monetary policy management, exchange rate policies, and progress on structural reforms. The mission will scrutinise data related to budget deficits, public debt, inflation rates, and the current account balance.

Understanding the Resilience Support Facility

Alongside the EFF review, the mission will also conduct the third review of the Resilience Support Facility (RSF). The RSF is a relatively newer instrument introduced by the IMF, designed to help countries build long-term economic resilience against structural challenges, including climate change and pandemic preparedness.

While the EFF focuses on immediate macroeconomic stabilisation, the RSF aims at addressing deeper, long-term vulnerabilities. For Pakistan, a country highly susceptible to the impacts of climate change, the RSF provides an opportunity to access financing that supports investments in climate adaptation and mitigation measures, as well as reforms that enhance overall economic resilience.

Details regarding the specific commitments under Pakistan's RSF programme are typically aligned with national strategies for sustainable development and climate action. The third review will assess the progress made in implementing these specific policy measures and reforms that contribute to the nation's long-term resilience goals.

Key Areas of Discussion and Potential Reforms

While official sources have not disclosed the specific agenda items for this round of talks, previous reviews and the general objectives of the EFF suggest several key areas will be central to the discussions. Fiscal consolidation remains a top priority for the IMF. This involves measures to enhance tax collection through broadening the tax base and improving administrative efficiency, alongside efforts to control non-development expenditures.

Energy sector reforms are another persistent area of focus. Pakistan's power sector has faced long-standing issues, including circular debt and inefficiencies. The IMF typically presses for reforms aimed at tariff rationalisation, reducing subsidies, and improving the operational efficiency of power distribution companies. These measures are considered vital for reducing the fiscal burden on the government and ensuring the sector's sustainability.

Monetary policy and exchange rate management will also likely be discussed. The IMF generally advocates for a market-determined exchange rate and an independent central bank focused on price stability. Discussions might cover the State Bank of Pakistan's efforts to manage inflation and maintain financial stability.

Structural reforms extending to state-owned enterprises (SOEs) are also part of the broader reform agenda. The government's plans for privatisation or restructuring of loss-making SOEs are often a subject of review, given their significant drain on public finances. Discussions might also touch upon improving the business environment and attracting foreign investment.

Government's Commitment and Economic Outlook

The government of Pakistan has repeatedly reaffirmed its commitment to the IMF programme and its associated reform agenda. Senior government officials have consistently stated their resolve to implement difficult but necessary economic adjustments to steer the country towards sustainable growth and stability.

The successful completion of this review is not only about securing the next financial tranche. It also sends a strong positive signal to international markets and investors, suggesting that Pakistan is on track with its economic reforms. This can help in attracting foreign direct investment, improving credit ratings, and easing access to other forms of international financing.

Conversely, any delays or disagreements during the negotiations could introduce uncertainty, potentially impacting market sentiment and the country's ability to secure external financing. Therefore, the stakes for a positive outcome from these two weeks of discussions are considerably high for Pakistan's economic trajectory.

The ongoing engagement with the IMF highlights Pakistan's continued reliance on external financial support to manage its balance of payments and implement a long-term economic stabilisation strategy. The outcomes of the negotiations over the coming fortnight will be closely watched by economists, investors, and the general public, all keen to ascertain the future direction of the nation's economy.
