By Umer Javed | 13 September 2026 | 3 min read

Pakistan has called on the UN to close gaps in counter-terrorism, urging stronger cooperation and intelligence sharing. The move follows a series of attacks that highlight the evolving threat.

Pakistan has called on the United Nations to close gaps in counter‑terrorism, urging stronger cooperation and intelligence sharing.

Pakistan’s Call for Action

The Foreign Ministry issued a statement today demanding that the UN adopt more effective measures to address the changing nature of terrorist threats. Islamabad stresses that global security is only as strong as its weakest link, and that international bodies must act decisively to prevent new attacks.

Existing UN Counter‑Terrorism Efforts

The UN has long coordinated efforts through the Global Counter‑Terrorism Strategy, the Counter‑Terrorism Committee, and the Human Rights Council. These frameworks aim to harmonise legal standards, share best practices, and support capacity building for member states. However, critics argue that implementation remains uneven across regions.

Identified Gaps by Pakistan

Pakistan highlights three primary weaknesses: limited intelligence exchange, uneven training standards, and inconsistent legal frameworks for prosecuting terrorists. The country also points to a lack of coordinated cyber‑security initiatives that could curb online radicalisation. These shortcomings, the ministry says, create opportunities for extremist groups to regroup and expand.

Official Statements

Foreign Minister Shah Mahmood Qureshi told reporters that “the international community must bridge the divide between policy and practice.” He added that Pakistan is ready to collaborate with the UN and other partners to develop joint training modules and intelligence‑sharing protocols.

UN Response

The UN Secretary‑General’s spokesperson confirmed that the organization has received Pakistan’s concerns and will review them in the upcoming session of the Counter‑Terrorism Committee. The spokesperson noted that the UN is committed to enhancing cooperation among member states, particularly in the areas of legal harmonisation and capacity building.

Global Perspective

Other countries, including India and the United Arab Emirates, have echoed similar calls for tighter coordination. The United States has also urged its allies to strengthen intelligence networks, citing the rise of transnational terrorist cells that operate across borders.

Pakistan’s Experience with Terrorism

Since the 2008 attack on the Pakistani parliament, Islamabad has intensified counter‑terrorism operations. The country has dismantled several militant groups, yet the threat remains persistent, especially in the Khyber Pakhtunkhwa and Balochistan provinces. Recent incidents, such as the bombing of a railway station in Lahore, highlight the need for comprehensive global action.

Recommendations for the UN

Pakistan proposes the establishment of a dedicated UN counter‑terrorism task force that would oversee intelligence sharing, standardise training curricula, and monitor legal reforms. The ministry also suggests a rapid response mechanism for emerging threats, funded through a voluntary contribution by member states.

Potential Benefits

Strengthening the UN’s counter‑terrorism framework could lead to faster detection of plots, improved cross‑border cooperation, and more effective prosecution of suspects. Enhanced training would also help national forces adapt to evolving tactics used by extremist groups.

Challenges Ahead

Implementing these recommendations requires consensus among a diverse group of nations with varying priorities. Funding constraints, differing legal systems, and political sensitivities may slow progress. also, ensuring that intelligence is shared securely without compromising national security remains a delicate balance.

Looking Forward

Pakistan’s appeal arrives at a time when the UN is preparing for a major summit on global security. The country hopes that the meeting will produce concrete actions that close existing gaps and deter future attacks. If successful, the initiative could set a new standard for international cooperation in the fight against terrorism.

Conclusion

By urging the UN to strengthen its counter‑terrorism mechanisms, Pakistan signals its commitment to global security and its willingness to collaborate on a shared threat. The outcome of these discussions will be closely watched by governments and security agencies worldwide.
