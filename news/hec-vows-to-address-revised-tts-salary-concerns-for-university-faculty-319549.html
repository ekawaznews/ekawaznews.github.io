HEC Vows to Address Revised TTS Salary Concerns for University Faculty

By Umer Javed | 20 September 2026 | 2 min read
Photo: Nothing Ahead / Pexels

The Higher Education Commission has pledged to resolve the long-standing issues surrounding the revised Tenure Track System (TTS) salary structure for university faculty across Pakistan. This commitment offers a glimmer of hope to academics grappling with financial uncertainties and disparities.

The Higher Education Commission (HEC) has affirmed its commitment to addressing the revised Tenure Track System (TTS) salary issue, a matter that has caused considerable concern among university faculty members across Pakistan. This declaration from the country’s apex higher education regulatory body signals a potential breakthrough in a protracted dispute affecting a significant portion of the academic community.

The HEC’s vow comes amidst persistent calls from faculty associations and individual academics for a fair and equitable resolution to what they describe as inconsistencies and inadequacies in the current TTS salary framework. The issue has been a point of contention for some time, impacting faculty morale, research output, and the overall quality of higher education in the nation.

Understanding the Tenure Track System

The Tenure Track System (TTS) was introduced in Pakistani universities by the HEC in 2002 with the primary objective of attracting, recruiting, and retaining highly qualified faculty members. The system was designed to promote a culture of research, innovation, and academic excellence by offering competitive salaries and a performance-based career progression model, distinct from the traditional Basic Pay Scale (BPS) system.

Under the TTS, faculty members are assessed based on their teaching, research, and service contributions, with progression linked to achieving specific performance indicators. It was envisioned as a mechanism to modernize higher education, align Pakistani universities with international academic standards, and foster a meritocratic environment where academic achievement is duly rewarded. The system aimed to reduce the dependence on BPS, which often faced delays in revisions and did not adequately incentivize research productivity.

Unlike the BPS system, which is standardized across government departments, TTS offers a more flexible and potentially lucrative pay structure, intended to be attractive to top talent. Faculty on TTS are typically offered a contract for a probationary period, after which they may be granted tenure based on rigorous evaluation of their academic contributions. This model was expected to elevate the status of university professors and researchers, encouraging them to remain in Pakistan rather than seeking opportunities abroad.
