International bodies demand release of lawyers

Global Coalition Demands Release

According to reports by BBC Urdu, a coalition consisting of twenty-three international organizations has issued a joint statement. The group includes Amnesty International, the International Commission of Jurists, the International Bar Association Human Rights Institute, Front Line Defenders, and the Law Society of England and Wales. These institutions have called upon Pakistani authorities to ensure the immediate and unconditional release of human rights lawyer Iman Mazari and her husband, Hadi Ali Chatha. The statement also asks authorities to take steps to overturn the sentences recently handed down against them.

The collective appeal describes the outcome of the recent legal proceedings as an example of the alleged misuse of the criminal justice system. The signatories expressed serious concerns regarding the fairness of the trial proceedings conducted inside Adiala Jail. They claimed that the defendants were not provided with adequate access to their legal counsel throughout the hearings. The joint statement highlighted reports indicating that the couple was kept inside a cage-like enclosure within the courtroom for the duration of the sessions.

also, the international organizations pointed out that the prosecution relied primarily on the testimony of police officers. They stated that the defense was not afforded a sufficient opportunity to challenge the evidence presented by the prosecution in an effective manner. The appeal emphasizes the urgency of reversing the decisions made by the judicial authorities in Islamabad. The participating entities maintain that the legal steps taken in this matter fall short of recognized standards for a fair trial.

Details of Court Sentences

As reported by BBC Urdu, an anti-terrorism court in Islamabad delivered its verdict on October twenty-ninth. The judicial authority sentenced Iman Mazari and Hadi Ali Chatha to ten years in prison each in connection with a security violation case. Additional penalties were also handed down during the same ruling. Both individuals received one-year prison terms and fifty thousand rupees in fines under section 186 of the Pakistan Penal Code, which pertains to obstructing public servants in the discharge of public functions.

In addition to the aforementioned charges, the court applied section 353 of the Pakistan Penal Code. This section deals with using assault or criminal force to deter a public servant from executing their duties. Under this specific provision, the court sentenced both defendants to two years of imprisonment. The legal proceedings that led to these outcomes took place at Adiala Jail, where the two lawyers have been held in custody for a significant period.

The case itself stems from an incident that occurred in October of the previous year. According to the allegations, the event took place during the visit of the England cricket team to Islamabad and Rawalpindi. The prosecution alleged that the couple interfered with government duties by removing security barriers set up on the road. Both Iman Mazari and Hadi Ali Chatha have consistently denied these accusations. The trial involved several hearings held within the prison premises.

During the court proceedings, both defendants recorded their statements under section 342 of the Code of Criminal Procedure on October twenty-seventh. Iman Mazari stated on that occasion that she was recording her statement by way of protest. The framing of charges against the couple also took place while they were detained inside Adiala Jail. The defense team has raised multiple objections regarding the manner in which the trial was conducted and how evidence was gathered by the state machinery.

Defense Statements and Allegations

BBC Urdu detailed the accounts presented by Iman Mazari and Hadi Ali Chatha during their statements before the judge. Iman Mazari explained that on the day of the incident, she was traveling to an anti-terrorism court to represent a client. She noted that she had only fifteen minutes to reach her destination when police stopped traffic to create a route for the visiting foreign cricket team. Police personnel at the scene informed her that the team had already reached the stadium using an alternative path.

According to her statement, police officers could not produce any written order from a magistrate authorizing the road closure, and the prosecution failed to present such a document during the trial. After waiting for approximately fifteen minutes, she requested the officers to open the road, arguing that citizens' constitutional freedom of movement was being hindered. She maintained that she only remarked that the road was not private property. She questioned why motorcyclists were allowed to pass if the barriers were strictly necessary for security.

Iman Mazari further alleged that the prosecution deliberately withheld video evidence that would have contradicted their claims. She claimed that a police officer in plain clothes threw an iron rod at her, causing an injury. According to her testimony, her husband took defensive action to protect her from harm, and a medical report from Pims hospital documenting her injuries was submitted to the court. She asserted that no evidence of any police officer sustaining injuries was presented, and added that officers had previously apologized for their behavior.

Hadi Ali Chatha supported his wife statements and testified that officers at the site acknowledged the road had been closed for only a brief period. He maintained that a police officer attacked his wife first, prompting him to intervene defensively. He stated that the medical report from Pims hospital verified the injuries sustained by his wife. also, he claimed that after the Islamabad High Court suspended the judicial remand, subsequent investigative actions, including forensic and voice messaging procedures, were carried out unlawfully in their absence.

Broader Context and Implications

The statements recorded by the defendants suggest a wider pattern of pressure. Iman Mazari asserted that the case against her is part of a broader effort to target human rights defenders and lawyers in the country. She contended that her legal troubles stem directly from her work providing free legal services and following up on enforced disappearance cases. She maintained that even after securing bail from the Supreme Court, she faced re-arrest from within Adiala Jail and experienced physical abuse.

Legal analysts following these developments suggest that the international response could draw increased attention to procedural aspects of anti-terrorism trials in the country. Observers note that concerns raised by twenty-three global organizations may prompt discussions among legal bodies regarding trial transparency and access to counsel. The defense intends to pursue legal remedies through available appellate channels to contest the convictions handed down by the trial court.

The situation highlights ongoing debates regarding the application of anti-terrorism legislation in matters involving public demonstrations and interactions with law enforcement personnel. Civil society groups in Pakistan continue to monitor the proceedings closely. The juxtaposition of state security measures against citizens' freedom of movement remains a central theme in the arguments presented by the defense teams.

Next Steps in the Legal Process

As the legal battle continues, observers are watching for the formal appeals to be heard in higher judicial forums. The defense team is expected to challenge the ten-year sentences based on the grounds of unfair trial procedures and lack of independent witnesses. Legal experts indicate that the arguments regarding the absence of a magistrate's written order for the road closure will likely form a primary component of the upcoming appeals in the higher courts.

At the same time, domestic and international human rights advocates are expected to maintain pressure on government authorities regarding the conditions of detention and access to legal representation. The response of the Pakistani government to the joint statement issued by the international organizations remains to be seen. Public interest in the case persists as the appellate phase approaches, with various stakeholders analyzing the broader implications for legal practitioners handling sensitive cases.
