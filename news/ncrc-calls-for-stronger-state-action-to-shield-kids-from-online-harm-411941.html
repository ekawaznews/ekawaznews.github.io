NCRC Calls for Stronger State Action to Shield Kids from Online Harm

By Umer Javed | 4 October 2026 | 4 min read
Photo: PJ Mullen / Pexels

The National Commission on the Rights of Child urged Islamabad High Court to tighten safeguards for children under 16 on social media, proposing capacity boosts and special units for exploitation investigations.

The National Commission on the Rights of Child (NCRC) has urged the Islamabad High Court to strengthen state measures against online harm to children. In a 17‑page response filed in a case that examines social media usage by minors below 16, the commission highlighted the urgency of protecting young users from grooming, exploitation and harmful content.

Background of the Court Case

The Islamabad High Court is reviewing a petition filed by parents and child advocates concerned about the rising exposure of children to inappropriate material online. The case focuses on whether existing legislation and enforcement mechanisms adequately shield minors from digital risks. The court has asked the NCRC to provide recommendations on how the state can better fulfill its protective duties.

Key Recommendations from NCRC

In its detailed submission, the NCRC proposes several concrete steps. First, it calls for an increase in the capacity of the National Commission on Children and Adolescents (NCCIA). The commission argues that a well‑equipped NCCIA would be better positioned to monitor and respond to online threats faced by children. Second, the NCRC suggests the establishment of dedicated units within law enforcement agencies to investigate cases of child exploitation online. These units would specialize in digital evidence collection and analysis, ensuring swift action against offenders.

Third, the NCRC recommends a review of the Peca framework from a child rights perspective. The commission believes that current provisions should be examined to guarantee that they align with international standards on child protection. By re‑examining Peca, the state could strengthen its legal tools to combat online grooming and other forms of abuse.

State Responsibility in Digital Protection

According to the commission, safeguarding children on social media is a core responsibility of the state. It noted that the rapid growth of digital platforms has increased the vulnerability of minors to grooming, exploitation and exposure to harmful content. The NCRC stressed that the government must act proactively, not only by enforcing existing laws but also by updating policies to keep pace with technological changes.

Context of Child Online Safety in Pakistan

Pakistan has seen a surge in internet penetration over the past decade, with social media becoming a primary source of information and entertainment for young people. This trend has brought new challenges, as children often lack the digital literacy needed to navigate online spaces safely. Recent incidents of child exploitation on digital platforms have drawn public attention to the gaps in the country's protective framework.

In response, the government has taken steps such as launching awareness campaigns and working with tech companies to flag and remove harmful content. However, experts say that these measures alone are insufficient. The NCRC’s proposals aim to fill the gaps by strengthening institutional capacity and ensuring that child rights are central to any regulatory overhaul.

Implications for Policy and Enforcement

If the court adopts the NCRC’s recommendations, it could lead to the creation of specialized investigative teams that focus on cyber‑related child abuse. Increased NCCIA resources would allow for more comprehensive monitoring of online activity affecting children. A revised Peca framework might introduce stricter penalties for offenders and clearer guidelines for content moderation on social media platforms.

Such changes would align Pakistan’s approach with international best practices, ensuring that children enjoy safer online environments. The commission’s submission highlights that protecting minors from digital harm is not merely a legal obligation but a moral one that the state must uphold.

Next Steps and Public Response

The Islamabad High Court is scheduled to review the NCRC’s recommendations in the coming weeks. While the court has not yet issued a ruling, stakeholders anticipate that the decision will set a precedent for how the country manages child protection in the digital age. Public reaction to the NCRC’s call for stronger measures has been largely supportive, with many parents expressing relief at the prospect of increased safeguards for their children.

As the legal process unfolds, the focus will remain on translating the commission’s proposals into actionable policy. The outcome will determine whether Pakistan can effectively shield its younger generation from the growing risks associated with online engagement.
