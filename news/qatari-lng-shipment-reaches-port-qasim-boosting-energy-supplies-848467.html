Qatari LNG Shipment Reaches Port Qasim, Boosting Energy Supplies

By Umer Javed | 11 September 2026 | 6 min read
Photo: Natalya Rostun / Pexels

A cargo ship carrying 82,000 tonnes of liquefied natural gas from Qatar arrived in Pakistan through the Strait of Hormuz. This marks the first Qatari LNG shipment to Pakistan via this route since July, docking at the Engro LNG Terminal.

A cargo ship carrying approximately 82,000 tonnes of liquefied natural gas, sourced from Qatar, arrived in Pakistan on Thursday after transiting the Strait of Hormuz. The vessel, named Al Marrouna, docked at the Engro LNG Terminal situated at Port Qasim in Karachi.

This delivery signifies the first shipment of Qatari LNG to Pakistan through the strategically important Strait of Hormuz since July. According to Asad Altaf, a spokesman for Port Qasim, the last LNG cargo originating from Qatar had arrived on July 11. The Al Marrouna, laden with its vital cargo from Qatar’s Ras Laffan terminal, completed its transit through the strait on Monday, reaching its destination in Pakistan's economic hub on Thursday morning, as reported by Anadolu news agency.

Pakistan's Growing Energy Demand

Pakistan, a nation with a rapidly growing population and developing economy, faces a persistent energy deficit. The country's demand for electricity and natural gas consistently outstrips indigenous supply, making imported fuels, particularly liquefied natural gas, an indispensable component of its energy mix. LNG serves as a cleaner burning fossil fuel compared to furnace oil and is critical for power generation, industrial operations, and meeting the heating and cooking needs of millions of households.

The arrival of regular LNG shipments is therefore essential for maintaining energy security and supporting economic activity across various sectors. Industries rely heavily on a consistent supply of gas for their manufacturing processes, while the power sector utilizes LNG to fuel numerous thermal power plants, ensuring grid stability and preventing widespread blackouts. Without these imports, the energy shortfall would significantly impede national progress and cause considerable inconvenience to citizens and businesses alike.

The government has consistently emphasized the need to diversify energy sources and ensure reliable supply chains to power the nation's growth trajectory. LNG, with its relative ease of transport and flexibility, plays a prominent role in this strategy, bridging the gap between domestic production and soaring consumption.

Port Qasim: A Vital Energy Gateway

Port Qasim, located near Karachi, stands as Pakistan's second-largest port and a critical gateway for international trade, particularly for energy imports. Its strategic location and well-developed infrastructure make it an ideal hub for receiving large cargo vessels, including LNG carriers. The Engro LNG Terminal at Port Qasim is one of the country's key facilities dedicated to the regasification of imported LNG, converting the super-cooled liquid back into its gaseous state for injection into the national pipeline network.

The operational efficiency of terminals like the one at Port Qasim is vital for Pakistan's energy supply chain. These facilities are designed to handle massive volumes of LNG, offloading the cargo from specialized tankers and processing it for distribution across the country. The continuous functioning of these terminals ensures that gas can be delivered to power plants, factories, and homes without interruption, thereby minimizing energy shortages and their associated economic repercussions.

The port's ability to accommodate large vessels and its connectivity to the national gas transmission system are factors that underline its importance in the broader context of Pakistan's energy security framework. Investments in port infrastructure and LNG handling capabilities are ongoing, reflecting the country's commitment to securing its future energy needs.

The Strategic Significance of the Strait of Hormuz

The Strait of Hormuz is a narrow, yet globally significant, waterway connecting the Persian Gulf with the Arabian Sea and the open ocean. It is one of the world's most vital choke points for oil and natural gas shipments, with a substantial portion of the world's seaborne crude oil and liquefied natural gas passing through its waters daily. For countries like Pakistan that import a significant amount of their energy from the Middle East, transit through the Strait of Hormuz is unavoidable.

The strait's strategic importance means that any disruption or instability in the region can have far-reaching implications for global energy markets and supply chains. Therefore, the safe and unhindered passage of vessels, such as the Al Marrouna, through this waterway is a matter of considerable importance for importing nations. The consistent flow of energy through the Strait of Hormuz is a prerequisite for maintaining stable energy prices and ensuring supply reliability for consumers worldwide, including Pakistan.

The journey of the Qatari LNG shipment through this strait highlights the intricate global network that sustains Pakistan's energy economy. Monitoring and ensuring the security of these maritime routes is a continuous concern for all nations dependent on energy imports from the Gulf region.

Pakistan-Qatar Energy Partnership

Qatar is one of the world's largest exporters of liquefied natural gas, possessing vast natural gas reserves. Pakistan has established a strong and enduring energy partnership with Qatar, centered on the long-term supply of LNG. This collaboration is a cornerstone of Pakistan's energy strategy, providing a reliable source of gas to meet the country's escalating demands. The relationship between the two nations in the energy sector is built on mutual benefit and a shared understanding of global energy dynamics.

Long-term LNG supply agreements with Qatar provide Pakistan with a degree of price stability and supply assurance, insulating it from some of the volatility of the spot market. These agreements are essential for national energy planning, allowing the government and energy providers to forecast availability and costs with greater certainty. The consistent arrival of shipments from Qatar, as exemplified by the Al Marrouna, reinforces the strength of this bilateral energy relationship.

The partnership extends beyond mere transactions, often involving discussions on energy infrastructure development, technical cooperation, and broader economic ties. Qatar's role as a dependable energy supplier is highly valued in Pakistan, contributing significantly to the nation's energy security objectives.

Economic Implications and Future Outlook

The arrival of LNG shipments directly contributes to Pakistan's economic stability and growth. A consistent energy supply supports industrial output, prevents production halts, and ensures that businesses can operate efficiently. This, in turn, helps in sustaining employment levels and generating revenue for the national exchequer. The availability of gas also impacts the cost of electricity generation, which can influence inflation and the overall cost of doing business in the country.

The government's strategy for energy procurement involves a mix of long-term contracts and spot purchases, aiming to optimize costs while ensuring supply. The reliance on LNG, while addressing immediate energy needs, also presents challenges related to global price fluctuations and the need for continuous investment in import infrastructure. Pakistan is actively exploring various avenues to enhance its energy security, including the development of indigenous renewable energy sources and the optimization of existing energy infrastructure.

However, for the foreseeable future, imported LNG will remain a vital component of Pakistan's energy. The timely arrival of shipments like the one from Qatar through Port Qasim is the ongoing efforts to keep the nation's economy powered and its citizens supplied with essential energy resources.
