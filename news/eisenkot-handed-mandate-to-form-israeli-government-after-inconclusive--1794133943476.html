Eisenkot Handed Mandate to Form Israeli Government After Inconclusive Election

Presidential Mandate Follows Election Stalemate

Israel entered another period of severe political friction over the weekend after President Isaac Herzog asked opposition leader Gadi Eisenkot to attempt to form a governing coalition. The formal step followed an inconclusive parliamentary election held on October 27, which left both the ruling alliance and its opponents without a direct parliamentary route to power. The choice came after consultations with party leaders in the wake of the vote, ending the immediate question of who would receive the first chance to construct a viable administration.

According to reports by ARY News and Dawn, President Herzog invited the former military chief to his official residence to formally assign him the responsibility. A spokesperson for the Israeli head of state confirmed the invitation, stating that President Herzog invited Gadi Eisenkot to the presidential quarters where he would receive the task of forming the administration. The presidency in Israel is largely a ceremonial office, but the power to designate a prime ministerial candidate during a hung parliament places the head of state in a decisive procedural role.

Following the presidential invitation, Eisenkot released a public statement on social media outlining his intentions. In a message posted on X, Eisenkot wrote that now is the time to form a national emergency government, adding that he will do everything he can to ensure the leadership is worthy. His words signaled an immediate desire to project stability, even as domestic commentators suggested that the political atmosphere makes rapid consensus exceptionally difficult to achieve across competing ideological factions.

Parliamentary Numbers and Competing Blocs

The allocation of parliamentary seats highlights the scale of the arithmetic problem facing all major leaders in the country. ARY News reported that Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu and his party, Likud, managed to secure 27 seats, retaining the status of the single largest political entity in the 120-seat Knesset. However, his wider religious-nationalist partnership managed to collect only 53 seats overall. This left the long-serving leader eight seats short of the 61-seat threshold required to command an absolute majority in the chamber.

On the opposing side, Eisenkot heads the Yashar party, which emerged from the voting booths as the second-largest grouping after securing 21 seats. When combined with three other Jewish-led partner parties across the centre-left and right of the spectrum, his immediate coalition reached 48 seats. BBC News similarly observed that neither the bloc led by Netanyahu nor the opposing alliance led by his challenger managed to secure a majority in the balloting, leaving the legislature divided into rigid and mutually hostile segments.

The procedural deadlock was shaped significantly by recommendations submitted to the head of state by other factions. Fox News reported that Eisenkot managed to gather 67 endorsements from newly elected lawmakers, surpassing Netanyahu in the formal consultation phase. While these endorsements gave the president sufficient legal justification to tap Eisenkot first, they did not translate into a unified or stable coalition ready to govern on day one.

The Position of Arab Parties and Coalition Math

A key factor in the endorsement tallies was the performance of factions representing the Arab minority in Israel. In the October 27 balloting, two parties representing Arab citizens captured 19 seats in the Knesset, which ARY News described as an unprecedented tally for these factions. Their strong showing effectively deprived Netanyahu of his majority while creating a complex strategic dilemma for the opposition.

Despite winning their endorsements, Eisenkot has maintained a clear separation from Arab factions. Reports from ARY News and Dawn indicated that the former military chief has declined to join forces with Arab parties, even though their direct participation would immediately grant him the numerical majority required to govern. For their part, Arab political leaders decided to endorse Eisenkot primarily out of hostility toward the sitting prime minister and his hardline allies, whose policies they described as racist. Their tactical support was aimed at denying Netanyahu another term rather than entering a formal governing pact.

As Fox News pointed out, deep disagreements over Arab political participation and related domestic matters, such as military conscription, have created substantial barriers for any coalition builder. Without the formal inclusion of Arab lawmakers, Eisenkot must look toward other factions that have historically held deep animosities toward his political partners. This creates an acute numerical dilemma where ideological boundaries prevent any simple combination of lawmakers from reaching the 61 seats needed to take power.

Eisenkot’s Profile and Personal Background

Eisenkot brings an unusual personal and professional background to the center of Israeli politics. At 66 years old, he is the son of Moroccan immigrants and would become the first prime minister from the Mizrahi community, which includes Jews who trace their family roots to North Africa and the Middle East. He has generally been described by media observers as reserved and hesitant to seek the public spotlight, traits that contrast with the rhetorical style of the current prime minister.

His political career is relatively short, having only entered parliamentary politics in 2022. After the Hamas attack on Israel on October 7, 2023, Eisenkot agreed to serve in Netanyahu’s emergency wartime cabinet. That arrangement was brief, however, and he later stepped down to return to the opposition benches. Since that departure, he has been an outspoken critic of Netanyahu, attacking the prime minister’s administrative conduct and specifically his management of the war in Gaza. He has repeatedly sworn that he would refuse to sit in any government led by Netanyahu.

Beyond his political statements, Eisenkot’s public standing in the country has been heavily influenced by his family’s experiences in recent conflict. According to ARY News, his family paid a steep personal cost after 2023, with one of his sons and two of his nephews killed while fighting in Gaza. This personal loss has featured prominently in domestic perceptions of his leadership and his posture on national defense matters.

Security Doctrine and Criticisms of Far-Right Figures

Throughout his recent campaign, Eisenkot relied heavily on his prior military record, reminding voters of his role as an army commander. ARY News reported that he pointed directly to his formulation of the Dahiyeh doctrine, an operational approach established during the 2006 conflict with Iran-backed Hezbollah in Lebanon that relies on the application of disproportionate military force in response to attacks. He framed this doctrine as evidence of his security credentials, attempting to reassure right-leaning voters that his administration would remain firm on national defense issues.

At the same time, Eisenkot sought to separate himself from the far-right elements that have formed the backbone of Netanyahu’s political support. He has openly criticized the surge in settler violence against Palestinians that developed after 2023, a development that has heightened friction between Israeli authorities and Western partners. His campaign also expressed vocal opposition to Netanyahu’s far-right partners, specifically Itamar Ben-Gvir and Bezalel Smotrich.

Both Ben-Gvir and Smotrich have faced punitive sanctions from certain Western governments over allegations of inciting violence against Palestinian civilians. By rejecting these figures, Eisenkot has sought to present an alternative governing vision that could appeal to moderate voters and ease tensions with foreign allies, though this rejection simultaneously closes off any realistic prospect of forming a government with the existing right-wing religious bloc.

Potential Pathways and Likelihood of Fresh Elections

Under the legal framework governing Israeli elections, Eisenkot has been granted an initial window of 28 days to conclude coalition agreements, with the president retaining the authority to grant a 14-day extension if progress is being made. Analysts cited by ARY News suggest that Eisenkot essentially faces three theoretical routes to power, though each presents immense practical difficulties. He could attempt to construct a minority government that relies on external votes from Arab parties on specific legislation, seek an agreement with ultra-Orthodox parties that his secular allies have historically opposed, or attempt a national unity arrangement with Netanyahu.

Political observers consider all three possibilities highly improbable given the personal rivalries and conflicting policy agendas separating the major factions. If Eisenkot fails to build a governing coalition within his allotted timeframe, the mandate can be handed to another political figure to attempt the same task. Should that candidate also fail, members of the Knesset may attempt to coalesce around an alternative candidate of their choosing.

The overall legal clock for this consultative process spans approximately 100 days from start to finish. If no leader is able to assemble a majority by the conclusion of that statutory timeline, the Knesset will dissolve automatically and voters will be called back to the polls for another national election. For readers in Pakistan and across the wider region, the continuation of political instability in Israel suggests that foreign policy postures, diplomatic engagement, and the ongoing crises across the Middle East will remain subject to shifting internal political pressures for the foreseeable future.
