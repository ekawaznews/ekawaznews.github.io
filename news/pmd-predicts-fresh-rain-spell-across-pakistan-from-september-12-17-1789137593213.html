PMD Predicts Fresh Rain Spell Across Pakistan from September 12-17

U
Umer Javed
Political Reporter
| 🕒 11 September 2026 at 07:39 pm | 📖 5 min read
Share: 💬 WhatsApp 📘 Facebook ✈️ Telegram 🐦 Twitter
📷 Photo: Ann H / Pexels | ekawaznews.github.io

The Pakistan Meteorological Department (PMD) on Friday predicted a fresh spell of rain across the country starting tomorrow, September 12, and continuing until September 17. This anticipated weather change is expected to bring a noticeable drop in temperatures during the forecast period.

According to the Met Office, monsoon currents are currently penetrating the upper parts of the country, setting the stage for these upcoming precipitation events. also, a westerly wave is likely to enter the upper regions of Pakistan during the weekend, with expectations for it to strengthen significantly by September 14. This combination of meteorological phenomena is poised to influence weather patterns across a wide area.

Detailed Forecast for Northern Regions

Specific attention has been drawn to Kashmir, where rain-wind and thundershowers are expected. The forecast includes the possibility of isolated hailstorms, which can sometimes accompany intense thundershowers. Heavy showers are also predicted for several specific areas within Kashmir, including Neelum Valley, Muzaffarabad, Rawalakot, Poonch, Hattian, Bagh, Haveli, Sudhanoti, and Kotli.

These concentrated heavy showers in mountainous and hilly terrains often raise concerns about potential flash floods and landslides, particularly in vulnerable areas. Residents and local authorities in these regions are advised to remain vigilant and take necessary precautionary measures as the weather system develops.

Understanding Monsoon Currents and Westerly Waves

The interaction of monsoon currents and a westerly wave is a significant aspect of Pakistan's weather dynamics. Monsoon currents are typically responsible for bringing moisture from the Arabian Sea and the Bay of Bengal into the subcontinent, leading to the characteristic summer rains that are vital for agriculture and water reservoirs.

Westerly waves,, are weather systems originating in the Mediterranean Sea that travel eastward, bringing rain, and sometimes snow, to Pakistan during the winter months. However, their influence can also be felt during other seasons, especially when they interact with existing monsoon systems, amplifying precipitation and causing more widespread weather disturbances.

The current forecast highlights a unique confluence where the tail end of the monsoon season is meeting an incoming westerly wave. This interaction can lead to prolonged periods of rain and a more widespread impact than either system might produce in isolation. Such meteorological convergences are closely monitored by weather authorities due to their potential for intense weather events.

Potential Impacts Across the Country

While the detailed forecast specifically mentions Kashmir, the PMD's statement indicates a countrywide impact. This suggests that various regions, beyond the immediate focus on the north, will also experience changes in weather. Lower temperatures are generally welcomed after the summer heat, providing relief to the populace.

Rainfall, especially during this period, can have mixed implications. For agricultural areas, timely and moderate rain is beneficial for late-season crops and preparing the ground for winter sowings. However, excessive or prolonged heavy rainfall can pose risks to standing crops, lead to waterlogging, and damage infrastructure.

Urban centers across Pakistan, particularly those with less developed drainage systems, often face challenges during heavy downpours. Water accumulation on roads and in low-lying areas can disrupt daily life, affect traffic flow, and sometimes lead to temporary power outages. Local municipal corporations and disaster management bodies typically prepare for such eventualities by clearing drains and issuing advisories.

Preparedness and Public Safety

In light of the PMD's forecast, authorities and the public are urged to exercise caution. People residing in low-lying areas or close to riverbeds are often advised to take necessary precautions against potential flooding. Those traveling, especially in mountainous regions like Kashmir, should be aware of the increased risk of landslides and ensure their routes are safe.

The consistent monitoring of weather patterns by the Pakistan Meteorological Department plays a vital role in providing timely warnings and enabling preparatory measures. Such forecasts allow government agencies, disaster management authorities, and the general public to respond effectively, minimizing potential damage and ensuring public safety.

Farmers, in particular, rely heavily on accurate weather predictions to make informed decisions regarding irrigation, harvesting, and planting schedules. The timing and intensity of rainfall are critical factors that directly influence agricultural productivity, which forms a significant part of Pakistan's economy.

Historical Context of Monsoon Variability

Pakistan's experience with monsoon seasons has shown considerable variability over the years. Some years bring abundant, well-distributed rain, leading to healthy harvests and replenished water reservoirs. Other years witness either deficit rainfall, resulting in drought-like conditions, or excessive and concentrated downpours, causing widespread flooding.

The devastating floods experienced in various parts of the country in previous years serve as a stark reminder of the power of monsoon systems and the necessity of strong infrastructure and early warning systems. While the current forecast does not specify extreme events on that scale, the mention of heavy showers and isolated hailstorms necessitates vigilance.

The interaction of monsoon and westerly waves can sometimes lead to unpredictable localized weather phenomena, making it important for the public to stay updated through official channels. The Met Office's continuous updates are therefore indispensable for public safety and operational planning across various sectors.

Looking Ahead

As the fresh spell of rain is set to begin, the focus remains on its progression and impact. The expected drop in temperatures will offer a welcome respite from the prevailing warmth, but this comes with the need for careful management of potential rain-related challenges. The PMD will continue to issue updates as the weather system evolves, providing essential information to the nation.

The coming days will demonstrate the extent of this weather system's influence, from the northern mountains to the plains. Adherence to official advisories and proactive measures by individuals and communities will be key to mitigating any adverse effects of this forecasted change in weather.
