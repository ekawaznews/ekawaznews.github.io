Four-Day Weather Overview for Coastal and Southwestern Hubs

Meteorological data recorded on Saturday, 10 October 2026 outlines a distinct split in atmospheric patterns across Pakistan over the coming four days. Southern and southwestern cities are projected to experience largely dry conditions, accompanied by rising afternoon warmth in coastal zones and cool overnight readings in highland valleys. Residents across these areas can anticipate settled weather through Tuesday, 13 October, with minimal disruption to open-air movement and commerce.

In Karachi, the forecast indicates steady conditions with a gradual warming trend. On Saturday, 10 October, temperatures are predicted to reach a high of 31 degrees Celsius alongside an overnight low of 27 degrees Celsius under partly cloudy skies, with rain chances standing at zero percent. Sunday, 11 October will bring a high of 32 degrees Celsius and a low of 26 degrees Celsius, maintaining partly cloudy conditions and a zero percent chance of precipitation. By Monday, 12 October, daytime highs are projected to increase to 34 degrees Celsius, with a minimum of 26 degrees Celsius, partly cloudy conditions, and an eight percent probability of rain. On Tuesday, 13 October, the sky will clear completely as the maximum temperature rises to 35 degrees Celsius with a low of 26 degrees Celsius and zero percent chance of rain.

Quetta presents a very different thermal environment characterized by pleasant afternoons and distinctly cool nights. On Saturday, 10 October, the provincial capital is forecast to record a maximum of 30 degrees Celsius and a minimum of 16 degrees Celsius under partly cloudy skies with no rain anticipated. Sunday, 11 October will see temperatures peak at 29 degrees Celsius before dropping to a low of 14 degrees Celsius, again with zero percent precipitation. On Monday, 12 October, daytime readings will hold at 29 degrees Celsius, with an overnight mark of 15 degrees Celsius, partly cloudy conditions, and a sixteen percent chance of rainfall. By Tuesday, 13 October, the afternoon high will ease to 27 degrees Celsius, while the low settles at 16 degrees Celsius under partly cloudy skies with rain chances returning to zero percent.

Atmospheric Instability in the North and Federal Capital

Northern sections of the country will face a markedly unsettled sequence of weather over the four-day forecast span. Federal territory and surrounding districts are expected to experience frequent precipitation, recurring thunderstorms, and reduced daytime highs as moist air settles across the higher elevations. Residents across these regions should anticipate changeable skies through the middle of the week.

In Islamabad, stormy conditions are projected to dominate much of the outlook window. On Saturday, 10 October, the federal capital faces thunderstorms, with daytime temperatures reaching 26 degrees Celsius, an overnight low of 21 degrees Celsius, and an eighty-eight percent chance of rain. Sunday, 11 October brings continued thunderstorm activity, with a high of 26 degrees Celsius, a low of 19 degrees Celsius, and a sixty-five percent likelihood of rainfall. Monday, 12 October will offer a brief shift toward lighter precipitation, bringing drizzle alongside a daytime high of 28 degrees Celsius, an overnight low of 18 degrees Celsius, and a thirty percent rain chance. Thunderstorms and heavier showers will return on Tuesday, 13 October, when rain is expected with a high of 27 degrees Celsius, a low of 19 degrees Celsius, and a ninety percent chance of precipitation.

Peshawar will encounter similarly unsettled conditions throughout the period. Saturday, 10 October brings thunderstorms with a maximum temperature of 28 degrees Celsius, a minimum of 20 degrees Celsius, and a seventy-six percent chance of rain. On Sunday, 11 October, thunderous activity continues as the high reaches 27 degrees Celsius and the low touches 19 degrees Celsius, accompanied by a ninety percent precipitation probability. Monday, 12 October will feature drizzle, with temperatures rising slightly to a high of 29 degrees Celsius, an overnight low of 19 degrees Celsius, and a seventy-three percent chance of rain. Tuesday, 13 October maintains drizzle, with temperatures remaining at a high of 29 degrees Celsius and a low of 20 degrees Celsius, alongside a seventy-six percent rain probability.

Further north in Gilgit, conditions will transition from quiet skies into significant rainfall accompanied by declining temperatures. Saturday, 10 October begins with partly cloudy weather, a high of 26 degrees Celsius, a low of 14 degrees Celsius, and zero percent rain probability. On Sunday, 11 October, drizzle enters the forecast, pulling the high down to 24 degrees Celsius while the low rises to 16 degrees Celsius, with a thirty-five percent chance of rain. Monday, 12 October will see cooler conditions with drizzle, a high of 22 degrees Celsius, an overnight low of 12 degrees Celsius, and an eighty-four percent likelihood of precipitation. On Tuesday, 13 October, steady rain is expected as the daytime temperature drops to 21 degrees Celsius, with an overnight minimum of 14 degrees Celsius and a ninety-nine percent probability of rainfall.

Lahore is forecast to see fluctuating storm chances over the four days. On Saturday, 10 October, the provincial capital is projected to record a high of 31 degrees Celsius and an overnight low of 23 degrees Celsius with thunderstorms, though the chance of rain remains limited to eight percent. Sunday, 11 October will bring more widespread thunderstorm activity, with the maximum temperature declining to 29 degrees Celsius, the overnight low dropping to 20 degrees Celsius, and rain probability rising to fifty-one percent. Monday, 12 October will feature rain, with temperatures matching Sunday at a high of 29 degrees Celsius and a low of 20 degrees Celsius, alongside a twenty-two percent chance of precipitation. On Tuesday, 13 October, skies will become partly cloudy, with temperatures reaching 29 degrees Celsius by day and 21 degrees Celsius at night, accompanied by a fifty-three percent probability of rain.

Multan presents an interesting contrast, beginning the forecast window with intense daytime heat before experiencing cooler conditions. On Saturday, 10 October, the city will experience drizzle alongside a hot daytime high of 37 degrees Celsius, a warm night of 27 degrees Celsius, and a forty-seven percent chance of rain. Sunday, 11 October brings a sharp change as thunderstorms arrive, lowering the daytime peak to 31 degrees Celsius and the overnight low to 22 degrees Celsius, with rain probability recorded at fifty-five percent. On Monday, 12 October, thunderstorms remain possible with a high of 32 degrees Celsius, a low of 21 degrees Celsius, and a reduced rain probability of twelve percent. Tuesday, 13 October will see drizzle return, with temperatures holding at 32 degrees Celsius during the day and 24 degrees Celsius at night, with a thirty-seven percent chance of precipitation.

The diverse weather across the country will naturally affect daily travel habits and long-distance transit. In cities experiencing significant rain probabilities, such as Islamabad and Peshawar, wet pavement and localized ponding can slow peak-hour commuting. Thunderstorm periods can lead to diminished visibility, making caution advisable for motorists traveling along major ring roads, intercity highways, and regional link corridors.

In northern upland corridors leading toward Gilgit, rising chances of rain from thirty-five percent on Sunday to ninety-nine percent on Tuesday present clear operational considerations. Wet road conditions on mountain arteries often require drivers to reduce speeds and maintain safe distances. Commuters in these areas may wish to monitor localized transit updates before embarking on journeys across elevation passes.

Conversely, travel within Karachi and Quetta should proceed under favorable conditions. With rain probabilities remaining close to zero percent across the four days, transport operations by air, rail, and road are expected to face minimal disruptions caused by precipitation. In Karachi, however, drivers and outdoor delivery workers may need to contend with increasing afternoon warmth, which will climb toward 35 degrees Celsius by Tuesday.
