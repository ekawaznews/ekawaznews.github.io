Effects on Farming and Agricultural Activities

Agricultural conditions are tied closely to regional weather patterns and available water resources. As reported by Dawn, the cultivation of Rabi crops, including wheat and oilseeds, has begun in lower Sindh amid a twenty-two percent irrigation water shortage forecast by Irsa. Farmers in Sindh are increasingly turning to oilseed crops, particularly mustard, for quicker economic returns.

Greater domestic production could help reduce Pakistan dependence on imported edible oil, which consumes substantial foreign exchange. According to Dawn, Pakistan imported a record 3.48 million tonnes of palm oil worth $3.78 billion in the financial year 2026, compared with 3.21 million tonnes costing $3.4 billion in the financial year 2025, with the average import price rising to $1,078 per tonne from $1,056.

Data from the Pakistan Oilseed Department and the Pakistan Bureau of Statistics, cited by Dawn, shows that Pakistan edible oil import bill reached $5.86 billion in 2025 for 4.52 million tonnes. This total included 3.671 million tonnes of imported oil and 0.853 million tonnes extracted from 3.848 million tonnes of imported oilseeds, illustrating an ongoing national shift toward domestic agricultural adjustments in response to external market costs and water constraints.
