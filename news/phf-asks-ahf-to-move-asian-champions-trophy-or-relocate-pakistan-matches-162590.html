PHF Asks AHF to Move Asian Champions Trophy or Relocate Pakistan Matches

By Umer Javed | 16 September 2026 | 4 min read

The Pakistan Hockey Federation (PHF) has formally asked the Asian Hockey Federation (AHF) to move next month’s men’s Asian Champions Trophy out of India or shift all of Pakistan’s matches to a neutral venue, citing the “sensitive bilateral situation” between the two countries.

Details of the Request

The request effectively rules out Pakistan playing their matches at the tournament’s scheduled venues in Jalandhar and Mohali unless the AHF agrees to the proposed change. PHF president Mohyuddin Ahmed Wani, who is also federal secretary of the Ministry of Inter‑Provincial Coordination, confirmed to Dawn on Wednesday that the federation is seeking a neutral ground for its team. The move reflects the ongoing diplomatic freeze that has limited sporting exchanges since 2019.

Background to the Asian Champions Trophy

The Asian Champions Trophy is a biennial competition organized by the Asian Hockey Federation to showcase the strongest men’s national teams in the region. India has hosted the event on several occasions, most recently in 2023. Pakistan, a historic powerhouse in field hockey, has consistently participated and often secured podium finishes. The tournament is considered a key platform for qualifying for larger events such as the Hockey World Cup.

India‑Pakistan Bilateral Situation

Diplomatic relations between India and Pakistan have been strained for more than a decade, marked by border skirmishes, differing positions on Kashmir, and limited official interactions. Since 2019, both governments have imposed travel restrictions on sports personnel, making joint ventures rare. The “sensitive bilateral situation” cited by PHF mirrors the broader context in which sporting events are evaluated for security and political feasibility.

Procedural Aspects of Tournament Hosting

According to the AHF constitution, the host nation is selected by a majority vote of the executive board, but there are provisions for relocating specific matches to neutral venues when security concerns arise. Previous editions have seen games moved to alternative cities within the host country when required. The AHF has also granted exceptions for nations facing diplomatic constraints, allowing participation through neutral grounds. The decision will be made by the AHF executive board after reviewing the PHF proposal.

Potential Outcomes

If the AHF accepts the request, Pakistan could play its fixtures in a neutral country, ensuring the team’s presence while respecting the political sensitivities. A neutral venue could be arranged in a third nation such as Malaysia, Japan, or even Bangladesh, all of which have hosted international hockey events. Should the AHF decline, Pakistan may consider withdrawing from the tournament, which would reduce the field to eight teams and potentially affect the competitive balance. The precedent set here could influence future cross‑border sporting engagements across other disciplines.

Official Statements

PHF president Mohyuddin Ahmed Wani told reporters that the federation’s priority remains the participation of Pakistani players while safeguarding national interests. He added that the request was made after consultations with the Ministry of Sports and the Pakistan Olympic Association. The AHF has not yet issued a public response, but sources close to the federation indicated that the matter will be discussed at an upcoming meeting of the executive board.

Future Considerations

The outcome of this request will be watched closely by other national federations that face similar geopolitical challenges. It may prompt the AHF to formalize guidelines for neutral venue hosting, balancing the desire for regional competition with the reality of diplomatic tensions. Stakeholders in Pakistani hockey are hopeful that a solution can be found that keeps the tournament intact and allows players to showcase their talent on an international stage.

Conclusion

The PHF’s formal appeal to the AHF reflects the complex interplay between sport and politics in the subcontinent. By seeking either a change of host or a neutral venue for its matches, Pakistan aims to protect its athletes while keeping the Asian Champions Trophy alive. The AHF’s response will shape not only this tournament but also the future of regional hockey diplomacy.
