Shan Masood, Azan Awais Hold Up England In Third Test

By Umer Javed | 11 September 2026 | 5 min read
Photo: Irma Sjachlan / Pexels

Pakistan posted 184 for three at lunch on day three, with Shan Masood and Azan Awais both scoring half‑centuries to deny England a victory at Edgbaston.

Shan Masood and Azan Awais both scored half‑centuries as Pakistan held up England’s victory charge in the third Test at Edgbaston on Friday.

Match Overview

England entered the final day of the third Test needing a rapid over‑run to chase a target set by Pakistan. The home side had posted a total that left the visitors with a demanding 136 runs to add after the first two days had been dominated by the hosts. The match had already seen a rain interruption on Thursday, which had briefly halted play and altered the rhythm of the contest. When the weather cleared, England resumed their chase but found themselves facing a disciplined Pakistani bowling unit that had tightened their lines after an early wobble.

Pakistan appeared to be in serious trouble on the previous evening when they lost two wickets for no runs. The collapse left the hosts at 45 for two, a position that threatened an ignominious two‑day defeat. However, a combination of inclement weather and a stubborn batting display prevented the collapse from becoming a calamity. The rain arrived just as the hosts were struggling, and the interruptions allowed the Pakistani batsmen to settle and rebuild the innings.

Key Performances

Shan Masood, the experienced middle‑order batsman, anchored the recovery with a measured half‑century. He added 125 runs for the third wicket alongside Azan Awais, who also contributed a valuable 50. The partnership was built on solid technique and a willingness to occupy the crease, forcing England to consume overs without gaining much momentum. Masood’s innings was noted for his range of strokes, including a crisp cover drive and a powerful pull that brought boundaries. Awais complemented with a series of defensive strokes that kept the strike rotating and allowed the senior player to take the lead in the partnership.

Their collaboration came at a critical juncture when Pakistan needed runs to avoid a further deficit. By the time the pair were dismissed, they had taken the score past the 150 mark, providing a platform for the lower order to add further runs before lunch. The bowlers who followed, including the seamers and spinners, were able to exploit the fatigue that had set in among the English fielders after a long day of chasing.

England’s Challenge

England’s batting line‑up struggled to generate the required momentum after the rain delay. The early loss of wickets meant that the team had to rely on a flurry of boundaries to catch up. The pitch, which had offered some assistance to the fast bowlers earlier, began to settle into a more balanced surface as the day progressed. England’s top order managed to score at a reasonable rate but could not convert their starts into large totals. The middle order also faced difficulty in rotating the strike, which left the tailenders with an uphill task.

According to reports, the English captain expressed disappointment after the innings, stating that his team needed to improve their application when facing a challenging target. He confirmed that the team would review their batting strategy ahead of the final day, focusing on building partnerships and limiting unnecessary risks. The bowler’s performance was also highlighted, with the Pakistani pace attack extracting important wickets at key moments, which kept England under pressure.

Weather Interference

Rain played a significant role in shaping the day’s events. The interruptions forced both sides to adjust their plans, with England having to accelerate their scoring after each pause. The wet conditions also affected the quality of the ball, making it slightly harder for the bowlers to maintain consistency. However, the Pakistani seamers adapted by using a slightly more aggressive line, which resulted in a few edges that were caught in the slip cordon.

The weather also impacted the field conditions, with patches of moisture making the surface uneven at spots. This added an element of unpredictability, and the Pakistani batsmen had to be careful not to get caught in the rough. The rain, however, proved to be a blessing for Pakistan, as it halted England’s momentum and allowed the hosts to recover from their early setback.

Series Implications

With the third Test still in progress, the series stands at 2‑0 in England’s favour. A further defeat would mean a humiliating 3‑0 whitewash for Pakistan, a scenario that the team is keen to avoid. The performance on Friday has injected new confidence into the Pakistani side, showing that they still have the ability to resist strong opposition. The experience gained in this match could prove valuable in the remaining fixtures, if any, and in future series planning.

Analysts noted that the resilience shown by Masood and Awais could set a precedent for how Pakistan approaches high‑pressure situations. The half‑century partnership demonstrated that disciplined batting, combined with effective use of the available resources, can level the playing field even against a top-ranked side like England. The series outcome will likely depend on Pakistan’s ability to replicate similar performances in any subsequent matches.

Future Outlook

Looking ahead, Pakistan will need to build on the foundations laid in Edgbaston. The team’s coaching staff have confirmed that they will focus on strengthening the middle order and improving the consistency of the bowling unit. Players have indicated a determination to avoid a clean sweep and to leave the series on a more competitive note. The upcoming fixtures, if any, will serve as a platform for the side to showcase their resurgence and to regain confidence ahead of the next international schedule.

The match also highlighted the importance of adaptability in modern cricket, where weather, pitch conditions, and opposition tactics can shift rapidly. Both teams will be keen to analyze the performance data and adjust their strategies accordingly. As the series progresses, fans can expect continued excitement, with Pakistan aiming to prove that the result at Edgbaston was not an isolated incident but the start of a turnaround.
