HEC Invites Applications for GRE Training for US Scholarship

Launch of the GRE Preparation Initiative

The Higher Education Commission of Pakistan has officially opened the application process for individuals interested in receiving comprehensive GRE training. This initiative is designed to support the broader US-Pakistan Knowledge Corridor PhD Scholarship Programme. According to ARY News, the commission is seeking applications from talented nationals residing in Pakistan as well as those from Azad Jammu and Kashmir who wish to improve their academic prospects for doctoral studies in the United States. The announcement was shared on social media, detailing the opening of the application window for prospective scholars.

This training program is being offered in an in-person format to ensure that participants receive direct guidance. Interested candidates are encouraged to review the specific criteria and submit their applications before the designated cutoff date of December 13, 2026. The training sessions are set to take place in five major urban centers across the country, specifically in Islamabad, Karachi, Lahore, Peshawar, and Quetta. This geographic distribution aims to accommodate candidates from various provinces and administrative regions without requiring excessive travel for remote participants.

The initiative highlights the ongoing efforts by educational authorities to prepare local students for the rigorous testing requirements associated with international university admissions. Standardized tests often present a hurdle for applicants who may possess strong academic records but lack familiarity with the specific format of the graduate record examination. By organizing these sessions, the commission intends to bridge the gap and encourage a higher volume of competitive applications from qualified individuals across the country.

Prospective applicants are advised to consult the official web portal of the Higher Education Commission for comprehensive guidance on how to complete the submission process. Meeting the administrative requirements is a necessary first step for anyone hoping to secure a spot in the upcoming training cohorts. Officials have not indicated any intentions to extend the submission window, which means that timely completion of the paperwork remains vital for all interested parties.

Background of the Knowledge Corridor

The US-Pakistan Knowledge Corridor represents a long-term academic partnership between the two nations. This initiative originated from discussions held in June 2015 under the Education, Science and Technology Working Group of the Bilateral Strategic Dialogue. The primary objective of the program is to facilitate the enrollment of 10,000 Pakistani scholars in American universities over a ten-year duration. By doing so, the program aims to enhance the research and academic capabilities of Pakistan in various specialized fields.

The formal approval for the first phase of this initiative was granted by the Executive Committee of the National Economic Council on January 25, 2017. Following this initial step, the project underwent a revision in August 2020. Under this revised framework, the government aims to provide 1,000 doctoral scholarships to high-achieving students. These scholars will be placed in universities across the United States to pursue advanced degrees, contributing to the overall development of the nation's human capital.

Bilateral educational frameworks of this scale require continuous coordination between domestic institutions and foreign academic bodies. Over the years, policymakers have viewed higher education as a vital instrument for national advancement, particularly in technical and scientific disciplines. The structured approach of the Knowledge Corridor reflects a concerted effort to align domestic academic output with international standards of excellence.

As the project progresses through its approved phases, authorities continue to monitor the enrollment and performance of participating scholars. The integration of preparatory training like the GRE classes represents an evolution in how support is delivered to candidates before they enter the international admissions arena. This foundational work is expected to yield positive results as subsequent cohorts prepare for placement abroad.

Eligibility Criteria for Prospective Applicants

To qualify for this GRE training, applicants must meet several academic and professional standards set by the Higher Education Commission. Candidates are required to hold a four-year bachelor degree or its equivalent, which constitutes at least 16 years of formal schooling. The commission has clarified that throughout an applicant's entire academic history, they are allowed a maximum of one second division or an equivalent grade. This high threshold reflects the competitive nature of the scholarship program that follows the training.

There are also specific age requirements for those applying to the program. For regular government employees, faculty members at public-sector universities, staff at government colleges, and researchers at approved development organizations, the maximum age limit is 40 years on the closing date of the application. Also, the commission has specified that individuals who are currently benefiting from any other scholarship are not eligible to apply. These rules ensure that the training resources are directed toward those who are fully prepared to commit to the doctoral scholarship path.

The inclusion of strict academic thresholds is intended to filter candidates based on their past performance, ensuring that only those with consistent records proceed. Academic consistency is viewed by selection panels as a reliable indicator of a student's ability to cope with the demands of a rigorous doctoral program overseas. The limitation regarding existing scholarships also ensures fairness, allowing opportunities to be distributed among a wider pool of deserving candidates.

Applicants from various professional backgrounds are encouraged to review these requirements carefully before initiating their paperwork. Minor discrepancies in educational records can lead to disqualification during the initial screening phases managed by the commission. Ensuring compliance with every listed rule protects the applicant's time and allows the administrative staff to process submissions more efficiently.
