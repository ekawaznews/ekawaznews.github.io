Afghan Medical Graduates File Petitions in Lahore High Court Against PMDC Return Orders

By Umer Javed | 19 September 2026 | 4 min read
Photo: Abdellah Benziane / Pexels

Afghan medical graduates have filed petitions in Lahore High Court challenging Pakistan Medical and Dental Council's orders to return them to Afghanistan. The case highlights tensions over professional recognition and regulatory compliance.

More Afghan medical graduates have filed petitions with the Lahore High Court challenging the Pakistan Medical and Dental Council’s decision to order them to return to Afghanistan. The move follows a series of regulatory actions that have raised questions about the status of foreign‑trained doctors in Pakistan’s health sector.

Background

Since the early 2000s, Pakistan has hosted a significant number of Afghan medical students who completed their degrees in Pakistani institutions. Many of these graduates have been employed in public and private hospitals across the country. Over time, a number of them have sought to register with the Pakistan Medical and Dental Council (PMDC) in order to practice officially. However, the council has maintained strict criteria for foreign‑qualified doctors, including proof of registration in their home country and compliance with additional licensing requirements.

PMDC Orders

In late 2022, the PMDC issued a directive ordering Afghan medical graduates who had not met its registration criteria to return to Afghanistan. The council cited concerns over the legitimacy of their qualifications and the need to safeguard the quality of medical practice. The orders were announced in a public statement that highlighted the council’s commitment to uphold national standards for medical professionals.

Court Interventions

Earlier this year, the Lahore High Court ruled in favour of a group of Afghan doctors who had challenged the PMDC’s decision. The court acknowledged the rights of foreign‑trained doctors to practice in Pakistan, provided they met certain procedural requirements. According to reports, the court emphasized the importance of fair treatment and due process for all medical professionals, regardless of nationality.

Current Petitions

The latest wave of petitions comes from a new cohort of Afghan graduates who were not represented in the earlier case. The petitioners argue that the PMDC’s return orders violate their right to work in the country where they earned their degrees and where they have been practicing. They request the court to declare the orders void and to allow them to register with the council under a revised framework.

Reactions

The Lahore High Court has confirmed that it will hear the petitions on a scheduled date in the coming weeks. The court’s spokesperson said that the court will examine the legal merits of the case and the procedural steps taken by the PMDC. The PMDC, on its part, stated that it remains committed to enforcing its regulations and that it will continue to monitor the compliance of all medical practitioners.

Healthcare Implications

Pakistan’s health sector has relied on the services of Afghan doctors, especially in rural and underserved areas. The potential removal of these practitioners could strain the already stretched medical workforce. Hospitals that have employed Afghan graduates have expressed concern over the continuity of patient care, especially in specialties where these doctors have specialised.

Legal Context

The legal framework governing medical registration in Pakistan is governed by the PMDC Act of 1962 and subsequent amendments. The act outlines the qualifications required for registration and the procedures for foreign‑qualified doctors. The Lahore High Court’s decision to intervene in previous cases demonstrates the judiciary’s willingness to scrutinise regulatory actions that may affect professional rights.

Possible Outcomes

Should the Lahore High Court rule in favour of the Afghan graduates, the PMDC may be required to amend its registration procedures to accommodate foreign‑trained doctors who meet specific criteria. A contrary ruling could reinforce the council’s authority to enforce its return orders, potentially leading to a larger exodus of Afghan medical staff. Either outcome will set a precedent for how Pakistan handles foreign professionals in regulated fields.

Expert Opinion

Medical education specialists have noted that the case highlights broader challenges in balancing national regulatory standards with the realities of a globalised workforce. According to experts, a transparent and inclusive registration process would benefit both the healthcare system and the professionals involved. They also warn that abrupt enforcement of return orders could damage Pakistan’s reputation as a destination for medical education.

Looking Ahead

The forthcoming hearing by the Lahore High Court will be closely watched by medical practitioners, regulatory bodies, and policymakers. The outcome will influence not only the immediate status of Afghan graduates but also the future framework for foreign‑qualified doctors in Pakistan. The court’s decision is expected to clarify the legal boundaries of the PMDC’s authority and to address the concerns of a growing community of medical professionals who have chosen Pakistan as their training ground.
