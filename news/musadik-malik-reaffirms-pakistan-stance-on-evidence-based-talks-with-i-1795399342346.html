Musadik Malik Reaffirms Pakistan Stance on Evidence-Based Talks with India

Ministerial Participation at Regional Dialogue in New Delhi

Federal Minister Musadik Malik has arrived in New Delhi to represent Pakistan at the SCO Civilisational Dialogue Forum. The three-day gathering brings together delegates to engage in regional discussions hosted in the Indian capital. This visit marks a notable moment in cross-border diplomatic activity, as it represents the first time a Pakistani minister has traveled to India since former foreign minister Bilawal Bhutto-Zardari attended a Shanghai Cooperation Organisation event in May 2023. Observers of regional affairs are paying close attention to the visit to assess the state of contact between the two neighboring countries.

The arrival of a senior cabinet member in New Delhi provides a rare occasion for direct communication, even if primarily situated within the framework of a multilateral convention. For several years, formal high-level bilateral engagements have been scarce, leaving international and regional forums as the main avenues for interaction. The decision to participate in this three-day event demonstrates an intent to maintain representation in regional frameworks where shared concerns and diplomatic stances can be expressed.

Media attention surrounding the minister's presence has been high since his arrival. The visit allows Pakistani officials to articulate their official positions directly while present on Indian soil. News coverage of the event highlights how multilateral platforms often serve as vital bridgepoints when standard bilateral channels remain constrained. By attending the forum, the Pakistani delegation ensures that its perspective is conveyed in person to both regional counterparts and the international press corps gathered in the capital.

Insistence on Verifiable Proof for Terrorism Discussions

During his interactions with reporters in New Delhi, Minister Musadik Malik articulated the fundamental prerequisite for engaging in bilateral talks concerning terrorism. According to Dawn, the federal minister emphasized that any future discussions between Pakistan and India on the subject of terrorism must be "based on evidence". This position reflects a policy framework that prioritizes verifiable facts over unproven statements, aiming to construct a clear foundation for any potential dialogue between the neighboring nations.

As reported by ARY News, the minister clarified that Pakistan stands ready for talks with India provided those talks are grounded in concrete evidence rather than simple allegations. This distinction forms a central element of Pakistan's diplomatic message. By specifying that discussions must rely on substantiated facts, the government seeks to ensure that any future dialogue remains structured and focused on documented realities rather than rhetoric that has previously stalled progress.

Video footage circulated by the Indian news agency ANI on Sunday showed the minister answering questions from journalists regarding the prospects of dialogue. Speaking to the media, Malik noted that he had repeatedly set forth Pakistan's official stance to the press during his visit. He made it clear that establishing a process anchored in tangible proof is essential if both countries are to move toward addressing mutual security concerns effectively and transparently.

Addressing Outstanding Disputes and Regional Stability

Beyond the subject of security and terrorism, Pakistan has consistently expressed its willingness to engage in comprehensive talks with India to resolve a range of unresolved bilateral issues. Among these long-standing matters, the water issue remains a prominent point of concern for Islamabad. The minister's visit to New Delhi serves as a platform to reiterate this broader willingness to talk, signaling that Pakistan maintains an open stance on negotiating solutions to essential disputes that affect both populations.

Water management and resource sharing have historically formed a major component of bilateral discussions between the two countries. By bringing attention to outstanding disputes including water, Pakistan shows the necessity of addressing basic structural grievances alongside security matters. Official communications during the visit reflect the belief that long-term stability in South Asia requires an approach that does not ignore core regional issues that directly impact everyday life and economic conditions.

Analysts may point out that Pakistan's ongoing offer for dialogue highlights a preference for peaceful negotiations to manage cross-border frictions. However, the requirement for evidence remains a central boundary for discussions on security matters. By presenting these positions together, the government aims to show that while it remains prepared to talk about all unresolved disputes, it expects discussions on sensitive security issues to adhere to established standards of proof.

Direct Remarks to Media and the Pursuit of Lasting Solutions

In his comments to reporters on Sunday, Minister Musadik Malik offered a firm stance on how discussions should proceed if New Delhi is interested in achieving progress. According to Dawn, the minister affirmed, "I am giving you a stable response that whenever discussions take place, they will be based on evidence, and they will certainly happen." This direct statement was delivered to clear up ambiguity regarding Pakistan's readiness to talk under the right conditions.

The minister explained that if New Delhi truly seeks a "lasting solution" to the underlying tensions that affect the region, it must approach dialogue with a willingness to present and examine verifiable evidence. He reiterated that discussions are not being avoided by Islamabad, but rather that the framework for those discussions must be logical, stable, and fair. This message emphasizes the idea that lasting peace cannot be built on unsubstantiated accusations.

By delivering these statements directly to the local and international press in New Delhi, the minister ensured that Pakistan's perspective was recorded clearly. The use of direct media engagement during official visits allows cabinet members to outline official policy without reliance on third-party interpretation. The minister's repeated assertions to reporters demonstrate a coordinated effort to keep the focus on factual substantiation as the primary condition for progress.

The Role of Multilateral Diplomacy in South Asia

The SCO Civilisational Dialogue Forum provides a structural environment where member countries can participate in cultural and diplomatic exchanges even when direct bilateral relations are cold. For Pakistan, participating in such forums in India offers a double function: fulfilling regional institutional obligations while providing an occasion for diplomatic messaging. The visit of Musadik Malik follows the precedent set in May 2023 when former foreign minister Bilawal Bhutto-Zardari traveled to India for an SCO meeting.

Multilateral platforms like the Shanghai Cooperation Organisation have become vital tools for maintaining a baseline level of contact between South Asian neighbors. When official bilateral summits are not taking place, these regional gatherings offer neutral ground where officials can share space and present their respective foreign policy frameworks. Observers note that such visits prevent total diplomatic silence and keep open the potential for future interaction.

For the general public in Pakistan, the dynamics of relations with India remain a subject of deep concern due to their direct influence on regional peace and economic stability. Maintaining representation at multilateral forums in India reassures citizens that the government is actively representing national interests abroad. It demonstrates a commitment to participating in regional affairs while firmly maintaining established national positions on security and bilateral disputes.

Assessing the Trajectory and What Lies Ahead

As the three-day SCO forum proceeds in the Indian capital, political commentators and diplomatic experts will be watching to see if any secondary interactions or responses emerge from the host nation. The minister's clear assertion that talks "will certainly happen" if grounded in evidence sets a clear precedent for how future contact might be established. The key question remains whether Indian authorities will choose to respond to this emphasis on evidence-based dialogue.

The outcome of this visit could influence how diplomatic engagements are structured in the coming months. While one single visit to a multilateral conference may not immediately resolve deep-seated tensions, it establishes a public record of Pakistan's position on Indian soil. Policy experts may observe whether this interaction opens pathways for further technical or diplomatic exchanges, or whether the relationship will remain steady at its current status.

Looking ahead, the focus will turn to the conclusion of the forum and the return of the Pakistani delegation. Media outlets across the region will continue to monitor official statements for any indications of follow-up communication. Whether this trip becomes a reference point for renewed dialogue or stands as an isolated event within multilateral diplomacy, Minister Musadik Malik's statements in New Delhi have firmly established evidence as the core prerequisite for future talks between the two countries.
