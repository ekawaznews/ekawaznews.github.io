Introduction to the Four-Day National Weather Outlook

Welcome to the Ek Awaz News weather outlook for Wednesday, October 14, 2026. This report examines the upcoming four-day meteorological patterns across major urban centers in Pakistan, utilizing official forecast data from Open-Meteo. Understanding these weather shifts is essential for planning daily routines, managing agricultural activities, and ensuring public health safety as seasonal transitions unfold.

Weather conditions vary significantly across different regions of the country, ranging from coastal warmth to northern rainfall and mountain chill. In this comprehensive review, we examine the city-by-city forecasts for Karachi, Lahore, Islamabad, Peshawar, Quetta, Multan, and Gilgit. Following the regional breakdown, we analyze what these atmospheric states mean for travel, farming, health, and everyday life, while noting specific warnings attributed to reporting outlets.

City-by-City Forecast Breakdown for Karachi and Lahore

Turning first to the southern port city of Karachi, the forecast indicates warm conditions with varied cloud cover. On October 14, the city expects a high of 35 degrees Celsius and a low of 26 degrees Celsius with partly cloudy skies and a zero percent chance of rain. October 15 brings a high of 33 degrees Celsius and a low of 26 degrees Celsius under similar partly cloudy conditions and no rain probability. By October 16, temperatures are projected to reach a high of 35 degrees Celsius and a low of 24 degrees Celsius with fog and a six percent chance of rain, followed on October 17 by a high of 35 degrees Celsius, a low of 25 degrees Celsius, partly cloudy skies, and a six percent rain chance.

In connection with Karachi, Dawn reported that the city experienced hot weather on Tuesday as the temperature rose to 38.7 degrees Celsius with humidity recorded at 43 per cent, making conditions uncomfortable for people. A Met official told Dawn that October is usually a warm month in Karachi and is often referred to as the city second summer. The official added that the recent rise in temperatures is linked to a high pressure area in the upper atmosphere, which has weakened winds and caused temperatures to rise. The official warned that the mercury could touch 40 degrees Celsius if the current trend continues. Dawn also noted the official statement that October is generally marked by warm weather in Karachi, even as the summer season begins to move towards cooler months, and that the high temperatures combined with humidity levels of 43 per cent have added to the discomfort, particularly during the daytime.

Moving eastward to Lahore, the meteorological outlook shows active precipitation early in the period. On October 14, Lahore expects a high of 31 degrees Celsius and a low of 22 degrees Celsius with drizzle and a 53 percent chance of rain. October 15 features thunderstorms with a high of 29 degrees Celsius, a low of 20 degrees Celsius, and a 71 percent probability of precipitation. Conditions clear up by October 16, registering a high of 28 degrees Celsius, a low of 19 degrees Celsius, partly cloudy skies, and a two percent chance of rain. Finally, October 17 brings clear skies with a high of 29 degrees Celsius, a low of 19 degrees Celsius, and zero percent rain probability.

October 17 concludes the period with clear skies, a high of 27 degrees Celsius, a low of 17 degrees Celsius, and a two percent rain chance.

October 15 shifts to partly cloudy skies with a high of 27 degrees Celsius, a low of 16 degrees Celsius, and a two percent rain probability. October 16 and October 17 both maintain partly cloudy conditions with highs reaching 28 degrees Celsius, lows dropping to 16 degrees Celsius and 15 degrees Celsius respectively, and zero percent chance of rain on both days.

Further west in Quetta, cooler and stable weather persists throughout the four-day span. October 15 shows a high of 23 degrees Celsius and a low of 11 degrees Celsius with partly cloudy conditions and no rain. October 17 finishes the sequence with clear skies, a high of 25 degrees Celsius, a low of 10 degrees Celsius, and a zero percent chance of rain.

Implications for Travel, Farming, Health, and Daily Life

The projected weather conditions carry direct consequences for daily routines, travel safety, agricultural planning, and public health across Pakistan. In cities facing rain, thunderstorms, and drizzle such as Lahore, Islamabad, Peshawar, Multan, and Gilgit, wet roads could present travel challenges, leading analysts to suggest that commuters and drivers exercise caution. Fog predicted in Karachi on October 16 may similarly affect local transit visibility. Conversely, clear and partly cloudy skies in Quetta and later days in other regions should support smooth travel conditions.

For the agricultural sector, rainfall and drizzle in areas like Lahore, Islamabad, Multan, and Gilgit provide necessary moisture for soil preparation and crop hydration, though heavy downpours or thunderstorms could pose risks to vulnerable crops. Farmers in these regions may need to monitor field drainage closely. Meanwhile, persistent warmth and humidity in Karachi, where Dawn reported temperatures reaching 38.7 degrees Celsius and potential spikes toward 40 degrees Celsius, highlight health considerations related to heat stress and discomfort, advising residents to stay hydrated during daytime hours.
