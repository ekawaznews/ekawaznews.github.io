Nepra Clears $58bn 11-Year Power Plan Amid Major Reservations

By Umer Javed | 12 September 2026 | 6 min read
Photo: Tope J. Asokere / Pexels

The National Electric Power Regulatory Authority (Nepra) has conditionally approved the Integrated System Plan 2025, a $58 billion, 11‑year investment in power generation and transmission. The decision drew strong dissent from all three board members, who questioned project selections and the bypass of the Council of Common Interests.

The National Electric Power Regulatory Authority (Nepra) on Friday gave its conditional approval to the Integrated System Plan (ISP) 2025, a sprawling 11‑year roadmap that earmarks roughly $58 billion for Pakistan’s power generation and transmission infrastructure. The 45‑page decision, issued after a heated deliberation, marks a key moment for the country’s energy sector, but it also opens a fresh chapter of debate among regulators, policymakers and industry observers.

Approval and Investment Scope

The plan, covering the period from 2025 to 2035, is designed to address the persistent gap between electricity demand and supply that has plagued Pakistan for years. According to the decision, the investment will be spread across new generation projects, upgrades to existing plants, and the expansion of transmission networks. The authority framed the approval as a step toward achieving energy security and reducing the reliance on costly imported fuels.

Nepra’s chairman, while reading the decision, noted that the plan aligns with the government’s broader economic objectives. He said the conditional approval reflects a balance between the urgent need for capacity addition and the requirement to ensure fiscal prudence. The conditionality, however, is tied to a series of performance benchmarks and compliance requirements that the implementing agencies must meet before the projects can proceed.

Dissent and Reservations

What sets this approval apart is the unprecedented level of internal disagreement. All three members of the Nepra board, including the chairman, authored separate advisory notes that together exceed 12 pages. In these notes, the members questioned the rationale behind the inclusion and exclusion of specific projects. They expressed concern that the selection process lacked transparency and that critical assessments were omitted.

One board member stated that the plan’s financial projections appeared optimistic and that the risk of cost overruns was not adequately addressed. Another member highlighted the absence of a clear strategy for integrating renewable energy sources into the national grid. The chairman’s own note, while supporting the overall vision, warned that bypassing established oversight mechanisms could set a problematic precedent.

Bypass of the Council of Common Interests

The most contentious aspect of the decision is the reported bypass of the Council of Common Interests (CCI). The CCI, a constitutional body, is mandated to review projects of national significance to ensure they serve the interests of all provinces. The dissenting notes argue that the ISP 2025 should have undergone a similar multi‑province review before final approval.

According to reports, the council’s procedural requirements were not followed, prompting accusations of unilateral decision‑making. Critics within the political arena have called for a rehearing before the CCI, warning that such a move could undermine federal‑provincial cooperation on critical infrastructure. The government, however, maintains that the urgency of the energy crisis justified a streamlined approval process.

Background of Pakistan’s Power Sector

Pakistan’s electricity sector has historically struggled with a combination of insufficient generation capacity, aging infrastructure and frequent transmission losses. The country’s installed capacity hovers around 40 GW, while peak demand regularly exceeds 45 GW, leading to rolling blackouts and reliance on expensive diesel generation.

Over the past decade, successive governments have launched various initiatives to boost renewable energy, improve grid efficiency and attract private investment. The ISP 2025 is positioned as the latest in a series of strategic plans, building on earlier frameworks such as the National Electricity Policy 2017 and the Renewable Energy Development Plan. The new plan seeks to add roughly 20 GW of generation capacity, with a strong emphasis on gas‑fired and hydel projects, while also allocating funds for transmission upgrades.

Key Projects and Their Controversies

Among the projects highlighted in the ISP 2025 are a large‑scale coal‑fired power plant in Sindh, an expansion of the Karakoram hydro complex in Gilgit‑Baltistan and a new gas‑to‑liquid pipeline linking the north‑west to the southern grid. The inclusion of the coal plant has drawn criticism from environmental groups, while the hydro expansion faces scrutiny over potential displacement of local communities.

The dissenting members argued that the plan’s cost‑benefit analysis for these projects was incomplete. They pointed out that the projected revenue streams from the coal plant were based on assumptions about coal prices that were volatile and uncertain. Similarly, the hydro project’s timeline was questioned, with concerns that monsoon variability could delay commissioning beyond the planned schedule.

Economic Implications

Financing a $58 billion program over 11‑years will require a mix of domestic resources, foreign direct investment and possibly International Monetary Fund support. The plan’s success hinges on securing affordable capital and maintaining macroeconomic stability. Analysts estimate that the additional generation capacity could reduce the country’s electricity import bill by several billion dollars annually, providing a significant relief to the national balance of payments.

However, the dissenting notes caution that the plan’s heavy reliance on gas‑fired generation could expose Pakistan to price volatility in international markets. They recommend accelerating renewable energy deployment to diversify the energy mix and hedge against fuel price shocks. The government has indicated that renewable targets will be integrated gradually, but the timeline remains a point of contention.

Political and Federal‑Provincial Dynamics

The bypass of the CCI has ignited a political firestorm, with provincial governments accusing the federal authority of overstepping its mandate. Sindh and Balochistan have demanded a seat at the table, arguing that the energy plan must respect provincial autonomy and address regional development needs.

In a recent briefing, a provincial minister told reporters that the plan’s benefits should be equitably distributed and that any project affecting local ecosystems must undergo rigorous environmental impact assessments. The federal ministry of energy, for its part, stated that the urgency of the power crisis necessitated swift action and that the plan includes provisions for provincial participation in project governance.

Future Outlook and Implementation Challenges

Even with Nepra’s conditional approval, the ISP 2025 faces a mountain of implementation challenges. Building the required infrastructure will demand coordinated effort among multiple agencies, including the Ministry of Energy, the Water and Power Development Authority and private sector players. Delays in land acquisition, regulatory clearances and financing agreements could derail the ambitious timeline.

Observers warn that the dissenting views within Nepra signal deeper systemic issues that could affect future regulatory decisions. If the authority’s internal disagreements are not addressed, they may erode confidence among investors and stakeholders. Conversely, a transparent and inclusive implementation process could turn the plan into a model for large‑scale infrastructure projects in Pakistan.

Going forward, the government is expected to issue detailed implementing regulations and a project‑wise roadmap. Meanwhile, the opposition in parliament has pledged to monitor the plan’s progress and demand regular briefings from the relevant ministries. The outcome of these efforts will likely shape Pakistan’s energy for the next decade and beyond.
