Pakistan's Table Tennis Teams Suffer Humiliating Start at Asian Games

By Umer Javed | 21 September 2026 | 5 min read

Pakistan's table tennis teams endured a chastening opening to their Asian Games campaign, with the women's side failing to win a single match in the group stage and the men's team also going down in both of their opening ties. The results raise serious questions about the state of the sport in the country heading into the continental multi-sport event.

Women's Team Crushed in All Three Group Matches

The women's team completed a miserable campaign on Monday with a 3-0 defeat to India in their final Group F match at the Sky Hall Toyota. The loss sealed their exit from the group stage in disappointing fashion, coming just days after identical losses to Singapore and Indonesia on Sunday. In all three outings, the Pakistani pairings were unable to secure even a single game, highlighting a significant gap in class against their continental opponents.

Against India, 15-year-old Aleena Khan was dispatched in just 10 minutes, losing 11-3, 11-3, 11-4 to the experienced Sutirtha Mukherjee. The result was a brutal introduction to the level of competition at the Asian Games for the young Pakistani player. Kulsoom Khan, 19, also faced a difficult task in her outing and was unable to turn the contest in Pakistan's favour. The Indian pairings proved far too accomplished, and the Pakistani players were left searching for answers against the pace and precision of their opponents.

The defeat to Singapore on Sunday had already set the tone for the women's campaign. The team was then handed another heavy loss by Indonesia, leaving them with little to play for in the final group match against India. The three consecutive losses paint a picture of a team that was outclassed in every department, from serve and receive to rally play and tactical awareness.

Men's Team Also Loses Both Opening Ties

The men's team did not fare any better, losing both of their opening ties in the group stage. While specific details of those matches were not fully available from the early sessions, the results confirm that Pakistan's men's side also struggled to compete at the Asian Games level. The defeats mean the men's team faces an uphill battle to salvage any hope of progressing further in the tournament.

The Asian Games table tennis competition draws top-ranked players from across the continent, including China, Japan, South Korea, and India, all of whom regularly produce medal contenders at the world and Olympic levels. For Pakistan, a nation with a rich sporting history in other disciplines, table tennis has often struggled to attract the same level of investment and attention. The results at this edition of the Asian Games reflect that reality on the court.

Background: The State of Table Tennis in Pakistan

Table tennis in Pakistan operates within a challenging sporting. The Pakistan Table Tennis Federation governs the sport at the national level, organising domestic tournaments and selecting squads for international events. However, the federation has long operated with limited resources compared to its counterparts in India, China, and several other Asian nations. Access to high-quality training facilities, coaching expertise, and competitive domestic schedules remains a persistent challenge for Pakistani players.

Pakistan has historically produced individual players of promise, but the absence of a structured pipeline from grassroots to elite level has hindered consistent progress at major tournaments. Several Pakistani players have competed at the Commonwealth Games and the South Asian Federation Games over the years, with occasional medal-winning performances offering brief moments of positivity. However, the Asian Games represent a far more demanding stage, where the best players from across the continent compete for honours.

The women's team's participation at the Asian Games was itself a notable milestone, representing the country's commitment to developing the women's game. The involvement of young players such as Aleena Khan indicates that the federation is investing in youth, even if the results at this tournament have been harsh. The experience gained at this level will be invaluable for the development of these players as they move forward in their careers.

Looking Ahead for Pakistani Table Tennis

The Asian Games results are a sobering reminder of the work that lies ahead for Pakistani table tennis. The gap between Pakistan and the top Asian nations is significant, and closing it will require sustained investment in coaching, infrastructure, and competitive opportunities for players at all levels. International exposure through tournaments such as the World Championships, ITTF World Tour events, and bilateral series will be essential in helping Pakistani players gain the experience needed to compete with the best.

The federation has in recent years taken steps to promote the sport domestically, with efforts to introduce table tennis programmes in schools and clubs across the country. These initiatives, if supported over the long term, could help build a broader talent base. For now, however, the focus remains on regrouping after a difficult campaign and preparing for future opportunities to represent Pakistan on the continental stage.

The players and officials will reflect on the lessons of this tournament as they look to build towards the next major international event. The dedication shown by the young members of the squad, including Aleena Khan and Kulsoom Khan, will be important in shaping the future of the sport in Pakistan.
