Official Announcement of Fuel Price Revisions

The government of Pakistan has officially declared a reduction in the ex-depot prices for petrol and high-speed diesel. This decision, which comes into effect on Friday, November 13, 2026, marks a change in the cost of energy resources for the public. According to the Ministry of Energy and the Oil and Gas Regulatory Authority, these price adjustments are meant to reflect the current state of the fuel market. The notification confirms that the cost of petrol has been reduced by three rupees per litre, while high-speed diesel has seen a more significant decrease of four rupees per litre.

Daily Pakistan reported that following these adjustments, the new price for petrol in Pakistan stands at three hundred and thirty-two rupees per litre. Meanwhile, the price for high-speed diesel has slipped to three hundred and sixty rupees per litre. This development has been widely covered by various news organizations, including Dawn and Geo News, which confirmed the specific reductions applied to both fuel types. The process of adjusting these rates is a standard procedure handled by the regulatory bodies to align domestic costs with broader market trends.

Citizens across the nation pay close attention to these periodic notifications because petroleum products form a foundational element of the economy. The Ministry of Energy issues these directives after thorough evaluations conducted alongside technical experts. When the Oil and Gas Regulatory Authority finalizes its calculations, the formal paperwork is dispatched to relevant departments for immediate implementation at retail outlets. Understanding the formal steps behind these announcements helps the public contextualize why rates fluctuate from time to time.

The timing of the implementation is specified clearly in the official documents to prevent any confusion among fuel station owners and consumers. Retailers are expected to adopt the new pricing structures promptly upon the arrival of the effective date. Local authorities often monitor compliance to verify that pumps are charging the correct rates. This administrative oversight is designed to protect consumers from overcharging and to maintain order within the retail energy sector.
