Arshad Nadeem Finishes Fourth as Pathirage Claims Javelin Gold in Nagoya

By Umer Javed | 29 September 2026 | 5 min read
Photo: Elkhan Ganiyev / Pexels

Pakistan's javelin star Arshad Nadeem secured a fourth-place finish at the Asian Games final in Nagoya on Monday, despite achieving a season-best throw of 80.29 metres. Sri Lanka's Rumesh Pathirage, the World No. 1, won the gold medal in a rain-affected competition.

Arshad Nadeem, Pakistan's Olympic champion javelin thrower, finished fourth at the Asian Games final in Nagoya on Monday, despite achieving a season-best throw of 80.29 metres. The event, held at the Nagoya City Mizuho Park Athletic Stadium, saw Sri Lanka's Rumesh Pathirage claim the gold medal amidst challenging rain-soaked conditions.

Nadeem, who entered the competition with high hopes, faced a difficult start under heavy skies and on a slick runway, factors that significantly impacted performance for many athletes. His initial attempt measured 76.69 metres, placing him outside the medal contention early in the final.

The second attempt proved even more challenging for the Pakistani athlete, resulting in a foul. This put considerable pressure on Nadeem, requiring a substantial improvement in his subsequent throws to remain competitive against a strong field of international javelin throwers.

Demonstrating his resolve, Nadeem delivered a powerful third throw, which significantly improved his standing. This attempt soared to 80.29 metres, marking his season's best performance and elevating him to fourth position. The throw showcased his ability to respond under pressure and his continued dedication to the sport.

However, the momentum from his impressive third throw could not be sustained. Following this season-best effort, Nadeem recorded three consecutive fouls in his subsequent attempts. These fouls ultimately brought an end to his challenge for a medal, settling him in fourth place.

The gold medal was secured by Sri Lanka's Rumesh Pathirage, the reigning World No. 1 and pre-event favourite. Pathirage lived up to his billing, delivering a dominant performance that saw him clinch the top honour in the javelin final.

The Impact of Adverse Weather Conditions

The javelin final in Nagoya was contested under particularly challenging weather conditions, with heavy rain and a slick runway significantly influencing the athletes' performances. Javelin throwing is a highly technical discipline that relies heavily on a precise run-up, a stable plant, and a powerful, controlled release.

A wet track can make the run-up treacherous, increasing the risk of slips and reducing the explosive power athletes can generate from their legs. The slick surface also affects the final plant, where the thrower braces to transfer momentum to the javelin, potentially leading to instability and a loss of accuracy and distance.

also, the rain can make the javelin itself slippery, complicating the grip and release. This demands exceptional control and adaptability from the throwers, as even a slight miscalculation due to a compromised grip can result in a foul or a significantly shorter throw. The heavy skies and reduced visibility could also have played a role in the athletes' concentration and depth perception.

For an athlete like Arshad Nadeem, who relies on meticulous technique and explosive power, these conditions present a formidable obstacle. His ability to still achieve a season-best throw of 80.29 metres under such circumstances speaks volumes about his skill and resilience, even if it was not enough for a podium finish on this occasion.

Arshad Nadeem's Standing in Pakistani Athletics

Arshad Nadeem holds a distinguished position in Pakistan's sporting. As an Olympic champion, he has brought unprecedented global recognition to Pakistani athletics, particularly in a field event that traditionally has seen limited international success for the nation. His achievements have inspired countless young athletes across the country.

Nadeem's journey to becoming an Olympic champion has been a source of immense national pride. His dedication, discipline, and consistent efforts have made him a national hero, with the hopes of an entire nation often resting on his shoulders during major international competitions. His presence at the Asian Games final highlighted the high expectations placed upon him by sports enthusiasts and officials alike.

His performance in Nagoya, despite not yielding a medal, highlights the continuing challenge and competitive nature of elite international sports. Competing against the world's best, including a dominant figure like Rumesh Pathirage, demands peak physical condition, mental fortitude, and a measure of luck with external factors such as weather.

The Significance of the Asian Games

The Asian Games represent a major multi-sport event held every four years among athletes from all over Asia. It is the second-largest multi-sport event after the Olympic Games, providing a significant platform for athletes to test their abilities against continental rivals and gain valuable experience on the international stage.

For many participating nations, including Pakistan, the Asian Games serve as a benchmark for athletic development and a stepping stone towards global championships and the Olympics. Medals won at these games are a source of national prestige and often lead to increased support and recognition for the athletes and their respective sports federations.

The javelin event, in particular, attracts strong competition from various Asian countries known for their athletic prowess. Securing a top-four finish in such a competitive environment, even without a medal, reflects the high standard of performance required and the significant effort put in by athletes like Arshad Nadeem.

Looking Ahead for Pakistani Athletics

Arshad Nadeem's performance at the Asian Games, while ending in a fourth-place finish, provides valuable insights for his future training and competition strategy. The season-best throw of 80.29 metres demonstrates that his form is strong, and he remains a formidable competitor on the international circuit.

For Pakistani athletics, Nadeem's continued participation at this elite level is vital. His presence inspires new generations of athletes and draws attention to the need for sustained investment in sports infrastructure, coaching, and athlete support programmes within the country. Ensuring access to state-of-the-art training facilities, sports science expertise, and consistent international exposure will be essential for Nadeem and other aspiring Pakistani athletes.

The experience gained from competing in challenging conditions, such as the rain-soaked final in Nagoya, also contributes to an athlete's mental toughness and adaptability. These are qualities that will be invaluable as Nadeem continues his journey towards future international championships, where he will undoubtedly aim to once again stand on the podium.

The focus will now shift to continued preparation and strategic planning for upcoming events. The goal remains to refine technique, enhance physical conditioning, and ensure that all factors are optimised to allow Arshad Nadeem to perform at his absolute best when it matters most, representing Pakistan with distinction on the global stage.
