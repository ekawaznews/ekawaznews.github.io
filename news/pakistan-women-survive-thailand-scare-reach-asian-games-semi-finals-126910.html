Pakistan Women Survive Thailand Scare, Reach Asian Games Semi-Finals

By Umer Javed | 17 September 2026 | 5 min read
Photo: Ketut Subiyanto / Pexels

Pakistan's women's cricket team secured a hard-fought victory against Thailand in the Asian Games quarter-finals on Thursday, winning by three wickets with just two balls to spare. This tense win at Korogi Sports Park in Nisshin, Japan, propels them into the semi-finals of the competition.

Pakistan's national women's cricket team successfully overcame a significant challenge from Thailand, securing a three-wicket victory in the quarter-final of the Asian Games Women’s Cricket Competition held at the Korogi Sports Park in Nisshin, Japan, on Thursday. This narrow win ensures Pakistan's progression to the semi-finals of the prestigious continental event, their resilience under pressure.

The match, affected by rain, saw its duration reduced to 11 overs per side, setting the stage for a high-octane contest where every ball and every run would hold immense significance. Thailand, put in to bat first by Pakistan, managed to post a total of 91 runs within their allotted overs, demonstrating their batting prowess and setting a competitive target for the Pakistani side.

The chase proved to be a nail-biting affair for Pakistan, as they found themselves pushed to the very limits of their batting capabilities. The team ultimately crossed the finish line with only two balls to spare, highlighting the intensity of the contest and the spirited performance by the Thai team.

Pakistan’s pursuit of the target was not without its difficulties, as the side lost seven wickets in the process, indicating moments of pressure and effective bowling from Thailand. It was the important contributions from captain Fatima Sana, who scored an impactful 21 runs off just 13 deliveries, and opener Shawal Zulfiqar, who provided a brisk start with 25 runs from 14 balls, that were instrumental in keeping Pakistan's chase alive.

A Test of Nerves and Composure

The quarter-final clash against Thailand served as a stern test for the Pakistani contingent, revealing their ability to perform under high-stakes conditions. Such close encounters, where victory is snatched in the dying moments, often build character and strengthen a team's resolve for subsequent challenges. The pressure of chasing a target in a reduced-overs match, compounded by the loss of regular wickets, demands exceptional composure and strategic batting, qualities that Pakistan ultimately demonstrated.

Shawal Zulfiqar's aggressive start was vital in setting the momentum for Pakistan, providing a much-needed impetus at the top of the order. Her ability to score quickly ensured that the required run rate remained manageable, even as wickets tumbled around her. This aggressive approach is often key in T20-style formats, especially when overs are limited.

Captain Fatima Sana then took on the mantle of guiding the chase in the latter stages. Her unbeaten knock, coming under immense pressure, showcased her leadership and batting temperament. Despite the context mentioning her being 'usually talismanic' and 'losi' (likely 'losing' her wicket in other matches, but here she was instrumental), her performance in this specific game was undeniably match-winning, steering her team through a precarious situation.

The Asian Games Context and Significance

The Asian Games represent a pinnacle of multi-sport competition for athletes across the continent, offering a platform for excellence and regional camaraderie. Cricket’s inclusion in the Games provides a significant opportunity for the sport to gain wider recognition and for national teams to compete for prestigious medals.

For Pakistan women's cricket, reaching the semi-finals of the Asian Games is a notable achievement. It places them among the top teams in the competition and provides a chance to contend for a medal, which would be a source of immense pride and motivation for the squad and the nation. Participation and success in such events are vital for the growth and visibility of women's sports in Pakistan.

The format of the cricket competition at the Asian Games, often featuring a blend of established and emerging cricketing nations, offers unique challenges. Teams like Thailand, despite being newer to the international cricketing compared to Pakistan, have shown considerable development and are capable of producing upsets, as evidenced by their strong performance against a more experienced Pakistani side.

Thailand’s Spirited Performance

Thailand's performance in the quarter-final deserves considerable acknowledgment. Posting 91 runs in just 11 overs against a team of Pakistan's calibre is their improving standards and disciplined approach. Their bowlers and fielders also applied significant pressure, making Pakistan work hard for every run and creating a genuinely competitive atmosphere.

The growth of women's cricket in countries like Thailand is a positive development for the sport globally. It reflects increasing investment and participation, leading to more competitive matches and a broader talent pool. Their ability to push Pakistan to the brink highlights the evolving of women's cricket in Asia.

The Road Ahead: Semi-Finals and Beyond

With their place in the semi-finals secured, Pakistan now looks forward to the next stage of the competition. The semi-final will undoubtedly present another formidable challenge, as they will face one of the other top teams that have progressed through their respective quarter-final matches.

The team management and players will be focusing on recovery, strategic planning, and addressing any areas identified during the close encounter with Thailand. The experience of surviving a scare can often serve as a valuable lesson, reinforcing the importance of maintaining focus and executing plans under pressure.

A strong performance in the semi-finals would not only pave the way for a potential gold or silver medal match but also further elevate the profile of women's cricket in Pakistan. Such achievements inspire younger generations of girls to take up the sport, contributing to the development of a deeper talent pipeline for the national team.

The journey through a major multi-sport event like the Asian Games is arduous, demanding consistent performance, mental fortitude, and teamwork. Pakistan's women's cricket team has demonstrated these qualities in their quarter-final victory and will aim to carry this momentum into the important semi-final clash, with the ultimate goal of bringing home a medal for the nation.
