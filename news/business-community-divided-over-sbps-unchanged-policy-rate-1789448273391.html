Business Community Divided Over SBP's Unchanged Policy Rate

U
Umer Javed

| 🕒 15 September 2026 at 09:57 am | 📖 4 min read
Share: 💬 WhatsApp 📘 Facebook ✈️ Telegram 🐦 Twitter

The business community across Pakistan on Monday registered a varied response to the State Bank of Pakistan's (SBP) decision to keep the policy rate unchanged at 11.5 per cent. The central bank's Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) announced its latest stance, sparking immediate debate among various segments of trade and industry, with some quarters backing the move and others advocating for reductions to revitalise economic growth.

This announcement from the SBP's MPC has highlighted a clear divergence in expectations and priorities within the nation's economic engine. While the central bank's primary mandate often involves balancing inflation control with economic growth, the business community's immediate concerns often gravitate towards the cost of doing business and the availability of credit for expansion and operations.

The Federation of Pakistan Chambers of Commerce and Industry (FPCCI), a prominent representative body for trade and industry, swiftly conveyed its disappointment regarding the SBP's decision. The FPCCI stated that the current economic environment, characterised by stagnation-prone conditions, necessitates a different approach to monetary policy.

According to the FPCCI, trade and industry are in dire need of some breathing space. This sentiment reflects the challenges faced by businesses operating within a constrained economic, where high borrowing costs can significantly impede operational viability and future planning. The chamber's position highlights a desire for policy interventions that directly alleviate financial pressures on businesses.

The call for further cuts to the policy rate from certain segments of the business community is rooted in the belief that lower interest rates are essential to revive investment and industrial activity. A reduction in the policy rate typically translates into cheaper access to credit for businesses, encouraging them to borrow more for expansion, new projects, and increased production.

Such a measure, proponents argue, could inject much-needed momentum into an economy that has been struggling with subdued activity. Lower interest rates can reduce the cost of capital for manufacturers, allowing them to invest in machinery, upgrade technology, and increase output. This, in turn, can lead to job creation and a broader uplift in economic indicators.

Understanding the SBP's Monetary Policy Framework

The State Bank of Pakistan, through its Monetary Policy Committee, is tasked with formulating and implementing monetary policy to achieve price stability and foster sustainable economic growth. The policy rate, also known as the discount rate or benchmark interest rate, is a primary tool used by the SBP to influence the supply of money and credit in the economy.

When the SBP raises the policy rate, it generally aims to curb inflation by making borrowing more expensive, thereby reducing demand. Conversely, lowering the policy rate is typically intended to stimulate economic activity by making credit more affordable, encouraging investment and consumption. The decision to keep the rate unchanged reflects the MPC's assessment of the prevailing economic conditions and its outlook on inflation and growth.

The MPC considers a range of economic indicators before making its decision, including inflation rates, exchange rate stability, fiscal developments, and global economic trends. The balance struck by the committee aims to safeguard macroeconomic stability while also supporting the economy's productive capacity. This delicate act often leads to differing perspectives on the appropriateness of any given policy rate.

Maintaining the policy rate at 11.5 per cent suggests that the SBP believes the current stance is appropriate for achieving its objectives, or that the risks associated with a change, either an increase or a decrease, outweigh the potential benefits at this specific juncture. This could be interpreted as a cautious approach, prioritising stability over immediate stimulus, or waiting for clearer signals from the economy.

The central bank's decision impacts various aspects of the economy, from consumer lending rates to the cost of government borrowing. For businesses, it directly influences the cost of working capital, project financing, and the overall investment climate. Therefore, any move by the SBP is closely watched and critically assessed by stakeholders across all sectors.

Calls for Economic Stimulus from Industry

The business community's appeal for lower interest rates stems from a desire to reduce the financial burden on enterprises. High borrowing costs can squeeze profit margins, particularly for small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) that often have limited access to alternative financing options. For larger industries, it can deter expansion plans and reduce competitiveness in both domestic and international markets.

Trade associations and industrial bodies frequently highlight how high interest rates impact their ability to compete regionally and globally. When local borrowing costs are significantly higher than those in competitor countries, it places domestic industries at a disadvantage, affecting exports and encouraging imports.
