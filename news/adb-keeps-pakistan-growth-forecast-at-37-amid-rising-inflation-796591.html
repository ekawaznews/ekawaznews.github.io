ADB Keeps Pakistan Growth Forecast at 3.7% Amid Rising Inflation

By Umer Javed | 23 September 2026 | 4 min read
Photo: Valeria Drozdova / Pexels

Asian Development Bank keeps Pakistan’s growth forecast at 3.7% for FY2027, below the 4% budget target. Inflation is projected at 8.3%, higher than the official 7% estimate, as regional tensions loom.

Asian Development Bank keeps Pakistan’s growth forecast at 3.7 per cent.

Context of the Forecast

The Manila‑based lending agency released its Asian Development Outlook September episode on Wednesday, maintaining the July projection of 3.7 per cent for the 2027 fiscal year. The outlook also highlights an inflation estimate of 8.3 per cent, a step above the official 7 per cent figure released by the State Bank of Pakistan for the current fiscal year.

Comparison with the Budget Target

Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif’s government set a 4 per cent growth target for the upcoming fiscal year. The ADB’s figure sits below that benchmark, signalling a potential shortfall that may force the ministry to tighten fiscal discipline or seek external support.

Inflationary Pressures

While the official inflation estimate rests at 7 per cent, the ADB’s projection of 8.3 per cent raises concerns about the cost of living for ordinary citizens. Food prices, transportation and energy costs have already pushed consumer price indices higher in recent months.

Downside Risks from Regional Conflict

The agency cites the conflict in the Middle East as a key downside risk. Disruptions to oil supplies and freight routes could elevate import costs, further feeding into domestic inflation.

Earlier Growth Projections

In an earlier report, the ADB had projected a 4.5 per cent growth rate for 2027, a figure that was later revised down to the current 3.7 per cent. The revision reflects a more cautious stance on the resilience of the Pakistani economy in the face of external shocks.

Implications for Fiscal Policy

With growth below the target, the Finance Ministry may need to reassess its spending plans. A tighter fiscal stance could involve reducing subsidies, delaying non‑essential projects or enhancing revenue collection through tax reforms.

Monetary Policy Response

The State Bank of Pakistan has already tightened its policy stance to curb inflation, raising the policy rate to 6.5 per cent. The ADB’s inflation forecast may prompt further rate hikes, which could cool investment but also help stabilize prices.

External Environment and Trade

Pakistan’s export base remains heavily dependent on textiles and agricultural products. Any slowdown in global demand or increased shipping costs due to Middle East tensions could dampen export earnings.

Energy Sector Challenges

The energy sector continues to face supply shortages and infrastructure bottlenecks. Higher import bills for fuel and oil can increase the cost of production for industries, pushing up prices across the board.

Structural Reforms and Long‑Term Growth

Structural reforms, such as improving the business environment, expanding the formal sector and investing in human capital, remain essential for sustaining growth beyond the 3.7 per cent horizon. The ADB’s outlook does not alter the need for these reforms, but it does highlight the urgency.

Investment Climate

Foreign direct investment inflows have been volatile, with many projects stalled due to regulatory uncertainty. A stable growth outlook is likely to attract investors, yet the higher inflation forecast may deter some foreign capital.

Employment and Social Impact

Slower growth translates into fewer job creation opportunities, especially in the informal sector. The government’s social safety net programs may need to expand to cushion vulnerable households from rising prices.

Public Sentiment and Policy Acceptance

Public trust in economic management is closely tied to visible improvements in living standards. If inflation continues to climb, it could erode confidence in the current administration’s ability to deliver on its promises.

Potential Mitigation Strategies

To counter rising inflation, the government could focus on supply‑side measures such as improving logistics, reducing tariffs on essential goods, and enhancing agricultural productivity to reduce food price volatility.

Regional Cooperation and Trade Agreements

Engagement with neighboring economies and participation in regional trade agreements could open new markets for Pakistani goods, providing a buffer against global price swings.

Conclusion

The Asian Development Bank’s decision to keep Pakistan’s growth forecast at 3.7 per cent, while projecting higher inflation, paints a cautious picture of the country’s economic trajectory. Policymakers must balance fiscal restraint with social protection, and monetary tightening with growth incentives, to navigate the challenges posed by regional instability and domestic price pressures. The path ahead requires disciplined policy choices and sustained commitment to structural reforms to secure a resilient and inclusive economy.
