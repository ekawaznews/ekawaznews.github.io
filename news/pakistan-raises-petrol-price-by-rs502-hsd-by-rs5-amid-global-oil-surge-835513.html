Pakistan Raises Petrol Price by Rs5.02, HSD by Rs5 Amid Global Oil Surge

By Umer Javed | 12 September 2026 | 3 min read
Photo: Fahad Puthawala / Pexels

The government raised petrol prices by Rs5.02 per litre and high‑speed diesel by Rs5 on Friday, citing rising global oil costs and fiscal pressures.

The government on Friday increased the price of petrol by Rs5.02 per litre and that of high-speed diesel (HSD) by Rs5. This move comes as global oil prices surge, prompting the federal cabinet to adjust domestic fuel tariffs. The adjustment is part of a broader strategy to align local prices with international market trends.

Background on Fuel Subsidies

Fuel pricing in Pakistan has long been a balancing act between consumer affordability and fiscal sustainability. Since the early 2000s, the state has subsidised gasoline and diesel to keep transportation costs low for the public. However, the subsidy burden has grown, prompting periodic revisions to the tariff structure.

Drivers of the Price Increase

Analysts point to the sharp rise in Brent crude and WTI benchmarks as the primary driver behind the latest hike. When international oil prices climb, the cost of refining and importing crude into the country rises, forcing the government to pass some of that burden onto consumers. The government also cited the need to reduce the fiscal deficit as a key motive.

Impact on Transport and Economy

The immediate effect of the hike will be felt by commuters and logistics operators. A higher fuel price translates into increased transportation costs, which can ripple through the supply chain and push up the price of goods and services. Small businesses that rely on trucking for distribution may face higher operating expenses.

Government Justification

Officials said the price increase is temporary and will be reviewed in the coming months. They also confirmed that the government will monitor inflationary pressures closely. The cabinet has promised to explore alternative measures to cushion the impact on vulnerable populations.

Industry and Public Response

Industry groups have expressed concern about the potential slowdown in freight movement. The Pakistan Chamber of Commerce and Industry has urged the government to consider targeted subsidies for essential freight services. Meanwhile, consumer advocacy groups have called for a transparent communication strategy.

Inflationary Concerns

From an economic perspective, the fuel price hike could influence the inflation index. The Consumer Price Index (CPI) includes transportation as a significant component. A rise in fuel costs often leads to higher prices for food, fuel, and other consumer goods, thereby affecting the overall CPI.

Policy Adjustments and Future Outlook

The government has also announced a plan to adjust excise duties on petroleum products. By gradually increasing the duty, the state aims to reduce its subsidy outlay without imposing a sudden shock on the market. The policy shift is expected to bring Pakistan’s fuel pricing closer to world market levels.

Historical Context of Fuel Price Adjustments

Historically, Pakistan has seen several rounds of fuel price adjustments. The most recent change in 2023 saw an increase of Rs3 per litre for petrol. These adjustments are often accompanied by public consultations and media briefings to explain the fiscal rationale behind them.

Future Guidance

Looking ahead, the Ministry of Finance has indicated that future price adjustments will be data-driven. The government will rely on a mix of market indicators, fiscal reports, and inflation forecasts to guide its decisions. Stakeholders anticipate a gradual realignment of fuel prices that balances economic growth with consumer protection.
