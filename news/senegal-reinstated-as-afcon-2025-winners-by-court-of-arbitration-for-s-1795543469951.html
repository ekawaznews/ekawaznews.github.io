Senegal Reinstated as AFCON 2025 Winners by Court of Arbitration for Sport

Court Verdict Restores Continental Championship

The highest judicial body in global sports has formally reinstated Senegal as the winners of the 2025 Africa Cup of Nations, ending ten months of legal debate across the continent. The Court of Arbitration for Sport, operating out of Lausanne, announced a unanimous verdict that nullified an earlier administrative ruling against the West African team. BBC Sport reported that the tribunal validated the original outcome achieved on the pitch, declaring Senegal the legitimate holders of the continental trophy following an extensive review of the final match held in Rabat.

According to reports from Al Jazeera, the decision overturns a previous directive issued by the appeals board of the Confederation of African Football. That earlier directive had stripped Senegal of their victory nearly two months after the final match concluded, awarding a default win to host nation Morocco. By issuing a binding ruling, the Lausanne tribunal determined that sporting results established during active play under the control of match officials must be upheld as definitive.

ARY News noted that the governing body for African football has accepted the tribunal judgment without further dispute, committing to carry out all administrative procedures necessary to execute the tribunal order. The resolution provides official clarity to a championship contest that had remained unresolved since early 2026. Representatives of the Senegalese Football Federation publicly welcomed the determination, framing the outcome as a vindication of competitive fairness on the field.

Stoppage Time Refereeing Incidents in Rabat Final

The legal dispute originated during a highly charged final match played at the Prince Moulay Abdellah Stadium in Rabat, where the two top-ranked national teams in Africa contested the championship. Throughout ninety minutes of regulation play, neither side managed to score, leaving the match tied at zero to zero as the game entered second-half stoppage time. Within a brief span of minutes, two separate decisions by referee Jean-Jacques Ndala drastically shifted the course of the match.

BBC Sport detailed the sequence of events, beginning with an attack by Senegal that appeared to yield the opening goal of the contest. Forward Ismaila Sarr directed a header into the Moroccan net, but the Congolese referee had already blown his whistle to call a foul against Senegal player Abdoulaye Seck for a challenge on Moroccan right-back Achraf Hakimi. Because the referee sounded his whistle prior to the ball crossing the goal line, the video assistant referee was legally prohibited from intervening to review the contact, leaving the Senegalese bench frustrated by the ruling.

Shortly after the disallowed goal, action transitioned to the opposite end of the pitch during a Moroccan corner kick. Senegalese defender El Hadji Malick Diouf pulled back Moroccan playmaker Brahim Diaz inside the penalty area before clearing the ball. Diaz immediately voiced strong protests to match officials, prompting the video assistant referee to recommend a pitchside monitor review. Following his inspection of the footage on the monitor, referee Ndala awarded a penalty kick to the host nation in the final moments of stoppage time.

Pitch Walkout, Venue Delays, and Match Conclusion

The penalty ruling provoked immediate resistance from the Senegalese technical staff and squad. Senegal head coach Pape Thiaw gathered his team along the touchline before walking away from the pitch and moving down the stadium tunnel toward the changing room. Multiple Senegal players followed their head coach off the field of play, causing play to stop entirely as match commissioners and tournament administrators attempted to manage the situation.

As reported by BBC Sport, the walkout triggered unstable conditions inside the Prince Moulay Abdellah Stadium. A small group of Senegalese supporters in the stands engaged in physical scuffles with local security personnel while team officials held discussions behind closed doors. More than fifteen minutes elapsed between the initial penalty award and the eventual return of the Senegalese squad to the pitch to complete the fixture.

Upon returning to their positions, Senegal prepared to defend the penalty kick. Real Madrid midfielder Brahim Diaz executed a Panenka-style chipped kick down the middle of the goal, but Senegalese goalkeeper Edouard Mendy remained balanced and routinely caught the ball to maintain the zero to zero scoreline. The match proceeded into extra time, where midfielder Pape Gueye scored a goal to give Senegal a one to zero advantage. The West African side preserved that lead until the final whistle, initially prompting widespread celebrations for their second continental title.

Post-Match Complaints and CAF Appeals Board Action

The physical completion of the match did not end the disagreement between the two national federations. Almost immediately after the final whistle, the Royal Moroccan Football Federation lodged a formal legal challenge with the Confederation of African Football. Moroccan officials argued that Senegal had committed a direct infraction of Article 82 of the tournament regulations, which governs unauthorized departures from the pitch.

Under the specific text of Article 82, any participating team that refuses to continue playing or leaves the field of play prior to the normal conclusion of a match without explicit referee authorization must be declared to have lost the match and be eliminated from the competition entirely. Initially, the continental governing body's disciplinary board dismissed Morocco's challenge, choosing instead to issue financial penalties and disciplinary sanctions against individual players and staff members found responsible for unsporting conduct during the stoppage.

Dissatisfied with the initial dismissal, the Royal Moroccan Football Federation submitted an appeal to the higher judicial tribunal within the continental organization. In March, nearly two months after the match in Rabat, the appeals board issued a decision that overturned the disciplinary panel's finding. The appeals board declared Senegal to have forfeited the final, awarding Morocco a three to zero technical victory. The Senegalese Football Federation denounced the decision as unfair and unprecedented, immediately preparing a formal legal appeal to the international tribunal in Switzerland.

International Hearing and Judicial Rationale

The Senegalese Football Federation submitted its formal appeal to the Court of Arbitration for Sport, initiating an international arbitration process. The Lausanne tribunal convened a three-member panel to hear arguments behind closed doors on 8 October. French legal scholar Gerald Simon, founder of the Sports Law Laboratory at the University of Burgundy and a veteran arbitrator of twenty-three years, served as president of the panel.

To complete the panel, each national federation nominated an independent legal expert as permitted under arbitration guidelines. The Royal Moroccan Football Federation nominated Italian international law professor Luigi Fumagalli, while the Senegalese Football Federation selected Zurich University law professor and Hamburg-based attorney Ulrich Haas. Following six weeks of deliberation after the single-day hearing, the three arbitrators delivered a unanimous judgment upholding Senegal's appeal.

In its official statement, the Court of Arbitration for Sport clarified that the decision to restart or permanently abandon a match rests exclusively within the discretionary authority of the match referee. The tribunal concluded that when referee Ndala sounded his whistle to resume play after both teams re-entered the pitch, he formally determined that necessary conditions for resuming play were satisfied. Consequently, the result achieved on the field stood as final. While Senegal celebrated the ruling, the Moroccan federation expressed surprise and stated it would examine potential legal avenues to challenge the decision.

Regulatory Revisions and Future Implications

The prolonged controversy surrounding the final in Rabat has forced immediate changes to governance protocols within international football. According to BBC Sport, the International Football Association Board enacted new regulatory rules in April specifically addressing match walkouts. Under the updated laws of the game, referees are empowered to issue red cards to any player or team official who leaves the pitch or incites others to walk off in protest, while teams causing match abandonments will systematically face match forfeiture.

Beyond the regulatory shifts, both national teams have transitioned into new operational phases since the contentious final in January. Both Morocco and Senegal have replaced their head coaches in the intervening months while continuing their competitive schedules on the pitch. Earlier this month, both squads successfully secured qualification for the 2027 Africa Cup of Nations with two rounds of qualifying matches remaining.

Although the legal verdict of the Court of Arbitration for Sport officially confirms Senegal as the 2025 continental champions, sports analysts note that the extended period of administrative uncertainty leaves a lasting impact on the governance image of African football. The detailed written grounds for the Lausanne ruling are expected to be published in due course, providing further legal precedent for how match official authority interacts with competition forfeiture rules in global athletics.
