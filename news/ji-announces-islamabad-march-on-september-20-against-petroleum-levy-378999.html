JI Announces Islamabad March on September 20 Against Petroleum Levy

By Umer Javed | 12 September 2026 | 7 min read
Photo: Ann H / Pexels

Jamaat-e-Islami chief Syed Abu Bakr Riyani announced a mass march to Islamabad on September 20 to protest the government's petroleum levy. The party accuses authorities of exploiting citizens through increased fuel costs.

Jamaat-e-Islami (JI) has announced plans for a major public demonstration in Islamabad on September 20, 2023, targeting the government's recent petroleum levy policy. The announcement was made by JI chief Syed Abu Bakr Riyani, who described the move as a decisive step against what he termed economic exploitation by state authorities.

Petroleum Levy Sparks Public Outcry

The proposed petroleum levy has come under intense criticism from opposition parties and civil society groups. The policy, implemented by the PTI government, increases fuel prices and has contributed to rising inflation rates affecting common citizens. JI leaders argue the levy disproportionately impacts lower-income groups while benefiting government coffers.

According to party sources, the march will begin from the historic Minar-e-Pakistan in Lahore before proceeding to Islamabad. Organizers expect participation from various civil society organizations and student unions aligned with JI. The party has urged citizens to join the demonstration to voice their opposition to what they describe as predatory taxation policies.

Historical Context of Fuel Protests

This is not the first time such policies have triggered public unrest in Pakistan. Previous instances of fuel price hikes under different governments have led to widespread protests, including the dharna movement led by Imran Khan in 2019. The current JI-led march seeks to capitalize on public discontent over economic hardship amid ongoing challenges like energy shortages and power load-shedding.

Economists have warned that continued increases in petroleum costs could exacerbate Pakistan's balance of payments crisis. The levy, part of broader fiscal reforms, aims to boost revenue but risks deepening public dissatisfaction with economic management. Critics, including JI, argue alternative measures like taxing luxury goods would be more equitable.

Government Response and Political Dynamics

The PML-N government has yet to issue a formal response to JI's march announcement. However, ruling party sources stated that dialogue remains open if citizens' concerns are addressed. Some analysts suggest the protest could intensify political pressure on the coalition government, particularly as it manages a delicate balancing act between IMF conditions and public support.

Opposition leader Shehbaz Sharif has historically criticized similar levies, though his party's stance on this specific policy remains ambiguous. Analysts note that JI's march may attract broader opposition unity if economic hardship persists, potentially reshaping the political ahead of upcoming local elections.

Security Precautions and Public Advisories

Islamabad police have deployed additional security personnel in preparation for the demonstration. Authorities have advised citizens to maintain peace and avoid any actions that could disrupt public order. Legal measures, including section 144 restrictions if necessary, may be imposed to ensure compliance with law and order protocols.

JI leaders have emphasized their commitment to peaceful protest, calling on participants to uphold constitutional rights. The party has also urged media to provide fair coverage of the event, contrasting with past instances where protests faced information blackouts or biased reporting.

Broader Implications for Pakistani Democracy

The planned march reflects deepening public frustration with economic policies perceived as favoring elites over ordinary citizens. Civil society organizations have echoed JI's concerns, highlighting how rising fuel prices erode purchasing power and strain household budgets. UNICEF recently reported that over 40 percent of Pakistan's population now lives below the poverty line, compounding the impact of such levies.

Experts suggest that sustained protests could influence future budget allocations or prompt policy reversals. However, political analysts caution that without addressing root causes like corruption and inefficient subsidies, public anger may persist regardless of short-term policy adjustments.

International Dimensions and Aid Dependency

Pakistan's economic challenges are exacerbated by its reliance on external financial assistance. The IMF's extended fund facility program requires structural reforms, including tax increases, which JI and other critics argue harm vulnerable populations. The protest coincides with ongoing negotiations for additional aid tranches, raising questions about the balance between austerity measures and social protection.

International financial institutions have consistently urged Pakistan to broaden its tax base and reduce energy sector subsidies. While JI opposes these measures in their current form, alternative proposals—such as formalizing the informal sector to expand tax collection—remain under-discussed in public discourse.

Media and Information Warfare

State and private media outlets have provided varying coverage of the petroleum levy debate. JI officials have accused certain channels of downplaying public grievances to favor government narratives. Social media platforms have seen viral content showing long queues at fuel stations and rising utility bills, amplifying anti-levy sentiment.

The government has previously restricted access to social media during protests, citing national security concerns. Human rights groups have condemned such actions as infringements on freedom of expression, urging authorities to permit open dialogue without censorship or surveillance.

Religious and Tribal Solidarity

JI's appeal may resonate strongly in regions with historical ties to religious seminaries and tribal councils. In Khyber Pakhtunkhwa and Balochistan, where economic conditions are particularly dire, local elders and religious leaders could amplify the protest's message. Past movements led by JI have gained traction in these areas due to shared grievances over land rights and resource allocation.

Students from various universities, including Punjab University and Quaid-e-Azam University, have expressed interest in participating. Campus demonstrations have previously played catalytic roles in mobilizing youth against economic policies, though authorities often preemptively restrict academic activities during such periods.

Legal and Constitutional Considerations

Organizing public assemblies requires adherence to the Prevention of Attacks on Energy Installations (Amendment) Act, which mandates prior permission from relevant authorities. JI leaders have stated they will comply with legal procedures, though past instances show groups sometimes proceed without approvals, leading to clashes with police.

Constitutionally, citizens retain the right to peaceful assembly under Article 9 of the Pakistan Constitution. However, courts have occasionally deferred to executive powers to restrict such rights during periods of instability or security threats, as seen in previous protest-related cases.

Comparative Regional Trends

Similar fuel protests have erupted across South Asia. In Bangladesh, public demonstrations forced the government to suspend a fuel price hike in 2022. India's recent regional elections saw parties campaigning on promises to roll back fuel taxes, though implementation varies by state. JI's strategy mirrors these regional tactics, using economic grievances to challenge incumbent administrations.

In Afghanistan, the Taliban's recent fuel subsidies sparked riots, illustrating how energy policies remain volatile in conflict-affected regions. Pakistani policymakers may observe these dynamics while formulating responses to domestic protests, balancing reform imperatives with social stability.

International Support Networks

JI has historically maintained ties with global Islamist movements, though its current campaign appears domestically focused. The party has not yet sought endorsements from international organizations, instead emphasizing self-reliance in its messaging. Analysts suggest this approach aims to avoid external scrutiny that could complicate domestic political maneuvering.

Regional think tanks have noted that Pakistan's protests often gain transnational resonance through diaspora communities. Online forums in the UK and Gulf states have amplified JI's anti-levy stance, potentially influencing expatriate remittances or lobbying efforts abroad.

Future Outlook and Civil Society Engagement

The success of JI's march will depend on sustaining public momentum beyond September 20. Civil society groups have expressed readiness to support follow-up actions, including legal challenges to the levy's constitutionality. Labor unions representing transport and energy sectors have hinted at solidarity strikes if economic conditions remain unaddressed.

Political scientists argue that sustained protests can force policy recalibration, as seen in past instances where government concessions followed prolonged unrest. Whether JI can maintain unity among diverse stakeholders—religious groups, students, and labor unions—remains uncertain amid internal disagreements over protest strategies.

Economically, Pakistan's trajectory hinges on balancing IMF-mandated reforms with social stability. If the September 20 protests galvanize significant public pressure, authorities may face difficult choices between maintaining fiscal discipline and addressing immediate hardships. The outcome could influence not only the levy's future but also broader governance approaches in an era of economic uncertainty.
