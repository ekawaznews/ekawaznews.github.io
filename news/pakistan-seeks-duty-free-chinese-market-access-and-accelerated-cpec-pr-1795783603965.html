Pakistan Seeks Duty-Free Chinese Market Access and Accelerated CPEC Projects

High-Level Trade Talks Open in Beijing

According to ARY News, Pakistan has formally requested zero-tariff access for all of its domestic products entering the Chinese market. This comprehensive trade proposal was submitted during a high-level bilateral meeting held on Thursday, 26 November 2026, in Beijing. Professor Ahsan Iqbal, who holds the cabinet portfolio for planning, development and special initiatives, led the delegation representing Pakistan. He held extensive discussions with Mr Zhou Haibing, the vice chairman of China’s National Development and Reform Commission, to explore avenues for expanding bilateral economic engagement and addressing trade imbalances.

The request for duty-free access comes against the backdrop of a major trade imbalance between the two partner nations. Import records covering the 2025-26 fiscal period indicate that Pakistan imported goods valued at $22.34 billion from China. In sharp contrast, outbound shipments from Pakistan to the Chinese market amounted to just $2.71 billion during that exact timeframe. To bridge this wide commercial gap, the planning minister put forward a non-reciprocal framework. This proposed arrangement is intended to operate outside the boundaries of the existing bilateral free trade agreement that currently governs commercial relations between both countries.

The new trade proposal is patterned directly after the zero-tariff entry privileges that Beijing has previously extended to several African nations. During the session in Beijing, the Pakistani delegation suggested holding dedicated technical consultations between officials from both sides. These technical talks would focus primarily on establishing clear product coverage rules, defining origin criteria for eligible goods, and setting a definitive timetable for rolling out the proposed tariff exemptions. Such measures could open new channels for Pakistani exporters seeking broader market access across China.

Upgrading Highway and Railway Transportation Networks

Transportation routes and regional connectivity formed a major topic of discussion during the bilateral agenda in Beijing. As reported by ARY News, Professor Ahsan Iqbal urged the early completion of the financing agreement for the 102-kilometre Thor Nullah to Raikot section of the Karakoram Highway. This specific highway segment represents the primary priority phase of the broader Thakot to Raikot realignment project, which carries an estimated total cost of Rs576 billion.

The planned realignment of the Karakoram Highway is designed to ensure uninterrupted traffic flow along the vital northern land route. Existing sections of the highway face potential disruption from ongoing water resource development and hydropower construction projects situated along the Indus River. By re-routing and upgrading vulnerable stretches of the highway, both nations seek to protect a critical transport conduit that facilitates overland movement and regional trade between Pakistan and China.

In addition to highway infrastructure, the planning minister reviewed options for Chinese participation in modernizing the Main Line-1 railway network beyond the Karachi to Rohri segment. Pakistan is currently exploring financing possibilities with the Asian Development Bank to support this major railway overhaul. also, officials from both delegations assessed progress on four joint road feasibility studies during the session, which include technical plans for constructing the Babusar Tunnel to enhance regional road networks.

Port Expansion and Gwadar Infrastructure Plans

Port operations and coastal development at Gwadar constituted another key area of focus during the bilateral dialogue. According to ARY News, the planning minister requested assistance in reviving regular commercial shipping services at Gwadar harbour. He suggested corporate engagement with shipping lines such as COSCO to increase vessel calls, improve cargo handling volumes, and establish stable maritime connections with global commercial hubs.

The Pakistani delegation also proposed a joint research initiative to examine the feasibility of constructing strategic petroleum storage facilities directly at Gwadar port. Developing localized fuel storage capacity at the port site could enhance energy distribution networks and support expanded maritime logistics along the coast. During the meeting, officials also reviewed operational activities within the Gwadar Free Zone alongside the ongoing progression of the second phase of the Eastbay Expressway.

In a related logistics proposal, discussions covered a conceptual transport corridor designed to connect mineral extraction zones in Chagai directly to Gwadar port. Under this proposed layout, raw mineral resources mined in the Chagai region could be transported directly to Gwadar for local processing and subsequent export to overseas markets. This strategic link could assist in establishing an integrated processing and export pipeline for domestic mineral wealth.

Power Projects and Industrial Cooperation Under CPEC

The transition into the second phase of the China-Pakistan Economic Corridor brought several major power generation and industrial ventures into the discussion. ARY News reported that Professor Ahsan Iqbal asked for accelerated progress on the Kohala and Azad Pattan hydropower projects. Expanding clean energy generation remains an important goal within the bilateral energy agenda to support domestic power supply.

Officials also deliberated on collaborative assessments to evaluate whether selected power stations across Pakistan could undergo fuel conversion. Joint technical teams may examine the feasibility of switching operational power units from reliance on imported coal to locally sourced Thar coal. Replacing imported coal supplies could help lower energy production expenses and conserve foreign currency resources. also, the Pakistani delegation sought official approval from Beijing for the Siah Diq copper initiative and suggested joint work to construct two or three model special economic zones.

To foster greater involvement of private commercial enterprises within the corridor framework, the Pakistani side formally suggested incorporating three private-sector ventures into the economic corridor agenda. These proposed additions include an aluminium processing complex situated at Port Qasim, a dedicated fertiliser manufacturing plant located in Thar, and a combined oil refinery and fuel storage complex situated in Dhabeji.

Expanding Digital Infrastructure, Education, and Agriculture

Beyond heavy infrastructure and energy, the diplomatic encounter addressed digital connectivity, academic exchanges, and agricultural development. According to ARY News, participants reviewed the implementation status of two existing agreements focused on artificial intelligence development and technical collaboration.

Telecommunications infrastructure proposals featured prominently, with discussions reviewing a proposed 1,500-kilometre fibre-optic link intended to connect Rawalpindi to Karachi. This major digital cable project aims to strengthen national data transmission capabilities and expand high-speed network connectivity. In the educational sphere, officials examined a jointly funded academic initiative aimed at delivering 10,000 doctoral fellowships over a ten-year timeframe, aimed at building high-level research capabilities.

Modernization of the agricultural sector formed another key component of the meeting. The planning minister proposed establishing Chinese partnerships in farm modernization along with dedicated food processing zones. These processing zones are designed to minimize agricultural losses after harvests occur and bolster Pakistan's overall export capacity by combining local farming yields with advanced Chinese processing systems and capital investment.

Strategic Alignment and Next Steps for Economic Ties

Concluding the formal engagement, senior officials from both nations expressed optimism regarding the future direction of bilateral economic relations. According to ARY News, Mr Zhou Haibing praised the China-Pakistan Economic Corridor as a fundamental pillar of economic cooperation between the two countries. The Chinese official highlighted the tangible gains already realized through corridor projects in strengthening energy security, improving transportation networks, and driving regional economic activity.

Mr Zhou Haibing confirmed that the National Development and Reform Commission stands ready to collaborate closely with Pakistan’s Ministry of Planning, Development and Special Initiatives as the corridor transitions into its next phase. He emphasized the necessity of aligning future projects with Pakistan's national development goals and welcomed the various proposals put forward by the Pakistani delegation during the Beijing meetings.

Moving forward, observers may watch for subsequent technical meetings between representatives of both governments to determine how these trade proposals, financing arrangements, and project approvals develop. The outcome of upcoming consultations on duty-free market access, highway funding, and industrial zone establishment could play an important role in shaping future trade patterns and infrastructure execution schedules for both partner nations.
