Parliament to Decide on New Provinces, Says PML-N's Rana Sanaullah

U
Umer Javed
Political Reporter
| 🕒 13 September 2026 at 09:51 am | 📖 5 min read
Share: 💬 WhatsApp 📘 Facebook ✈️ Telegram 🐦 Twitter
📷 Photo: Swagoto Mondal / Pexels | ekawaznews.github.io

Adviser to the Prime Minister on Political Affairs, Rana Sanaullah of the Pakistan Muslim League-Nawaz (PML-N), stated on Saturday that the Parliament holds the ultimate authority to decide on the matter of creating new provinces.

Speaking at a press conference in Lahore, Sanaullah directly addressed recent remarks made by Interior Minister Mohsin Naqvi regarding the necessity of new administrative units within Pakistan. The PML-N leader's comments reaffirm the constitutional process required for any such significant restructuring of the country's administrative.

Sanaullah was specifically asked about Naqvi’s recent assertion that “new administrative units will eventually have to be created as Pakistan’s growing population and changing needs can no longer be adequately served by the existing structure.” This call for reform, articulated by Minister Naqvi in late July, initiated a renewed public and political discourse on the complex issue of provincial reorganisation.

The Interior Minister’s proposal for new provinces as part of broader reforms to the current governance system had previously drawn strong reactions from various government ministers and political stakeholders. His remarks highlighted a perspective that the current provincial setup, largely inherited from colonial times, may no longer be optimally suited to the demands of a rapidly expanding population and evolving societal requirements.

The Genesis of a Renewed Debate

The debate surrounding the creation of new provinces is not a new phenomenon in Pakistan's political history. However, Minister Naqvi's direct advocacy for new administrative units brought the issue back into the national spotlight with renewed intensity. His argument centred on the premise that the existing provinces, particularly the larger ones, face immense challenges in effectively managing resources, delivering services, and ensuring equitable development across their vast territories.

Proponents of new provinces often argue that smaller administrative units can lead to more efficient governance, closer interaction between the government and citizens, and a more targeted approach to development initiatives. They contend that local issues can be better understood and addressed when administrative power is decentralised, potentially leading to improved public service delivery and reduced regional disparities.

Conversely, the idea of creating new provinces often elicits concerns regarding potential ethnic or linguistic divisions, the financial implications of establishing new provincial capitals and administrative structures, and the political complexities of redrawing provincial boundaries. Such concerns contribute to the cautious approach many political parties and leaders adopt when discussing this sensitive subject.

Constitutional Framework and Parliamentary Sovereignty

Rana Sanaullah's emphasis on Parliament's role is a direct acknowledgement of the constitutional requirements for creating new provinces in Pakistan. The Constitution of Pakistan outlines a rigorous process for any alteration to provincial boundaries or the creation of new provinces. This process typically involves a constitutional amendment, which necessitates a two-thirds majority vote in both the National Assembly and the Senate.

Achieving such a consensus in a diverse and often fragmented political is a considerable challenge. It requires broad political will and agreement among major political parties, especially those representing the existing provinces that would be affected by any reorganisation. The parliamentary process ensures that any decision on new provinces is a collective one, reflecting the will of the representatives of all federating units.

The PML-N leader's statement thus serves as a reminder that while political leaders may express opinions or initiate debates, the ultimate decision-making power rests with the legislative body. This procedural clarity is essential for maintaining constitutional order and ensuring that profound administrative changes are undertaken through legitimate and democratic means.

Historical Demands for Provincial Reorganisation

Pakistan has a history of regional movements and demands for the creation of new provinces, driven by various factors including administrative convenience, cultural identity, and perceived economic neglect. Notable among these are the long-standing demands for a Seraikistan province in South Punjab and a Hazara province in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa.

The movement for a Seraikistan province, for instance, has its roots in the distinct cultural and linguistic identity of the Seraiki-speaking population, coupled with a sentiment that the region has been historically underdeveloped and underrepresented within the larger province of Punjab. Similarly, the demand for a Hazara province stems from a desire for administrative autonomy and a distinct identity within Khyber Pakhtunkhwa.

These historical precedents illustrate the deep-seated complexities associated with provincial reorganisation. While the arguments for administrative efficiency and equitable development are compelling, the issues of identity, resource distribution, and political power dynamics often intertwine, making the process of consensus-building particularly arduous.

Challenges and the Path Forward

The creation of new provinces involves intricate financial, administrative, and political challenges. Economically, establishing new provincial capitals, administrative infrastructure, and service delivery mechanisms would entail significant budgetary allocations. Questions often arise regarding the financial viability of smaller provinces and their ability to generate sufficient revenue to sustain themselves.

Administratively, the demarcation of boundaries, the division of assets and liabilities, and the transfer of personnel would require meticulous planning and execution. Politically, the process necessitates overcoming inter-provincial rivalries and forging a national consensus that transcends party lines and regional interests.

Rana Sanaullah's statement highlights that any move towards creating new provinces will require a comprehensive national dialogue, careful consideration of all implications, and ultimately, the broad endorsement of the country's elected representatives. It is a process that demands not just political will, but also a deep understanding of the diverse needs and aspirations of Pakistan's populace.

As the debate continues, the focus remains on Parliament as the appropriate forum for deliberation and decision-making on this critical issue. The path to any provincial reorganisation is expected to be long and complex, requiring sustained political engagement and a commitment to upholding the constitutional framework.
