Pakistan Prioritises Dialogue for Houthi Threat, Reaffirms Saudi Security

By Umer Javed | 18 September 2026 | 7 min read
Photo: Markus Winkler / Pexels

Pakistan has voiced deep concern over the 'exceptionally serious' situation in the Red Sea, advocating for dialogue to resolve the Houthi conflict. The nation also reaffirmed its security commitments to Saudi Arabia while indicating openness to mediation with Afghanistan under specific conditions.

ISLAMABAD: Pakistan on Thursday expressed concern over the “exceptionally serious” situation around the Bab al-Mandab Strait and reaffirmed its commitments to Saudi Arabia’s security, but stressed that the Houthi conflict should be addressed through dialogue and diplomacy rather than through coercion. This comprehensive statement from the Foreign Office (FO) in Islamabad outlined the nation's clear stance on the escalating tensions in a critical global maritime chokepoint and its consistent approach to regional stability.

The FO's briefing also confirmed the operational status of Pakistan's Strategic Mutual Defence Agreement with Riyadh, noting that some forces have already been deployed. also, Pakistan indicated its openness to foreign-led mediation efforts with Kabul, provided Afghanistan makes requisite anti-terror commitments, addressing persistent concerns regarding cross-border security.

Red Sea Tensions and Global Implications

The Foreign Office's characterization of the situation around the Red Sea chokepoint as “exceptionally serious” highlights the profound implications of the ongoing Houthi attacks on commercial shipping. This strategic waterway, connecting the Indian Ocean to the Mediterranean Sea via the Suez Canal, is vital for global trade, particularly for oil and gas shipments. Disruptions in this area have far-reaching economic consequences, affecting international supply chains and energy prices worldwide.

Pakistan, as a significant player in the broader Islamic world and a nation deeply invested in regional peace, views any escalation in the Red Sea with considerable apprehension. The potential for wider conflict in the Middle East, a region with which Pakistan shares strong historical and cultural ties, remains a constant concern for policymakers in Islamabad. The nation has consistently advocated for de-escalation and peaceful resolution to disputes that threaten international commerce and regional stability.

The Bab al-Mandab Strait, a narrow passage between Yemen on the Arabian Peninsula and Djibouti and Eritrea in the Horn of Africa, is a critical choke point for maritime traffic. Its strategic importance cannot be overstated, as a significant portion of global trade passes through it daily. Any threat to its security directly impacts the global economy, making Pakistan's concern a reflection of broader international anxieties.

Commitment to Saudi Arabia's Security

Addressing its bilateral relations, Pakistan confirmed that its Strategic Mutual Defence Agreement with Riyadh “is operational,” with some forces already deployed. This statement reaffirms Pakistan's long-standing security cooperation with Saudi Arabia, a relationship anchored in shared strategic interests and religious brotherhood. The agreement highlights Pakistan's readiness to contribute to the defence of the Kingdom, particularly given the volatile security in the Gulf region.

The deployment of Pakistani forces under this agreement is not a new development but rather a continuation of a historical partnership. Pakistan has a proud tradition of training and assisting friendly countries in defence matters, and its military personnel have served in various capacities across the Gulf states for decades. This specific agreement provides a structured framework for such cooperation, ensuring coordinated efforts in addressing shared security challenges. The reaffirmation of its operational status signals Pakistan's unwavering commitment to its strategic ally amidst regional uncertainties.

This commitment is rooted in a deep historical bond between the two nations, spanning economic, cultural, and defence sectors. Saudi Arabia is a key economic partner for Pakistan, and the security of the Kingdom is considered integral to Pakistan's own regional strategic interests. The current statement reiterates that this partnership remains steadfast, especially when challenges to regional security emerge. The ongoing instability in the Red Sea region adds another layer of significance to these established defence ties.

Dialogue Over Coercion in Houthi Conflict

While expressing solidarity with Saudi Arabia, Pakistan firmly reiterated its preference for dialogue and diplomacy to address the Houthi conflict. This position aligns with Pakistan's consistent foreign policy doctrine, which prioritizes peaceful negotiation and political solutions over military confrontation. The FO's statement emphasized that coercion is unlikely to yield sustainable peace in a complex regional conflict like the one involving the Houthis.

The conflict in Yemen, which has drawn in various regional and international actors, has resulted in a severe humanitarian crisis. Pakistan's call for dialogue reflects its belief that only an inclusive political process, involving all stakeholders, can lead to a lasting resolution that respects Yemen's sovereignty and ensures regional stability. This approach is consistent with Pakistan's advocacy for peaceful conflict resolution globally, often participating in or supporting mediation efforts in various international disputes.

The statement implicitly suggests that while security commitments are upheld, the path to resolving the underlying issues in Yemen lies in political engagement rather than solely military means. This nuanced stance allows Pakistan to maintain its strategic alliances while advocating for a broader, more sustainable peace framework for the region. The Pakistani government believes that long-term stability can only be achieved through comprehensive political settlements that address the root causes of conflict and provide equitable solutions for all parties involved.

Engagement with Afghanistan on Anti-Terror Commitments

In a separate but equally significant policy articulation, the Foreign Office indicated Pakistan’s openness to foreign-led mediation efforts with Kabul. This openness, however, is explicitly conditional on Afghanistan making requisite anti-terror commitments. This stance highlights Pakistan's persistent concerns regarding cross-border terrorism emanating from Afghan soil, an issue that has significantly strained bilateral relations.

For Pakistan, the security of its western border remains a vital national interest. Recent incidents of terrorism within Pakistan, which Islamabad attributes to militant groups operating from Afghanistan, have heightened the urgency of this demand. The conditionality attached to Pakistan's willingness for mediation reflects its firm position that addressing terrorism is a prerequisite for any meaningful progress in bilateral ties and regional stability.

The offer of openness to foreign-led mediation suggests a pragmatic approach from Pakistan, acknowledging the complexities of direct engagement and possibly seeking neutral facilitation to bridge the trust deficit with the interim Afghan government. Such mediation would ideally focus on establishing verifiable mechanisms for counter-terrorism cooperation, ensuring that Afghan territory is not used against Pakistan. This aligns with international expectations for any responsible government to prevent its territory from being used as a staging ground for terrorism against neighboring states.

Pakistan has consistently called upon the Afghan authorities to honour their international commitments regarding counter-terrorism. The willingness to engage through mediation, provided these commitments are met, signals a desire to find a constructive path forward, recognizing the shared challenges of regional security. It also reflects a hope that external facilitation might help in overcoming the impasses that have characterized recent interactions between the two countries.

Pakistan's Broader Foreign Policy Vision

These statements collectively articulate Pakistan's broader foreign policy vision, characterized by a commitment to peace, stability, and adherence to international law. Whether it is the volatile Red Sea, the enduring conflict in Yemen, or the complex relationship with Afghanistan, Pakistan consistently advocates for diplomatic solutions and responsible state conduct. The nation strives to maintain a delicate balance between fulfilling its strategic alliances and promoting a peaceful resolution of conflicts.

Pakistan's role as a responsible member of the international community necessitates a proactive approach to regional and global security challenges. The emphasis on dialogue over coercion, the reaffirmation of defence pacts, and the conditional openness to mediation all reflect a foreign policy framework designed to safeguard national interests while contributing to broader regional and international peace efforts. Islamabad remains committed to fostering an environment of cooperation and mutual respect among nations, particularly in its immediate neighborhood and the wider Islamic world.

The Foreign Office's comprehensive briefing highlights Pakistan's considered and principled approach to complex geopolitical challenges, aiming to contribute to stability through diplomacy and strategic partnerships.
