Overview of the Four Day Weather Pattern

Official forecast data provided by Open-Meteo presents a mixed meteorological trend across Pakistan from Monday, 12 October 2026 through Thursday, 15 October 2026. Different regions of the country are set to witness contrasting weather systems during this four day period. While high elevation territories in the north prepare for precipitation and early snowfall, southern parts of the country continue to experience elevated daytime temperatures under mostly clear to partly cloudy skies.

The transition between seasons often brings noticeable regional variation across the provinces. Data recorded by Open-Meteo shows atmospheric shifts moving across northern and northwestern areas, bringing rain and overcast skies to urban centers such as Islamabad, Peshawar, and Gilgit. Meanwhile, the central plains and coastal districts will see relatively drier conditions, though occasional moisture and temperature swings will alter day to day routines.

Understanding these forecasts enables residents, travelers, and working communities to plan their activities effectively. The divergence in temperature and moisture levels across major urban centers highlights the geographical diversity of the country, where northern valleys enter colder conditions while southern port hubs maintain summer like warmth.

Northern Valleys and Western Highlands

In Gilgit, cold and unstable conditions are expected to dominate throughout the four day timeline, based on figures from Open-Meteo. On 12 October, the city is forecast to reach a high of 23 degrees Celsius and a low of 10 degrees Celsius, accompanied by drizzle and a 92 percent chance of rain. Precipitation chances climb to 98 percent on 13 October, with temperatures shifting between a high of 20 degrees Celsius and a low of 13 degrees Celsius alongside steady rain. The weather turns significantly colder on 14 October, when snow is forecast with a 99 percent chance of precipitation, daytime highs reaching 19 degrees Celsius, and nighttime lows dropping to 10 degrees Celsius. By 15 October, drizzle continues with a 90 percent chance of rain, maintaining a high of 19 degrees Celsius and a minimum temperature of 8 degrees Celsius.

Further west, Peshawar experiences active weather systems during the early part of the week. Open-Meteo forecasts indicate a high of 27 degrees Celsius and a low of 19 degrees Celsius on 12 October, with drizzle and a 47 percent chance of rain. A thunderstorm is predicted for 13 October, when the mercury will peak at 26 degrees Celsius and drop to 19 degrees Celsius, accompanied by a 73 percent chance of precipitation. Rain remains in the forecast on 14 October, with a high of 25 degrees Celsius, a low of 19 degrees Celsius, and a 24 percent chance of wet conditions. The skies are anticipated to become partly cloudy on 15 October, bringing a daytime high of 26 degrees Celsius, an overnight low of 17 degrees Celsius, and an 18 percent chance of rain.

Quetta presents a somewhat different pattern within the western upland region. On 12 October, Open-Meteo reports a maximum temperature of 29 degrees Celsius and a low of 15 degrees Celsius, alongside drizzle and a minor 6 percent chance of rain. The remaining days turn dry and partly cloudy. On 13 October, Quetta will record a high of 26 degrees Celsius and a low of 16 degrees Celsius with zero percent rain probability. On 14 October, temperatures will slide between 25 degrees Celsius and 13 degrees Celsius, followed by a cooler 15 October with a high of 24 degrees Celsius, an overnight low of 11 degrees Celsius, and zero percent chance of rain.

Federal Capital and the Punjab Plains

Residents in Islamabad will encounter persistent damp conditions over the course of the week. According to Open-Meteo data, Monday, 12 October will begin with partly cloudy conditions, a maximum temperature of 27 degrees Celsius, a low of 19 degrees Celsius, and an 8 percent chance of rain. Moisture levels rise sharply on 13 October, bringing drizzle with an 82 percent chance of precipitation, a high of 25 degrees Celsius, and a low of 19 degrees Celsius. Drizzle continues on 14 October, where the thermometer will show a high of 24 degrees Celsius and an overnight minimum of 17 degrees Celsius, with rain chances recorded at 28 percent. By 15 October, drizzle persists as temperatures hit 25 degrees Celsius during the day and 17 degrees Celsius at night, with rain probability rising back to 61 percent.

Lahore starts the week on a milder note before seeing increased moisture later on. The forecast from Open-Meteo indicates a high of 29 degrees Celsius and a low of 19 degrees Celsius on 12 October, with partly cloudy skies and an 8 percent likelihood of rain. On 13 October, the daytime high reaches 30 degrees Celsius with a low of 20 degrees Celsius, accompanied by partly cloudy skies and a 47 percent chance of rain. Conditions on 14 October show a matching high of 30 degrees Celsius and a low of 20 degrees Celsius, with partly cloudy conditions and rain probability dropping to 12 percent. Rain arrives in full measure on 15 October, when temperatures ease to a high of 28 degrees Celsius and a low of 19 degrees Celsius, paired with a 60 percent chance of precipitation.

In southern Punjab, Multan remains relatively warm while seeing occasional cloud cover. Open-Meteo figures show a high of 33 degrees Celsius and a low of 23 degrees Celsius on 12 October, with partly cloudy conditions and an 8 percent rain chance. The temperature climbs on 13 October to reach 35 degrees Celsius alongside a low of 23 degrees Celsius, partly cloudy skies, and a 29 percent chance of rain. On 14 October, Multan experiences drizzle with a daytime peak of 34 degrees Celsius, a low of 24 degrees Celsius, and a 28 percent rain chance. By 15 October, partly cloudy weather returns with a high of 32 degrees Celsius, an overnight low of 22 degrees Celsius, and a 20 percent probability of rain.

The Southern Coastal Belt

Karachi stands apart from the northern and central regions by maintaining sustained warmth and dry conditions throughout the four day forecast window. Open-Meteo records show that Monday, 12 October features partly cloudy skies, a high of 33 degrees Celsius, a low of 25 degrees Celsius, and a negligible 2 percent chance of rain. The city warms up further on Tuesday, 13 October, where the mercury is expected to touch 36 degrees Celsius during the afternoon, settling at 26 degrees Celsius at night under partly cloudy skies with zero percent rain probability.

Clear skies will dominate the coastal metropolis during the latter half of the forecast period. On Wednesday, 14 October, Karachi will record a maximum temperature of 35 degrees Celsius and a low of 26 degrees Celsius with zero percent rain likelihood. Thursday, 15 October brings slightly reduced daytime heat, showing a high of 33 degrees Celsius and an overnight minimum of 26 degrees Celsius, again with completely clear skies and zero percent chance of rain.

This persistent dry heat separates the coastal belt from the wetter weather seen across the northern parts of the country. With rain completely absent after Monday, the port city maintains a very steady daytime rhythm that contrasts with the developing precipitation patterns in the north.

Implications for Travel and Transport

The forecast conditions will have direct consequences for travelers moving across regional road and transit networks. In northern corridors such as Gilgit, the combination of high precipitation chances, rain, and snow on 14 October could present noticeable hurdles for road connectivity. Travelers moving through high mountain passes may encounter slippery road surfaces, reduced visibility, and potential route delays caused by localized snow accumulation and sustained rain events between 12 October and 15 October.

In urban centers like Peshawar and Islamabad, scheduled commutes could experience interruptions during periods of peak rainfall. Thunderstorm activity expected in Peshawar on 13 October, alongside rain probabilities reaching 73 percent, may create localized water pooling on city roads and slow down rush hour traffic. Similarly, recurring drizzle across Islamabad throughout 13, 14, and 15 October will require drivers to maintain greater braking distances and prepare for slower movement along major avenues and intercity links.

Conversely, highway travel across southern routes between Multan and Karachi is expected to face fewer weather related disruptions. Because Karachi remains dry from 13 October through 15 October and Multan experiences only modest chances of drizzle, long distance freight and passenger movement along southern corridors will largely operate under standard conditions. Drivers in these areas will mainly need to account for warmer afternoon temperatures rather than slick roads or impaired visibility.

Agricultural Considerations and Daily Life

Farming communities in different parts of the country face distinct operational considerations during this window. In upper agricultural belts where rain and drizzle are anticipated, farmers may need to schedule field activities around wet days. The significant moisture expected in Gilgit and the damp spell across Islamabad and parts of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa could influence soil moisture levels, which can be beneficial for specific seasonal preparations but may temporarily hold up outdoor harvesting or field drying tasks.

In the fertile plains of Punjab, particularly around Lahore and Multan, the mixed forecast presents manageable parameters. Lahore sees a 47 percent chance of rain on 13 October and a 60 percent likelihood on 15 October, which could lead growers to pause spraying or irrigation routines on those specific days. Meanwhile, Multan experiences lower rain chances between 8 percent and 29 percent, allowing routine agricultural practices to proceed with relatively minor adjustments.

For urban households and working individuals, the forecast dictates practical choices in clothing and energy use. Residents in Quetta will see night temperatures drop to 11 degrees Celsius by 15 October, while Gilgit experiences lows of 8 degrees Celsius, requiring warmer attire during early mornings and late evenings. In contrast, families in Karachi and Multan will continue running fans and cooling appliances as daytime readings hover between 32 degrees Celsius and 36 degrees Celsius.

Health Awareness and Looking Ahead

In cities such as Islamabad and Peshawar, where drizzle, thunder, and rain will mark consecutive days, sudden cooling may trigger seasonal respiratory complaints, common colds, and allergic reactions among vulnerable populations, including young children and elderly citizens.

At the opposite end of the spectrum, residents in Karachi, where temperatures rise up to 36 degrees Celsius on 13 October, should remain attentive to hydration during peak daylight hours when direct heat is most pronounced.
