Lawmakers Seek Filling of Urdu Varsity Senate, Syndicate Vacancies

U
Umer Javed
Staff Reporter
| 🕒 23 September 2026 at 04:52 pm | 📖 7 min read
Share: 💬 WhatsApp 📘 Facebook ✈️ Telegram 🐦 Twitter
📷 Photo: Brett A / Pexels | ekawaznews.github.io

Members of the National Assembly and Senate have called for the immediate filling of vacant positions in the governing bodies of a national Urdu language university, saying that delays in appointing members to the Senate and Syndicate have left the institution without proper oversight. The lawmakers raised the issue during recent parliamentary proceedings, stating that the prolonged absence of filled seats in these key bodies is affecting the university's ability to function effectively.

Background of the Issue

The Urdu language university, which operates under federal oversight, has been functioning with a number of unfilled positions in its highest decision-making bodies. The Senate, which serves as the primary academic governing body of the university, includes members drawn from parliament, the academic community, and civil society. The Syndicate, which handles financial and administrative matters, has also been operating with several empty seats. These vacancies have persisted for a considerable period, drawing criticism from lawmakers and academic staff alike.

According to reports, the process of appointing members to these bodies requires notification from the federal government and the Higher Education Commission. However, bureaucratic delays and procedural hurdles have slowed the process significantly. Parliamentarians from multiple political parties have flagged the issue, pointing out that the university cannot make significant academic or financial decisions without a complete quorum in either body.

Role of Senate and Syndicate in University Governance

Under the regulations governing national universities in Pakistan, the Senate is responsible for approving academic programmes, setting examination policies, and determining criteria for degrees and diplomas. It also plays a role in appointing senior academic officials, including the Vice Chancellor, subject to government approval. When seats in the Senate remain vacant, the body cannot convene valid sessions, and important decisions are put on hold.

The Syndicate,, is tasked with managing the university's finances, approving budgets, and overseeing infrastructure development projects. Financial allocations for new academic buildings, research initiatives, and faculty hiring all require Syndicate approval. With several members absent, the Syndicate has reportedly struggled to reach decisions on pressing administrative matters. Officials familiar with the matter said that routine financial approvals have been delayed due to the lack of sufficient members attending meetings.

Lawmakers Express Concern

During a recent session, parliamentarians from various committees raised the matter with federal ministers responsible for education and housing. A member of the National Assembly's Standing Committee on Education stated that the delay in filling these positions was unacceptable and urged the government to issue notifications for the vacant seats at the earliest opportunity. The lawmaker said that universities across the country have faced difficulties when their governing bodies were not fully constituted, and the current situation at the Urdu university was a cause for serious concern.

Members of the Senate Committee on Education and Professional Development also discussed the issue among themselves. Several senators told reporters that filling these vacancies should have been prioritized much earlier. They said that the university's academic calendar and ongoing projects were being adversely affected by the absence of a complete governing team. The senators added that faculty members and university administrators had repeatedly drawn attention to the problem but had not received any concrete response from the federal authorities.

Impact on Academic and Administrative Operations

The extended vacancies have had a noticeable effect on the day-to-day operations of the university. Sources within the institution said that several proposed academic programmes, including postgraduate courses in Urdu literature and linguistics, have been pending Senate approval for months. Without a fully constituted Senate, these proposals cannot be reviewed or voted on, leaving students and faculty in a state of uncertainty.

Administrative matters have also been impacted. Plans for campus expansion, the construction of new faculty blocks, and upgrades to laboratory and library facilities have reportedly stalled due to the Syndicate's inability to convene meetings. University officials confirmed to reporters that financial proposals worth millions of rupees remained pending executive approval, affecting the pace of development work on campus.

Faculty members have expressed frustration over the situation. Some teachers said that hiring decisions for open lecturer and professor positions have been put on hold because the Syndicate must approve new appointments. This has led to a growing teaching workload for existing staff and has discouraged potential applicants from seeking positions at the university. According to reports, the institution has been operating with a significantly reduced teaching staff for the past several months.

Government Response and Next Steps

The federal Ministry of Federal Education and Professional Training has been approached for comment on the matter. According to reports, ministry officials acknowledged the issue and stated that the notification process for filling the vacant Senate and Syndicate seats was underway. A senior ministry official told reporters that the government was reviewing the list of nominees and expected appointments to be made soon. However, no specific timeline has been provided publicly.

The Higher Education Commission has also been asked to facilitate the process by coordinating with the university administration and the relevant parliamentary committees. Reports suggest that the HEC has forwarded the required documentation to the ministry, including lists of recommended candidates for the vacant positions. The commission stated that it remains ready to support the process in any way possible.

Parliamentary observers believe that the issue of filling university governing bodies is likely to come up again in upcoming parliamentary sessions. Several lawmakers have indicated that they plan to raise the matter during question hour and committee meetings in the near future. A senior parliamentarian involved in education policy told reporters that the government must act swiftly to resolve the vacancies, as the delay is harming the university's reputation and the welfare of its students and staff.

The National Assembly and Senate have both expressed interest in ensuring that national universities across the country maintain fully constituted governing bodies. This issue at the Urdu university has been seen as part of a wider pattern in which several institutions face similar challenges. Lawmakers have called for a review of the appointment process to ensure that vacancies are filled within a defined timeframe, so that academic and administrative operations are not disrupted in the future.

Context of National University Governance

Pakistan's system of national universities operates under federal legislation that outlines the composition and powers of governing bodies such as the Senate and Syndicate. The Vice Chancellor, who serves as the chief executive of the university, is appointed by the federal government after consultation with the Senate. The Vice Chancellor is responsible for the overall administration of the university and chairs both the Senate and Syndicate meetings. When these bodies are not fully constituted, the Vice Chancellor's ability to execute duties effectively is significantly limited.

The appointment process for Senate members typically involves nominations from the president of the university, the Vice Chancellor, and the federal government. Syndicate members include government nominees, university officials, and representatives from the business and professional communities. Under existing regulations, these bodies must meet at regular intervals, and decisions are taken by majority vote. Legal experts have noted that decisions taken in meetings that do not have the required quorum may be subject to challenge, which creates further complications for university administration.

The situation at the Urdu university reflects broader concerns about governance standards in Pakistan's higher education sector. Several national universities have experienced similar challenges in recent years, with governing bodies operating on ad hoc arrangements for extended periods. Education activists have called for stronger enforcement of timelines for filling vacancies and greater parliamentary oversight of the appointment process to prevent such delays from recurring.

As of the latest reports, no new appointments to the Senate or Syndicate of the Urdu university have been formally announced. The government has yet to issue official notifications, and parliamentarians continue to press for action. The matter remains under active discussion in both houses of parliament, with lawmakers from all major parties agreeing on the need for a prompt resolution.
